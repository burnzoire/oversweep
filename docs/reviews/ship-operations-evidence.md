## [1] CNATRA P-1211, Flight Training Instruction, Carrier Qualification, T-45, (7-01)

- p. 3: "Each of the five cells is angled slightly from the adjacent cell for a total vertical coverage of 1.5 degrees. The lenses are manufactured in such a way that only one cell, or part thereof, can be seen from a particular angle. Each cell projects a bar of horizontal light that appears to be a ball". "The ball is visible on the lens at plus or minus 3/4 of a degree vertically from optimum glideslope and about 20 degrees either side of centerline. These conditions create a wedge-shaped area in which the ball can be seen on the lens (Figure 4)." "Mounted horizontally and centered above the lens box are four green cut lights". ""Bingo" is signaled by alternating waveoff and cut lights."
- p. 6 (IFLOLS): "The number of Datum Lights has increased to 10. The vertical coverage has been increased to 1.7 degrees vice the 1.5 of FLOLS. Acquisition range has been increased from 3/4 nm to 1 1/2 nm."
- pp. 9–11: LSO calls, e.g. ""You're fast/slow." Adjust nose attitude/power to reestablish optimum AOA"; ""Bolter." Power to MRT, retract speed brakes, and rotate nose attitude to establish optimum AOA and climb"; ""Drop your hook." Extend arresting hook."
- p. 11 (grades): "OK 5 Perfect pass OK 4 Reasonable deviations with good corrections (OK) 3 Fair pass, reasonable deviations B 2.5 ... bolter -- 2 No grade, below average but safe pass PWO 2 Pattern waveoff WO 1 Waveoff, aircraft not set up properly for a safe approach (technique) C 0 ... cut pass, unsafe, gross deviations inside waveoff window".
- p. 11 (grades, continued): "TWO NC Test waveoff, practiced during FCLPs to demonstrate proper waveoff technique OWO NC Own waveoff, executed when clearance to land via Roger ball or cut lights are not received OWO 2 Own waveoff, executed when clearance to land via Roger ball or cut lights are received WOFD NC Waveoff—foul deck NC NC No count (used in grade column)". The list is introduced as "a partial list of symbols", and the grade "with appropriate comments, is recorded in your training jacket".
- p. 12 (Figure 7, LSO grading symbols 1 of 2): "See Figures 7 and 8 for additional symbols used by LSOs to describe landings on the Pilot Performance Record." Modifiers: "Parentheses around any symbol signify \"slightly\" (e.g., (F) means \"slightly fast\")"; "A square drawn around any symbol indicates that a signal was not answered by the pilot"; "A circle drawn around any symbol indicates that a signal was answered too slowly"; "When used as a prefix to any symbol, OC indicates \"over-controlled\""; "APC/AUTO A"; "Manual (APC-equipped aircraft) M"; "Pitching deck PD"; "Mode 1 ACLS (record in grade column) I"; "When placed between two symbols, indicates \"on\""; "An APC/AUTO approach downgraded to manual". Symbols AA through NERD as tabled on the page, for example "Eased gun EG", "Low LO", "Long in the groove LIG". Landed left wing down, landed right wing down and landed nose first are drawn symbols with no letters. The tpub text layer scrambles the two columns; pairs were matched by each item's position on the page image.
- p. 13 (Figure 8, LSO grading symbols 2 of 2): symbols NERR through TWA, "For emphasis (underline)", drawn symbols for "Landed 3 points", "Over the top", "Fly up through the glideslope" and "Fly down through the glideslope", and the places: "In the turn IT", "Out of turn (as aircraft starts to roll wings level) OT", "At the start (first one-third of glideslope) X", "In the middle (middle one-third of glideslope) IM", "In close (last one-third of glideslope) IC", "At the ramp AR", "To land TL", "In the wires IW", "Over the wires OW", "All the way AW".
- p. 21 (Under Powered): "Avoid the temptation to cut power or drop your nose when you are high or climbing in-close to at-the-ramp. Accept the high or take your bolter. A large power reduction in close to at-the-ramp is referred to as a cut or ease gun. This condition is unsafe and is never an acceptable correction—a high \"come down\" will result in a hard landing, blown tires, and possible structural damage."
- p. 18: "Never descend below 300 ft AGL without a ball. The glideslope is a 3.25 degree (above the horizon) fixed path determined by the angle of the Fresnel lens." "If the meatball is not acquired by the start, a "Clara" call will be made. GROOVE The groove is the portion of the approach from a wings-level start to touchdown, ideally this should be 15- 18 se[conds]".
- pp. 21–22: "Never accept a low ball. Never finesse a low ball." "A large power reduction in close to at-the-ramp is referred to as a cut or ease gun. This condition is unsafe and is never an acceptable correct[ion]". "CAUTION: Never accept a low ball. If you're low, add power immediately. Do not reduce power until the ball is centered."
- p. 30: "Exterior lights, to include position, collision, and strobe lights, will be extinguished prior to entering the break. SPIN PROCEDURES ... If the pattern is full, tower may instruct the flight to spin. At the bow, the flight lead begins a climbing 250 KIAS left turn to an altitude of 1,200 ft AGL".
- p. 31: "the flight leader will break on his interval or no earlier than 1 nm past the bow (or as directed) using 70-80 degrees AOB ... Each wingman breaks at 15-second intervals". "Although the landing area is angled approximately 10 degrees, the pattern is flown parallel to the BRC (base recovery course)."
- p. 32 (Figure 19, carrier landing pattern): "INITIAL ... 3 nm Astern ... 800 ft ... 250-300 KIAS .9 - 1.1 nm ABEAM ... ABEAM 600 ft Establish VSI: 200-300 fpm 27-30 AOB 45 325-375ft 90 450 ft".
- p. 33: "Verify your distance abeam (0.9-1.1 nm)." "At the abeam position, roll into 27-30 degrees AOB and adjust power and nose slightly to set up a 200-300 fpm rate of descent." "When at the 90, maintain optimum AOA, a 27-30 degree AOB turn ... cross-check altitude (450 ft AGL), and increase VSI to a 500-fpm descent." "Cross-check altitude 325-375 ft ... An advisory call from the LSO ("Keep your turn in") normally occurs from the 90 to the [45]". "If you do not see the ball, call "Clara." Do not descend below 300 ft."
- pp. 65–69 (glossary): "Air Boss: Officer (located in Pri-Fly) in charge of all flight deck and tower operations within 10 nautical miles of the ship." "Axial Winds: Winds down the longitudinal axis of the ship created by the ship's forward movement. This causes a right-to-left crosswind across the a[ngled deck]". "Clara: Meatball is not in sight." "Cross-deck Pendant (CDP): Arresting gear wire." "Pull Back: Action following arrestment whereby the wire is partially retracted to allow the pilot to raise the tailhook." "Shuttle: The portion of the catapult that attaches to the launch bar during catapult launches." "Spin: Depart and reenter the break. Normally performed at the bow if unable to break prior to 4 nautical miles upwind."

## [3] NAVEDTRA 14310, Aviation Boatswain's Mate E

- p. 3-1: the arrestment pulls the purchase cables "off the arresting engine, forcing a ram into the cylinder holding pressurized hydraulic fluid (ethylene glycol). This fluid is forced out of the cylinder through a control valve that meters the flow to an accumulator until the aircraft is brought to a smooth, controlled arrested landing". Before recovery: "the shuttle spreader cover installed ... Jet Blast Deflectors (JBDs) completely lowered and hydraulics secured".
- p. 3-3: "Determine proper aircraft weight setting in accordance with applicable aircraft recovery bulletin"; "Pickle switch is actuated, lighting the green clear deck landing status light".
- p. 3-31: "The polyester-core crossdeck pendant are made of 1 7/16-inch diameter ... with a minimum breaking strength of 205,000 pounds."
- p. 4-1: "Steam is the principal source of energy and is supplied to the catapults by the ship's boilers. The steam is drawn from the ship's boilers to the catapult wet steam accumulator, where it is stored at the desired pressure. From the wet accumulator, it is directed to the launch valve". Table: "Power stroke (in feet) 249-10" 309-8 3/4" 306-9"" for the C-13-0, -1 and -2.
- p. 5-1: "The jet blast deflector (JBD) installation consists of water-cooled panels that are mounted flush with the flight deck."

## [4] FM 1-564, Shipboard Operations, 1997

"DISTRIBUTION RESTRICTION: Approved for public release; distribution is unlimited."

- p. 1-12: single-spot "Currency requirements: Four single-spot shipboard landings must be conducted within 90 days. (a) Pilots whose currency has lapsed but have made four single-spot landings within the last 180 days will-- • Undergo training conducted by either a current DLQ PC or DLQ IP. • Perform four field deck landings before six shipboard landings (all within a ten-consecutive-day period)." Multispot day: "Five day field deck landings must be conducted before five day shipboard landings (all within a ten consecutive day period). (2) Day currency requirements: Four shipboard landings must have been made within the preceding nine months." Night: "the pilot must be day-qualified and current ... Six night field deck landings must be conducted before six night-shipboard landings (all within a ten consecutive day period)."
- p. 1-13: "Night currency requirements: To maintain currency, six night shipboard landings must have been made within the preceding 90 days. If more than 72 hours have lapsed since the last night shipboard landing, one day shipboard landing will be performed within 24 hours before the next night shipboard landing."
- p. 5-2: "During mixed aircraft operations, helicopters will enter the starboard Delta pattern which is located one mile to the starboard side of the ship at 300 feet and oriented on the BRC. ... the helicopter break altitude will not exceed 300 feet."
- p. 5-15: "flights will proceed and hold in the Delta pattern. ... The flights will plan their descent, break to meet the designated recovery time, and maintain an orderly flow of traffic into Charlie pattern." "Helicopter starboard Delta. Normally, a holding pattern on the right side of the ship is a right-hand racetrack pattern that is oriented on the ship's heading at an assigned altitude".
- p. 5-18: "The landing pattern for port spots is an approach that starts not later than when the aircraft is abeam the intended point of landing. A turn is then made to intercept the 45-degree line at the 90-degree position and flight continued straight into the spot."
- Appendix F, Table F-1 and para F-3: standardized clothing colours, e.g. "Aircraft Handling Crew and Chockmen Blue Blue", "LSE (Crew Directors) Yellow Yellow", "Maintenance Crews Green Green".

## [12] Rowe, "Automated Trend Analysis for Navy-Carrier Landing Attempts", NPS, I/ITSEC 2012

Used for the real comment examples, the typed marks and the extra fleet codes. "Results are shown on a sample of 85,571 passes representing about 20% of the current Navy records". "For example, \"(LO)SLOIC-AR\" means that an aircraft was slow and a little low, both when approaching the carrier and just at the edge of the carrier deck; the parentheses mean the lowness was only a little, and the hyphen means the comment applies to the period between being \"in close\" and \"at the ramp\"." Table 1 grammar: "e(code1-code2 loc) → e(code1 loc) <space> e(code2 loc) Loc is a location; hyphen means \"to\""; "e(_code_) → e(code) Mark emphasis"; "e([code]) → e(code) Mark missed communication"; "Interpret dot as \"on\"". "\"NEP/COIM LO IN CLOSE HALF FLAPS THRUST CAUTION\" gets transformed ultimately to \"NEP.COIM LOIC HALF+FLAPS ENG+PROBLEM\"". "for the LSO pass comments of \"HCDAR EWIT\", atomic comments \"H\", \"CD\", \"AR\", and \"EWIT\" were extracted". "Example variant abbreviations are \"EWI\", \"EZW/IT\", and \"EASYWITHIT\" for \"EWIT\"". "3-point touchdown -> 3pts". "Additional codes were observed that probably should standardized, like LTR (\"left to right\"), NELR (\"not enough left rudder\"), TMLR (\"too much left rudder\") ... TOB (\"talking on the ball\")".

## [13] "lex", "Landing grades", 2004, reposted on The Lexicans, 2017

Used for one worked pass. "The LSO's use shorthand to grade a pass – something written down as: (OK) OC NEP-BC /IM (NEP-CDIC) SDAR LOBDRIW 2, would translate into, \"Fair pass: over-controlled a little not enough power on the ball call, fly through up in the middle, a little not enough power on the come-down in close, settle/decel at the ramp, low, flat, drift right in the wires. Two-wire.\"" BC, DR and / are read from this translation. The page notes that the post reads SD as settle/decel where P-1211's SD is spotted deck.
