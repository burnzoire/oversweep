# Encyclopedia Aircraft revision 1 — helicopters evidence

Claim-to-source notes for `content/encyclopedia/aircraft/` records AH-64D
Apache Longbow, CH-47F Chinook, Ka-50, Mi-8MTV-2, Mi-24P, OH-58D Kiowa Warrior,
SA 342 Gazelle and UH-1H. Template and conventions:
`docs/reviews/encyclopedia-aircraft-us-jets-evidence.md`. These notes are review
evidence, not instructions to the reviewer.

## Narrative text and photographs

- Record bodies are written as narrative history, following the earlier
  batches. Every paragraph ends with its citation, and anecdotes come from the
  cited articles' development and operational-history sections.
- Where the two sources differ they are both given: the UH-1D's arrival in
  Vietnam (1963 in FAS; the first 77 in April 1965 in Wikipedia), and the OH-58D's maximum speed (129 kt from 240 km/h in
  Wikipedia; 125 KIAS in FAS).
- Each record leads with a photograph from Wikimedia Commons and adds one or two
  more (18 in all). Public-domain US government photographs are used where
  Commons has a suitable one; the Ka-50 in-flight, Mi-24P and Gazelle leads are
  CC BY or CC BY-SA, credited with a licence link. GFDL-only files were avoided.
  Captions are written from the Commons file description; alt text describes
  what the image shows.

## Modelling choices

- **Mi-8MTV-2**: the Wikipedia specification block is for the Mi-8MT, so the
  plate labels its figures as Mi-8MT figures.
- **SA 342 Gazelle**: the Wikipedia specification block is for the SA 341G. The
  plate uses the FAS SA 342 figures for ceiling and range and gives the SA 341G
  figure beside them; the speed (310 km/h) is the same in both.
- **OH-58D**: describes the armed Kiowa Warrior configuration; the narrative
  also covers the OH-58A in Vietnam and the OH-58C shot down over North Korea in
  1994, each labelled with its variant.
- **UH-1H**: the narrative covers the whole single-engine Huey family, naming
  the variant (UH-1B, UH-1D, UH-1E, UH-1F) where an episode involves another
  model. Speed is the Wikipedia figure at maximum take-off weight, which is
  also the never-exceed speed at that weight.
- **Unit conversions**: km/h ÷ 1.852 = kt; mph ÷ 1.15078 = kt; m × 3.2808 = ft,
  rounded to 100 ft.

## Marked as source-limited

- FAS pages describe late-1990s configurations; later upgrades (CH-47F digital
  cockpit, AH-64E, Ka-52) are cited from Wikipedia only.
- Casualty and loss figures (UH-1 in Vietnam, OH-58D in Iraq and Afghanistan)
  are given as Wikipedia states them and attributed to it.
