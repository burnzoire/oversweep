Training flight training instruction (cover: "BASIC FIGHTER MANEUVERING (BFM)
AND ALL WEATHER INTERCEPT (AWI) FLIGHT TRAINING INSTRUCTION ADVANCED UMFO
T-45C/VMTS 2014"; list of effective pages: "Revision …4…01 Jul 14"). CNATRA's
pat-pubs directory, where these instructions were published, now returns
404 (P-1289 too), so it was read on a public mirror at falcon.blu3wolf.com,
which is not on the review packet's host list. A 02-17 revision exists on
other mirrors and was not read. The instruction says of itself: "the SNFO is
learning skills, not tactics. Tactics will be taught at the FRS" (p. 15-1).

**Invented content.** Figure 1's intercept (call signs Rage and SABRE, the
bullseye name Vegas, positions, 3 NM fighter spacing, 2 NM contact spacing,
instantaneous turns, the "out south" turn) is made up and labelled so; its
ranges (commit 35, meld 25, shoot 20 NM) and call formats follow [1]. Figure
3's zone shape and its ratios (tail RMAX 35% of head-on, RNE 45% of RMAX, RMIN
6%) are invented; only the ordering (longest hot, shortest in the rear
quarter, RNE inside RMAX) is sourced. The 300 kt speed is [1]'s training
ground speed.

## [2] JP 3-01, Countering Air and Missile Threats, 23 March 2012

- p. III-11: "Unit commanders always retain the inherent right and obligation to exercise unit self-defense in response to a hostile act or a demonstrated hostile intent."
- p. III-13: "Positive ID is ID derived from visual recognition, electronic support systems, noncooperative target recognition techniques, IFF systems, or other physics-based ID techniques. Positive ID does not assume identity solely based on location or adherence to airspace procedures. To prevent fratricide or undesired engagements, positive ID normally is required by ROE as a basis for CID for engagement (shoot/no-shoot) decisions for air threats." "Procedural ID is based on adherence to airspace control measures and rules. ID is assumed to be friendly as long as rules are followed, but ID is assumed hostile if rules are not followed and the suspect vehicle is not otherwise positively identified."
- p. III-14: procedural ID "generally not for engagement decisions for which positive ID is normally required because of the risk of fratricide."
- p. III-16: "Based on the ROE, a positive engagement order normally is required"; "the engagement decision normally is based on positive ID or affirming hostile intent or a hostile act."
- p. III-17: "CID is the process of attaining an accurate characterization of detected objects in the operational environment sufficient to support an engagement decision."
