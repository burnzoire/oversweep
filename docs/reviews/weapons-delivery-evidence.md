## [1] CNATRA P-1219, Flight Training Instruction, Weapons, T-45, (4-03)

- p. 3: "The T-45A NATOPS manual contains delivery charts for MK 76 bombs and 2.75" FFAR rockets ... used to find the correct sight settings and release attitudes for each specific pattern. Target elevation must be added to the release altitude"; "The direction of the offset aimpoint ... is always directly into the wind. ... D = 1.7 X T X W".
- p. 4: "D = 1.7 X 7 X 12 = 142.8 ft. So, your offset final aimpoint will be about 140 ft north of the target." "Release airspeed is a true airspeed."
- p. 5: "Practice ordnance consists of water-sand fills that simulate the ballistics of general-purpose bombs." "The MK 76 simulates a Mk 82 500 lb. bomb".
- p. 11: breakaway cross "displayed only when the HUD determines that, with normal pilot reaction time and a normal 4-g recovery, the aircraft would bottom out with 1,000 ft or less of ground clearance ... disabled for a dive angle of less than 15 degrees." CCIP "compensates for dive angle, airspeed, altitude, and target wind to display a continuously computed impact point"; "an aiming line is projected above the CCIP marker to provide azimuth steering"; "the CCIP mode is not magical"; "releasing near your normal parameters will improve the system's accuracy"; "actual target winds may differ from the forecast winds".
- p. 20: "For a given and dive angle, the pattern of all possible flight paths form a cone above the target. In combat, you would maneuver so as to intercept this cone from the most advantageous direction".
- pp. 21–23: 30° pattern "Pattern altitude: 8,000 AGL Pattern airspeed: 250 KIAS Planned Release altitude: 3,000 AGL Minimum Release altitude: 2,700 AGL Release airspeed: 450 KTAS ... Mil setting: 128 mils Radalt: 1700 Weather requirements: 8,500/5"; 20°: 6,000 / 300 / 2,000 / 1,800 / 450 / 130 mils / 1200 / 6,500/5, "substitutes for the 30-degree pattern when the ceiling is unsuitable"; 10°: 2,500 / 350 / 1,000 / 900 / 450 / 117 mils (bombs) / 600 / 3,000/5, "simulates high drag or forward firing ordnance deliveries".
- p. 24: "The abeam distance is approximately two (2) nautical miles."
- p. 25: "strive to stay on the attack cone by placing the target at your 3 or 9 o'clock. Arcing around the attack cone is much like tacan arcing." Roll-in: "set power to release rpm and start a 15-17 unit pull towards the target ... about 30 degrees of heading prior to the run-in heading ... roll the aircraft to put the lift vector slightly below the target ... accept a variance of less than 10 degrees ... Because of the "pendulum effect" ... the roll-out must be commenced while the pipper is still short of the run-in line." "Pickle your bomb or fire no lower than minimum release altitude with zero AOB in approximately 1-g flight." "pause about half a second ... obtaining 4 g's over 2-3 seconds."
- p. 26: ""In hot" or "In cold" ... "Hot" means you have intention to release; "cold" means you do not ... "In Winchester" ... you have expended all your ordnance."
- p. 29: "It is what you do between roll-in and release that will determine the accuracy of your delivery." Mil: "1/6400 of a circle ... one foot apart after 1,000 ft ... roughly 17 mils in one degree."
- p. 30: definitions of dive angle, armament datum line ("parallel with the flight path at 450 kt release airspeed ... this is not the same as the AOA measured by the aircraft instrument"), trajectory drop, sight angle ("equals the trajectory drop of the weapon plus the angle of attack and any parallax"), time of fall, slant range, minimum release altitude ("based on ... the fragmentation pattern of the weapon, adequate time of fall for the fuse to arm, enemy weapons envelopes").
- p. 31: Z diagram items; bombing triangle ("The base of the triangle is the horizontal distance downrange ... The vertical component ... is the release altitude, and the hypotenuse ... is the trajectory drop"); "the pilot must decide on a release altitude and airspeed and consult the delivery data table to find the appropriate sight setting".
- p. 32: "if you release high ... the weapon will hit short ... if you release low, the hit will be long." "a fast release will decrease your AOA and bring the pipper short of the impact point causing a long hit." "A steep dive will cause a long hit and a shallow dive a short one." "For a 30-degree delivery, about 0.87 g ... A 10-degree dive requires almost 1 g. A 60-degree dive ... only 0.5 g." "Excessive g will cause an early sight picture ... your hit will be short ... Too much g will shallow the dive angle". "if you release with the pipper on target while you are in a right bank, your hit will be to the right and short."
- p. 33: "if the ball is out to the right ... your hit will be to the right of the target."
- p. 34: "You correct for wind effects by varying your bank angle as you fly around the pattern ... Wind effect on the bomb after release is corrected by the offset aimpoint".
- p. 36: "initial sight setting (65 mils for 30 degree bombs, 70 mils for 20 degree bombs, and 55 mils for 10 degree bombs) ... If the resultant dive angle is more than 5 degrees greater or less than planned, abort the run ... use quick wing dips".
- p. 37: "The pipper should reach the final aimpoint ... just as the aircraft arrives at the release altitude"; curvilinear: "g will gradually approach zero ... an attempted release will either hit long or become hung ... curvilinear only tracking is not practiced"; curvilinear/straight-path "Upon reaching one and a half times the release altitude, capture the current dive angle".
- p. 39: "In a combat environment, however, you normally get just one chance." Figure 20, bomb rows: 10° ±1° = ±10 kt = -/+70 ft = +80 ft; 20° and 30° ±1° = ±10 kt = -/+100 ft = +50 ft; rockets 20° and 30°: +20 ft.
- p. 40: "if in a 30-degree bomb run, your HUD indicates 32 degrees ... pickling 200 ft high"; "28 degrees ... increasing power by about 4% rpm, allowing the aircraft to accelerate to 470 kts"; "20 kts fast ... pickling 200 ft high or ... with the pipper 100 ft short ... 20 kts slow, you can allow the pipper to drift 100 ft past".
- p. 41: "If you release with the correct altitude, airspeed, dive angle, etc. (no wind), the weapon will impact where the pipper was positioned at release." Early sight picture: "pipper arrived at the aimpoint at 3,400 AGL ... pickling at 3,200 AGL"; "consistently getting an early sight picture ... rolling in too close". Late: "if the pipper is more than 150 ft short of the aimpoint at planned release altitude, abort ... let it track halfway ... less than one second ... adding power ... Resist the tendency to pull the nose up, as this just makes you shallow".
- p. 42: bombs "no last-minute correction for deflection"; guns "your correction should be 1.25 times the deflection error"; "1 degree shallow and ... 10 kts slow ... a total of 100 ft ... 1 degree shallow and 10 kts fast ... cancel".
- p. 45: "Most times, however, you will intercept the attack cone from an ingress heading and just pass briefly through it." "add power to MRT and execute a 15-17 unit level turn inbound to arrive over the run-in line with the target approximately 4 miles off the nose ... ingress toward the target at 700-1,000 AGL. Do not arm the Master Arm switch until your nose is pointed at the target!" "The pop is offset to one side so that you can keep the target in sight while climbing ... At approximately 2.5 nm, the target will be about 5 degrees down in your HUD ... roll 30 AOB away from the pattern and pull the nose 30 degrees up using a 17-unit pull. This will generate approximately 30 degrees of heading change ... roll wings level in the climb and quickly reacquire the target. Passing 2,000 AGL, roll to put your lift vector on the target and pull down to establish the aircraft in a 10-degree dive. The amount of time between rolling wings level ... and initiating your pull down ... is only a few seconds."
- p. 46: "during your pop-up sorties you will be bombing using CCIP mode ... Any significant longitudinal stick inputs during the final seconds ... large deviations"; "recover straight ahead to 1,000 AGL, make your off call".
- p. 49: "Do not depend on the cross as a cue; pull off when you are supposed to."
- p. 50: target fixation "some have ... flown into the ground ... flown right through minimum release altitude, the breakaway cross, and the RADALT"; "going low for release may not give sufficient time for your weapon to arm ... With live ordnance, you will also have a fragmentation pattern to avoid."
- pp. 67–71 (glossary): ADL, breakaway cross, CCIP, depressed sight line, dive angle, early/late sight picture, iron sight, manual delivery, mil, offset aimpoint, pendulum effect, pickle, pipper, roll-in, run-in line, sight depression angle, slant range, target fixation, time of fall.

## [2] CNATRA P-1208, Flight Training Instruction, Operational Navigation, T-45, (4-03)

- p. 5: "a steep dive angle requires a higher altitude and consequently more time in a climb from low altitude"; "high altitudes to avoid small-arms fire and AAA; low to avoid SAMs". "LAYDOWN Weapons are dropped from aircraft in level flight. Release point is usually determined by weapons systems. Weapons use high drag devices to allow the attacker to avoid the fragmentation pattern."
- p. 6: "LOFT Weapons are released by computer with aircraft in a nose-up attitude so that the weapon is actually thrown at the target in a ballistic trajectory. Loft maneuvers allow increased distance between weapon detonation and target area defenses. POP-UP The attacker makes a low-level run at the target, pulling up rapidly at a preplanned point to make a dive delivery. A pop-up may be a roll-ahead or an angle-off ... a heading change of 30-60 degrees at the preplanned point, combined with a hard climb ... This vertical and lateral displacement further complicates a defender's firing solution."
- p. 31: "Because it is difficult to achieve a consistent dive angle and determine a correct release altitude during a pop-up attack on targets of opportunity, the CCIP delivery mode is more accurate."
- p. 32: "Use 10-, 20-, and 30-degree dives, depending on distance to the target, simulated threat environment, and route structure".
- p. 37: "When reaching the IP for the planned target, or 10 miles, whichever comes first, Lead will initiate a "fenced-in" call. Fence procedures will include A/G mode (Bombs and CCIP selected), Master Arm--Arm, and a G's and Fuel check." "performing a simulated level lay-down delivery by dragging the CCIP cross over the target."

## [3] CNATRA P-1217, Operational Navigation FTI, Two-Plane Road Reconnaissance, (7-99)

- p. 33: altitude example (lead/wing: cruise 3,000/4,000; pulldown 5,500/6,500; apex 6,000/7,000; release or recovery initiation 4,000/5,000; recovery 3,000/no lower than 4,000 ft MSL); "In no case should the apex exceed the top of the route structure." "The pop-up and cut should be executed at four miles ... the target will appear to be 10 to 12 degrees below the waterline." Early and late pop. Pop: "Throttle - MRT ... Roll approximately 30 degrees angle of bank ... 17 unit pull in the oblique to 30 degrees nose up ... roll wings level, reacquire the target and place it at 10 o'clock or 2 o'clock."
- p. 34: "The roll-in will be executed upon reaching the 30-degree attack cone. This is approximately two miles from the target." "[call sign] in low" / "in high, visual".
- p. 35: "Overbank to put lift vector on the target ... and pull down at 17 units. Stop the pull-down with the waterline or velocity vector 4-5 degrees above the target".
- p. 36: "there is only 1,000 ft for the wing and lead to recover from their 30-degree dive. This will reacquire a smooth but aggressive 4-5 G pull wings level".
- p. 38: high cover: "Wingman has no visual on the lead. Wingman has no tally on the target. ... Wingman dive angle greater than 35 degrees."

## [5] Wikipedia, "Toss bombing"

"by the U.S. Air Force as the Low Altitude Bombing System, or LABS"; "The purpose of toss bombing is to allow an aircraft to bomb a target without flying directly over it. The technique both avoids overflying a heavily defended target and distances the attacking aircraft from the blast effects"; over-the-shoulder "the bomb is released past the vertical so it is tossed back toward the target."

## [6] Heatblur, DCS: F-14 Tomcat manual

"CMPTR TGT: Computer target, a semi-automatic computer-guided mode similar to a CCRP mode in newer aircraft." "CMPTR PLT: Computer pilot ... Similar to a CCIP mode in newer aircraft." Computer target: "the pilot steers the aircraft in azimuth to place the target along the BFL ... designated by pressing the target designate switch ... When the upper solution cue reaches the velocity vector, the WCS automatically releases set stores on the condition that the bomb release button is depressed." Computer initial point: "a preset initial point (IP) is designated instead of the actual target ... ALT ... RNG ... BRG". Manual: "set at a deflection from the ADL according to desired engagement speed, dive angle, and release altitude ... using weapon engagement tables".

## [7] Heatblur, DCS: F-4E Phantom II manual, "Offset Delivery"

"Offset bombing provides a blind bombing capability ... The crew chooses a known reference point (IP) and obtains target position by a means of an offset to that reference point"; "a pair of offset values ... one in the North/South axis, the other East/West"; VIP "initiation of the navigation assistance is performed with a direct flyover of the VIP"; RIP "acquisition of this point using a radar lock".
