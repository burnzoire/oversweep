## G1 — Recovery-case selection

Source: “CATCC CONTROL CRITERIA / Case of Departure and Recovery”, printed
**p. 11-21**.
[Readable source](https://navyaviation.tpub.com/14342A/Catcc-Control-Criteria-377.html).

Selected source sentence (16 words):

> The Air Officer determines the case departure and recovery based upon the existing weather conditions.

Original paraphrase: the controller works with CATCC officers/supervisors to
select approach/control arrangements after the Air Officer determines the
recovery case. This supports role allocation as a general carrier concept,
without asserting exact minima or the current governing edition.

## G2 — Clearance, fix and final bearing

Source: “Aircraft Recovery Information / Marshal Recovery Information /
Marshal Pattern and Marshal Fix”, printed **p. 11-32**.
[Readable source](https://navyaviation.tpub.com/14342A/Aircraft-Recovery-Information-388.html).

Selected source sentences (23 words total):

> The marshal controller should provide an inbound flight with the following information for a Case III recovery:

> The FB is the extended-landing-area centerline.

Original paraphrase of the intervening list: recovery case, approach type,
expected final bearing, altimeter, marshal instructions, EAT, approach button,
time check, a vector if needed, and stack radial/altitude information.
The following paragraph describes a TACAN marshal fix on a radial and at a
distance appropriate to the aircraft category. This supports a general clearance
and fix explanation. Weather, divert and bingo fields in the module's P-816
example remain attributed to that example; this list does not independently
establish them.

## G3 — Jet/turboprop marshal

Source: “Jet/Turboprop Marshal”, immediately preceding Table 11-11,
printed **p. 11-33**.
[Readable source](https://navyaviation.tpub.com/14342A/Table-11-11-Standard-Cv-1-Marshal-Pattern-389.html).

Selected source sentence (10 words):

> The holding pattern is a left-hand 6-minute racetrack pattern.

Original paraphrase: the fix is normally behind the expected final bearing;
its assigned distance and altitude depend on the pattern. The inbound leg passes
over the fix. This independently corroborates that the six-minute left-hand
pattern is not unique to the T-45C publication. It does not establish one
universal speed or unrestricted authority to extend legs. P-816 supplies its
own two-minute-turn/one-minute-leg example separately.

## G4 — Approach-control role and BRC

Source: Table 11-1, “Shipboard operations terms and definitions”,
printed **p. 11-2**.
[Readable source](https://navyaviation.tpub.com/14342A/Table-11-1-Shipboard-Operations-Terms-And-Definitions-358.html).

Selected source phrase from BRC definition (8 words):

> The ship's magnetic heading during flight operations

Original paraphrase: Approach Control provides positive control during Case II
and III approaches and arranges space/interval for bolter/waveoff traffic.
This supports the general controller-led recovery concept and BRC definition;
it does not specify any aircraft's bolter climb, speed, gear/flap actions,
turn distances or lost-communications actions.
