## JP 3-09.3, Close Air Support (2014)

- p. I-1: CAS "may be the most detailed and continuous integration task performed by the joint force"; "CAS is air action by fixed-wing (FW) and rotary-wing (RW) aircraft against hostile targets that are in close proximity to friendly forces and requires detailed integration of each air mission with the fire and movement of those forces."
- p. I-2: "The word "close" does not imply a specific distance; rather, it is situational." "TAC is the authority to control the maneuver of and grant weapons release clearance to attacking aircraft. A certified and qualified ... JTAC or ... FAC[A] will be recognized across the Department of Defense". Type 1/2/3 definitions. TGO: "Unless certified and qualified as a JTAC or FAC(A), personnel conducting TGO do not have the authority to control the maneuver of, or grant weapons release clearance to, attacking aircraft."
- p. I-3: "The ground commander at the lowest level is responsible for the employment of CAS assets unless specifically retained by a higher level commander".
- p. I-4: "Aircrews retain the primary responsibility for developing weaponeering recommendations and aircraft employment tactics, while the JTAC or FAC(A) focuses on target effects." "The United States Air Force (USAF) calls these groupings of aircraft flights, while the ... USN and ... USMC call them either sections (two aircraft) or divisions (three-to-four aircraft)" (pp. I-4 to I-5).
- p. I-5: RW "excellent response and loiter times, can conduct low altitude or nap of the earth ingress"; "Army RW attack assets use close combat attack (CCA) procedures. CCA is not synonymous with CAS, and the Army does not consider its attack helicopters a CAS system"; "Marine Corps RW attack assets are trained and equipped for CAS ... Sections normally fly in mixed configuration with attack helicopter (AH)/UH"; SOF "employ CAS, CCA, gunship, and FAC(A) procedures"; JAAT.
- p. I-6: conditions for effective CAS; UA can "direct the UA aircrew to conduct the target talk-on, laser spot handoff".
- p. I-7: "Target marking builds SA, identifies specific targets in an array, reduces the possibility of friendly fire".
- p. I-8: "RW aircraft can often operate effectively under low ceilings ... FW aircraft can operate above blowing surface dust".
- p. I-10: friendly fire causes "misidentification of targets, inaccurate target locations or descriptions, target locations incorrectly transmitted or received, and loss of SA".
- p. III-30: IR position markers, GLINT tape; "aircrew will call "VISUAL" ... and either "TALLY" ... or "CONTACT SPARKLE" on each and every pass/attack prior to receiving clearance."
- p. III-36: troops in contact "is an advisory call ... does not remove the aircrews'/JTACs' responsibility"; REDs "0.1 percent probability of incapacitation".
- p. III-37: "Ordnance delivery inside the 0.1 percent Pi distance will be considered "danger close."" Initials; "axis of attack should be parallel to the friendly force's axis".
- pp. III-40 to III-41: Figure III-6 clearance calls (ABORT, CLEARED HOT, CONTINUE, CONTINUE DRY, CLEARED TO ENGAGE); "Ragin' 41, flight, CLEARED HOT"; "The word CLEARED will only be used when ordnance is actually to be delivered".
- pp. III-41 to III-42: PLA "a procedure of last resort"; "silence is not consent".
- p. III-43: no specific risk level per type; change of type before "IN" / "COMMENCING ENGAGEMENT"; Type 1 "withhold clearance until the attacking aircraft has completed maneuvering"; "deliveries of GPS or INS guided weapons should not be controlled under Type 1."
- pp. III-44 to III-45: Type 1 and 2 procedures; Type 2 examples "troops in contact, night, adverse weather, and high altitude or standoff weapons employment"; IN call "IN from the South" or "IN heading 360".
- p. III-46: Type 3 restrictions "time, geographic boundaries, FAH[s], specific target set"; COMMENCING ENGAGEMENT, ENGAGEMENT COMPLETE; "maintains abort authority".
- pp. III-47 to III-49: Figure III-7; BOT/BOC examples; "If the aircraft is never required to be TALLY/CAPTURED the target or CONTACT the mark, it is a BOC attack"; "Aircrew will not modify coordinates once read back"; misapplication causes "confusion ... potentially cause friendly fire".
- p. III-50: two types of control at once.
- pp. III-51 to III-52: SAM break turn, AAA jink; shot down "usually by threats they were not aware of"; "Latch 01, SAM launch southwest of the target."
- pp. III-75 to III-77: "from the overhead"; offset direction; FAH examples; restrictions read back.

- pp. III-93 to III-94: BP, firing points, masked; hovering, running, diving fire ("diving fire may produce the most accurate results").
