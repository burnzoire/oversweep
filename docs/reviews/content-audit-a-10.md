# Editorial audit — A-10 Thunderbolt II

- Content: `content/encyclopedia/aircraft/a-10.md`
- Reviewed: 2026-11-02
- Type: Editorial / learning-design; not an aviation SME certification

## 1. Is it too long?

**Slightly.** The narrative is lively, but the design competition chronology, nickname lineage and combat chronology occupy a large proportion of the page. The first section repeats the GAU-8 detail covered more rigorously in the linked gun article. Keep one short paragraph on the gun-centred design, and move deeper mechanism detail to the GAU-8 link. Compress the A-X/YA-9/A-7D selection story and the nickname paragraph; retain the manual-reversion story as a vivid illustration of redundancy.

## 2. Imagery or interactivity

Two photographs already serve distinct purposes (in-flight aircraft, cannon firing). **Do not add interactivity by default.** An annotated airframe image identifying high-mounted engines, straight wing, gun and protected cockpit would convey the design trade-offs better than additional text. If sourced legally, include one static design-feature image.

## 3. Intended learning outcomes

Readers should be able to explain the A-10's close-air-support role, the aircraft/gun design relationship, why low-speed handling and survivability were prioritised, how A-10A and A-10C capabilities differ, and where the aircraft has served. These outcomes are largely supported but not explicitly foregrounded.

## 4. Missing important content

- Distinguish A-10A, OA-10 and A-10C by relevant role/equipment rather than blending their features.
- The statement concerning 2026 combat operations and future retirement is time-sensitive. Verify it with dated primary sources and record the as-of date before retaining it.
- Different range/speed figures in the plate use different conditions. Present the conditions more clearly rather than flattening them to single 'best' numbers.
- Directly cross-link the weapons-delivery module where it adds conceptual value.

## Priority

**P1 — dated claims and variant clarity; P2 — tighten narrative.**

## 5. Flight simulator relevance and differences

Distinguish DCS A-10A from A-10C II and clarify which systems/variants are flyable. Real-world survivability and upgrade history are not necessarily simulated.

**Review action:** establish what is modelled, simplified, absent or unverified, citing the specific simulator, module, version and source/test. Keep real-world facts primary and put simulator-only behaviour in the established visually distinct sim-specific treatment where applicable. **Fidelity verification pending; no unsupported feature-absence claim is made here.**

**Applied (2026-11-05):** the retirement plan now gives the 2023 target (2028 or 2029) and the April 2026 delay to 2030, as of October 2026, when the sources were read, and the record links the Weapons Delivery and Close Air Support modules. The 2026 Iran operations claim already carries its year. The A-10A/OA-10/A-10C split and the plate's range conditions need sourced research.
