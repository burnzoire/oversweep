## [1] USAF, Electronic Warfare Fundamentals (November 2000)

- **6-25 (direct and indirect masking).** "Placing prominent terrain features
  between the aircraft and threat radar systems effectively negates these
  systems and is an integral part of threat avoidance during combat mission
  planning." Indirect: "a pulse radar system may not be able to differentiate
  the target return from the return generated by the terrain. Indirect terrain
  masking is most effective when the aircraft is flying abeam the radar site.
  Pulse Doppler radars and radar systems employing moving target indicator
  (MTI) circuits may be able to negate the effectiveness of indirect terrain
  masking." Radar horizon: 6-23 to 6-25, "assumes the radar is operating over
  water or level terrain".
- **8-1 to 8-2 (phases).** "three distinct phases in any missile intercept:
  boost, mid-course, and terminal"; terminal: "the missile attempts to pass
  close enough to the target to detonate the fuse".
- **8-3 (command guidance).** "This beacon is usually masked until missile
  booster separation. This results in the missile being launched unguided for
  the first 2-3 seconds. This type of delay is one of the reasons that all
  command-guided missile systems have a minimum launch range. Command guidance
  is used by the SA-2, SA-3, SA-4, and SA-8."
- **8-5.** Advantages: "command guidance is difficult to jam since the missile
  beacon antenna is at the rear of the missile and can be relatively
  high-powered." Disadvantages: "the accuracy of the intercept geometry is only
  as good as the tracking information provided by the target tracking radar.
  Jamming, interference, or loss of signal will adversely affect the intercept
  accuracy ... command guidance is reactive ... This results in missile
  maneuvering lagging target maneuvers."
- **8-6 (semi-active).** "illuminates it with CW energy. The missile then
  passively homes on the reflected CW energy"; "use velocity as the primary
  target discriminator".
- **8-8.** "a semi-active missile can be guided almost immediately after
  launch. This gives it a very small minimum range ... a much quicker reaction
  to target maneuvers"; "The use of chaff and beam maneuvers, which result in a
  near zero target Doppler, may cause a missile or radar to break lock. Third,
  if a break-lock occurs, a CW homing missile normally cannot regain target
  track and complete the intercept."
- **8-9 to 8-11 (active).** Active guidance "is only active during the
  terminal phase of flight. The mid-course phase usually employs semi-active or
  command guidance"; "an active missile is extremely difficult to jam"; "the
  missile may still be susceptible to electronic jamming during the mid-course
  phase ... Jamming the target tracking radar may affect the missile's ability
  to 'see' the target near the terminal phase."
- **8-11 to 8-12 (TVM/SAGG).** "the missile transmits raw engagement data to
  the ground-based fire control system (FCS) in order to generate uplink
  guidance commands"; "they can respond very quickly to any actions taken by
  the target".
- **8-12 to 8-13 (aimed AAA).** "The classic role of AAA is point defense ...
  airfields, bridges ... and SAM sites." "Smaller caliber AAA guns generally
  rely on optical target acquisition ... can also use tracer ammunition ...
  Larger caliber AAA systems, with slow rates of fire, long range, and long
  projectile TOF, generally use a TTR and a fire control computer". "All these
  computations are based on the assumption that the target will continue on
  the same heading, at the same altitude, and at the same airspeed during the
  projectile TOF."
- **8-14 (barrage).** "Sector or barrage fire tactics are employed when the
  aircraft cannot be accurately tracked ... especially effective for point
  defense for a fixed target. Attacking aircraft may have to fly a predictable
  flight path during weapons delivery. Sector/barrage fire can be directed to
  cover the expected attack directions and altitudes."
- **8-15.** "A familiarity with the guidance technique employed by specific
  threat systems is the key to understanding the jamming techniques,
  chaff/flare employment settings, and tactical maneuvers designed to counter
  these systems."
- **13-1.** WINDOW: "The disruption of German AAA fire control and ground
  control intercept (GCI) radars rendered these systems almost totally
  ineffective."
- **13-2.** "When used with jamming and maneuvers, chaff can cause TTRs to
  break lock or generate survivable miss distances if a SAM is fired".
- **13-6 to 13-7.** Chaff RCS "should be larger than the aircraft's RCS. The
  chaff bundles must also bloom within the resolution cell of the radar."
- **13-12.** "Burst chaff dispensing, employed during the final phase of an
  engagement ... can generate tracking errors or a radar break-lock. Burst chaff
  effectiveness is greatly enhanced when accompanied by jamming and evasive
  maneuvers".
- **13-13.** TWS: "the TWS radar will automatically switch to the chaff. After
  dispensing chaff, the pilot can maneuver vertically or horizontally to move
  the aircraft out of the resolution cell." Conical scan: "Maneuvering outside
  the overlapping scan area enhances chaff effectiveness".
- **13-14.** "Chaff is most effective against monopulse radars when employed on
  the beam in order to create the maximum angular tracking error."
- **13-15.** "For self-protection chaff to be effective, the aircraft velocity
  relative to the radar site must also be near zero This occurs when the
  aircraft's aspect to the radar is 90°, or on the beam."
- **14-1.** "every infantry unit down to the platoon level is equipped with
  shoulder-fired IR missiles".
- **15-2 to 15-4.** Rise time: "some advanced IR missiles now look for a rapid
  rise in IR energy as a trigger"; kinematic flare and the "flare rejection
  trigger"; covert flares: "pre-emptively dropping flares in a target area
  carries a significant risk ... enemy air defense systems that may not have
  known the aircraft's location will surely see the flares ... Automatic
  systems tend to error on the side of caution".
- **15-4.** "The flare or IR source must appear in the field of view of the IR
  missile at the same time as the aircraft ... the IR missile seeker tracks the
  most intense IR signature".
- **15-5.** "Maneuvers compound the IR missile's tracking problems and increase
  the distance between the aircraft and flare ... When engaged by an IR threat,
  one tactic is to immediately dispense a flare and maneuver to put the missile
  on the beam. This increases the line-of-sight rate the missile guidance
  system must process and gives maximum separation between the aircraft and
  the flare (Figure 15-5)." "With the IR threat on the beam, the pilot has the
  best chance for achieving a 'tally-ho' on the missile ... Classified tactics
  manuals provide aircraft specific maneuvers and flare dispensing programs to
  defeat the IR threat." Power: "The engines produce the largest IR signature
  and are the only source of IR radiation that the pilot can influence."
- **15-6.** "Use of afterburner should be minimized ... The pilot should not
  reduce the power setting below the minimum required to maintain sufficient
  maneuvering airspeed." "Maneuvering into the sun masks the aircraft's IR
  signature from most IR threat systems. When attacking a target defended by IR
  systems, an attack axis that places the sun behind the attacker may limit the
  effectiveness of these systems." Clouds and smoke diffuse IR energy. "the
  radar warning receiver (RWR) will provide no attack warning unless the threat
  uses some detectable radar energy for acquisition prior to launching".
- **15-7.** MAWS: "In earlier systems the sun, flares, or the wingman's
  aircraft have been known to cause false alarms."

## [2] MCH 11-F16 Vol 5 (10 May 1996)

- **125.** "Do not fly lower than the altitude where you can safely and
  effectively perform all assigned tasks. Refer to MCM 3-1 Vol II for specific
  threat capabilities."
- **126.** Low-altitude advantages: "Detection by threats may be delayed.
  Exposure time to surface-to-air threats is reduced. Threat systems are
  restricted to line-of-sight." Disadvantages include fuel, navigation and
  "Low altitude operations put you in the heart of AAA engagement zones."
  "Medium altitude may be the correct option when the major threat is AAA";
  advantage "Threat avoidance from certain systems; especially light AAA."
- **127.** Medium disadvantages: "Vulnerable to certain threat systems. Easier
  detection by threat due to radar LOS. Threat is not isolated above you; 3D
  threat attack axis exists."
- **146.** Dive bomb: "Recoveries may be accomplished above some small AAA
  threats" / "Exposure to the SAM and air-to-air threat increases
  significantly." HARB/HADB: "Recoveries may be accomplished above small
  arms/light AAA threats" / "Increased exposure to SAM and air-to-air threats."
- **166.** "Recoveries should be flown IAW Dash 34 safe escape, MCI 11-F16 Vol
  3, or minimum altitude for AAA, based on the type of munition, threat, and
  target area tactics."
- **167.** "A curvilinear approach consists of constantly changing heading,
  altitude, airspeed, and G loading to arrive on final for a short tracking
  solution, thus decreasing AAA hit probability ... a descending turn in
  30º–60º of bank".
- **172–173.** Pop-up: "While such tactics place us within the AAA and small
  arms environment, a properly planned and executed pop-up attack should give
  us our best possible odds against SAM or air-to-air threats and, in
  addition, can provide surprise and deception to enhance survivability against
  AAA."
- **202–203.** Egress reasons include "Unacceptable target area defenses" and
  "Battle damage"; "General priorities are: Leave target area. Get away from
  threat envelope. Regain mutual support." Separated pilot: "follow the egress
  plan and provide his own threat lookout while proceeding to the prebriefed
  tactical rendezvous point ... Most importantly, join with someone as soon as
  possible." "In mountainous terrain, an in-trail option may be appropriate to
  mask defenses." "Perform a battle damage check on each flight member."
  Wounded bird: "Past the FEBA, if unable to cross back over the FEBA, ejection
  in the safe area(s) should be considered"; "The first priority for the
  wounded bird's pilot is to communicate the aircraft's status."
- **260.** Vertical jinks: "pull to a planned climb angle, not more than 30º,
  delay 3 - 5 seconds, roll unloaded to inverted and pull through level flight
  to a dive angle 10º less than the highest climb angle attained ... may also be
  accomplished in the oblique". S turns: "a series of banked turns linked by
  unloaded reversals as defensive reactions to ground threats ... roll to 60º -
  70º of bank and make a level turn away from the threat."
- **275.** Terrain masking: "Thorough route study prior to flight is imperative
  ... look long-range in the FLIR for saddles in hills or the path of least
  resistance." Threat reaction: "It may be more important to maintain
  orientation than to defeat a threat that may not be targeting you ... it is
  important that reacting pilots communicate information such as reference
  headings, altitudes, and type of reactions to other flight members ... During
  all threat reactions, dispense chaff and/or flares as required and use ECM as
  required". Medium altitude: "you can use altitude separation to deconflict
  ... If individual flight members threat react, they can flow to the back of
  the formation ... consider keeping elements together". Low altitude: three
  options (lower SCP and check away with direct and indirect terrain masking;
  climb to or above the MSA; "a 30-45° bank oblique turn ... Once reaching the
  MSA, over bank to approximately 90-120° of bank and pull").
- **275–276.** "While in a low altitude notch maneuver, a technique to dispense
  chaff with G on the jet is to momentarily pull up to 5-10º nose high with 3-4
  Gs while simultaneously dispensing chaff ... the auto TFR will bring you back
  down".

## [3] Korean AF TTP 3-3 Vol 5, Basic Employment Manual F-16C (1 Oct 2005)

- **3-22.** Line abreast "diminishes the opportunity for a ground threat to be
  alerted by the leader's overflight and carry out a successful engagement on
  the wingman. (At ingress airspeeds AAA gunners have an additional 2 to 4
  seconds reaction time on a wedge wingman.)"
- **3-26.** Sector 3 "can detect bandits converting, as well as surface-to-air
  missiles (SAM) that may be fired from the front quadrant."
- **5-15 to 5-16.** "Orthogonal Break. This maneuver is similar to the 'last
  ditch' maneuver used to defeat a Surface-to-Air missile (SAM) that you have
  confirmed is guiding on your aircraft. From straight and level flight, 400
  KCAS minimum, initiate a level hard turn to place the simulated threat at 3
  or 9 o'clock ... start a hard out-of-plane pull perpendicular to the
  intercept axis of the threat. The perpendicular pull resembles part of a
  barrel roll ... until threat overshoot ... nose-high and probably between 30
  and 90 degrees of bank ... apply the 10 degree rule to drop your nose back
  below the horizon." Figure 5.4: "4 -5 Gs", "400 KCAS+", "Back on Course",
  "VIPER 2, SINGER 6 NORTHEAST, DEFENDING 290°".
- **5-26.** "Threat avoidance from certain systems; especially AAA and
  man-portable air defense systems (MANPADS)."
- **8-9.** "Testing has proven NVGs viable for the detection of surface-to-air
  missiles (SAM) and antiaircraft artillery (AAA)."
- **A1-79.** "When defending against a surface-to-air threat, fighters will
  transmit 'defending (magnetic heading)' and their current bullseye
  position." Examples: "RAMBO 1, MUD 6, NORTH, MEDIUM, DEFENDING 270, BULLSEYE
  240/15." and "COBRA 1, SINGER 6, SOUTH, DEFENDING 090, BULLSEYE 040/20."
- **A1-82.** "ITW will be passed when a friendly aircraft is within 5 NM of the
  briefed, maximum effective range of any active threat ... If a fighter has
  reported MUD, DEFENDING, or SINGER to a specified threat, no ITW is required".

## [4] FM 3-04.126 (16 February 2007)

- **3-7.** "Terrain flying includes appropriate tactical application of
  low-level, contour, and NOE flight techniques as appropriate, diminishing the
  enemy's capability to acquire, track, and engage aircraft."
- **3-8.** "Vegetation and terrain features masking an aircraft from visual and
  electronic detection significantly degrade the capability of threat weapons
  ... Companies use the highest terrain flight altitude for a specific
  condition. A higher flight altitude reduces difficulty in navigation, permits
  a higher airspeed, reduces hazards to terrain flight, and minimizes fatigue."
- **A-1.** Sensor types; radar: "A few older radar systems had blind
  speeds—called a Doppler notch ... modern radar systems cancel blind speeds
  ... it is impossible to be in Doppler notch of more than one radar"; "Some
  modern radar systems can and do track not only the movement of the aircraft
  itself but also the rotor blades." IR: "IR missiles require the operator
  visually detect the target ... susceptible to atmospheric conditions (haze or
  humidity), the signature of the aircraft and its background, flares, decoys,
  and jamming. Generally, IR systems are difficult to— Detect before launch
  ... Predict location ... Respond to ... Hard kill".
- **A-2.** Laser/DEW and optical/EO sensors; "they rely on LOS".
- **A-5.** Risk reduction: "Planning mission time earlier or later to take
  advantage of night operations. Planning J-SEAD at critical points ...
  Altering flight routes to avoid known AD areas. Altering formation size".
  A-19: "the aircrew has only seconds ... Indication (determines immediate
  action and deploys to cover). Perform evasive maneuver and expend
  countermeasures (if applicable), if masking terrain is not readily available.
  Perform actions on contact (decision to continue or abort mission)." A-20:
  "'missile 3 o'clock, break right' ... indications do not require evasive
  maneuvering, such as radar search or acquisition."
- **A-6 to A-7.** A-21: "Formations and spacing intervals should be selected to
  provide all aircraft maneuver space ... 'Team 2 break right, missile' or
  'Team 1, tracers, three o'clock, break left' ... it may be the only one
  receiving indications due to terrain, narrow radar beam, altitude, or
  maintenance problems."
