Hand-fly the ILS

You are on vectors to an ILS in cloud, level below the glide slope and a little off the localizer, at 140 knots with the gear and flaps down. Intercept the localizer, capture the glide slope, and fly both needles down to the decision altitude with nothing but the panel.

Each run has a different wind, and you are not told it: find the heading that holds the localizer. At the DA you will either see the runway environment or not. Then decide at once: land or go missed.

  • Full-scale localizer on final: discontinue the approach (go missed).
  • Do not descend below localizer minimums if more than one dot low or two dots high on the glide slope.
  • At the DA, land only with the runway environment in sight and in position to land. In this exercise, in position means within one dot on both needles.

The aircraft, the approach and its minimums are invented for the exercise. The run lasts about 90 seconds.

A joystick, HOTAS or any other controller: select Joystick and set the axis for each control. A standard game controller works without setup. On a phone or with a mouse, press and hold the buttons below the panel. Invert pitch keys swaps ↑ and ↓. Auto power holds 140 knots for you, so you only fly the attitude: a learning assist, not part of the technique. Turn it off once tracking the needles is easy. The HSI's distance is to the runway threshold, a simplification for the exercise: a real ILS DME usually measures from its own antenna.

Joystick, HOTAS or game controller

Connect a controller and press any of its buttons.

Select Set, then move that stick or lever through its travel. The stick sets a pitch and roll rate, so centred it holds the attitude you have, as in a real aircraft; the throttle sets the power directly. Tick Invert if a control works backwards. Once any axis is set, the game controller's default sticks are ignored; its buttons still work.
