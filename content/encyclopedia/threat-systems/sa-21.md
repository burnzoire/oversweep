## Overview

The S-400 Triumf, NATO reporting name **SA-21 Growler**, is a Russian mobile long-range surface-to-air missile system developed in the 1990s by NPO Almaz as an upgrade of the S-300 family; it was first called the S-300PMU-3. [[1]](#source-1) It was approved for service on 28 April 2007, and the first battalion went on combat duty on 6 August 2007. [[1]](#source-1) It can be integrated with the shorter-ranged [SA-22](record:sa-22) Pantsir for close-in defence. [[1]](#source-1)

Many of the published numbers are manufacturer claims, and the article flags several of them as needing better sources. Treat the figures here as claimed capabilities, not demonstrated ones. [[1]](#source-1)

## The system

A command post (55K6E) controls the battalions. The 92N6E multi-function radar tracks up to 20 targets at 340 km and guides the missiles, and the 91N6E panoramic radar has a 340 km detection range. [[1]](#source-1) Optional radars, including the 96L6E all-altitude detector and the Nebo-M VHF radar, add coverage out to 300–400 km. [[1]](#source-1) One system of eight battalions can control 72 launchers and up to 384 missiles. [[1]](#source-1)

## The missiles

The S-400 fires several missiles, so the envelope depends on which one a site has. The source's missile table gives: [[1]](#source-1)

| Missile | Range | Altitude | Guidance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 40N6E | 380 km | 30 km | Semi-active or active radar homing |
| 48N6DM / 48N6E3 | 240 km | 27 km | Semi-active radar homing |
| 48N6E2 | 200 km | 27 km | Semi-active radar homing |
| 9M96 / 9M96E2 | 120 km | 30 km | Active radar homing |
| 9M96E | 40 km | 20 km | Active radar homing |

The 40N6 is so large that a launcher carries two instead of four. [[1]](#source-1)

## Envelope

Figure 1 draws the 40N6E: 2–380 km (1.1–205 NM) and up to 30 km (98,000 ft). It needs the very long scale. ## On the radar warning receiver

The cited AN/ALR-67 threat table lists no S-400 radar, so this page gives no RWR symbol. That is a gap in one table, not evidence that a warning receiver would stay silent.

## History

The first claimed S-400 shoot-down came on 25 February 2022, when a system based in Belarus is reported to have downed a Ukrainian [Su-27](record:su-27) near Kyiv. [[1]](#source-1) Ukraine has since claimed several S-400 radars and launchers destroyed, including by ATACMS missiles and drones. [[1]](#source-1) India, which agreed in 2016 to buy five regiments, claimed to have used it in the May 2025 conflict with Pakistan. [[1]](#source-1) Other operators include Algeria, Belarus and China. [[1]](#source-1)
