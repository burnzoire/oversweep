## Overview

The M163 Vulcan Air Defense System mounts the M168, a version of the 20 mm M61 rotary cannon carried by most US combat aircraft since the 1960s, on a modified M113 armoured personnel carrier. [[1]](#source-1) It gave US Army ground units mobile short-range protection against low-flying aircraft and helicopters, replacing the M42 Duster, and was designed to work alongside the Chaparral missile. [[1]](#source-1) Deliveries began in 1968, and the US Army turned in its last in 1994, replacing it with Stinger systems such as the Avenger. [[1]](#source-1)

## How it engages

The gunner aims with an M61 optical lead-computing sight; the small AN/VPS-2 radar measures range only and cannot search for targets, so the M163 is a fair-weather system, usable at night with night-vision sights. [[1]](#source-1) The gun fires 3,000 rounds a minute in bursts of 10 to 100 rounds, or 1,000 a minute continuously. [[1]](#source-1) The 1984 PIVADS upgrade improved accuracy and eased the gunner's work, but not the short reach of the 20 mm round. [[1]](#source-1)

## Envelope

The effective range is only 1,200 m, and the standard HEI-T shells destroy themselves at about 1,800 m. [[1]](#source-1) The source gives no effective altitude, so Figure 1 draws the range only.

## On the radar warning receiver

In the DCS F-14, the AN/ALR-67 shows the M163's radar as **A**, the same symbol as the [ZSU-23-4](record:zsu-23-4) and [Gepard](record:gepard). [[2]](#source-2) In the DCS F-16C, the HARM targeting pod shows it as **A** in threat class 8. [[3]](#source-3) See the [F-16 HARM threat tables](record:f-16-harm-threat-tables).

## History

In US service it was mostly used against ground targets, as in Panama in 1989, and last saw combat in Desert Storm. [[1]](#source-1) In Israeli service, as the Hovet, it made three kills in Lebanon in 1982, including a Syrian [MiG-21](record:mig-21), the first jet shot down by an M163. [[1]](#source-1) Chile, Ecuador, Jordan, Morocco, Saudi Arabia and Thailand still operate it. [[1]](#source-1)
