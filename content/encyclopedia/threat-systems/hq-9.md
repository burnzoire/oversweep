## Overview

The HQ-9 (Hongqi-9, “Red Flag-9”) is a Chinese long-range surface-to-air missile with the reporting name **CH-SA-9**; the naval version is the HHQ-9. [[1]](#source-1) It is built by CASIC. Western analysts see it as equivalent to, or derived from, the Russian S-300, which China began importing in 1993; RUSI's Justin Bronk calls it a hybrid based on the [SA-20](record:sa-20) with American- and Israeli-influenced radar, seeker and command elements. [[1]](#source-1) The HQ-9A entered service in 2001. [[1]](#source-1)

## How it engages

The missile uses track-via-missile guidance: inertial guidance, mid-course updates and terminal radar homing. [[1]](#source-1) It is about 6.8 m long, weighs nearly two tons, carries a 180 kg warhead and reaches Mach 4.2. [[1]](#source-1) The export FD-2000 may use the HT-233 acquisition radar, Type 120 low-altitude radar and Type 305A AESA radar, and may be fitted with the YLC-20 passive radar against stealthy targets. [[1]](#source-1)

## Envelope

The source gives the HQ-9 a range of 120 km (65 NM), the export FD-2000 125 km, and the improved HQ-9B 260 km and a 50 km ceiling. [[1]](#source-1) The HQ-9B, reportedly tested in 2006, adds a passive infrared seeker and has its own reporting name, CH-SA-21. [[1]](#source-1) Figure 1 draws the basic HQ-9's 120 km only, because the source gives no altitude limits for it.

## On the radar warning receiver

The cited AN/ALR-67 threat table lists no HQ-9 radar, so this page gives no RWR symbol. That is a gap in one table, not evidence that a warning receiver would stay silent.

## History

China has deployed HQ-9s to Hotan in Xinjiang near Kashmir (2015) and to Woody Island in the South China Sea (2016). [[1]](#source-1) Pakistan's Army took the HQ-9P into service in October 2021, and its first combat use was in the 2025 India–Pakistan conflict. [[1]](#source-1) Turkey reportedly selected it in 2013, but the purchase did not go ahead. [[1]](#source-1) Other users include Morocco, Algeria, Azerbaijan, Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan. [[1]](#source-1)
