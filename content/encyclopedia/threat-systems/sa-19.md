## Overview

The 2K22 Tunguska is a Soviet tracked vehicle armed with both guns and surface-to-air missiles, protecting tank and infantry regiments from low-flying aircraft, helicopters and cruise missiles by day and night in all weather. **SA-19 Grison** is NATO's name for its missile. [[1]](#source-1) It was designed to fix the ZSU-23-4's short range and lack of early warning, and to counter aircraft built to survive 23 mm cannon fire, such as the [A-10](record:a-10) and the [AH-64](record:ah-64d) Apache. [[1]](#source-1) It was accepted into service on 8 September 1982 with four ready missiles; the 2K22M of 1990 carries eight. [[1]](#source-1) The 1998 Army handout calls the 2S6 the replacement for the ZSU-23-4. [[2]](#source-2)

## The battery

A battery has six 2S6 combat vehicles, each with 9M311 missiles and two 2A38 30 mm cannon, and up to three 2F77 transloader trucks. Targets can be shared out between the six vehicles over an encrypted radio link from a Ranzhir or PPRU battery command post. [[1]](#source-1)

The 1RL144 **Hot Shot** radar combines a 10 kW E-band acquisition radar on the rear of the turret with a 150 kW J-band monopulse tracking radar on the front, and tracks targets as high as 3,500 m (11,500 ft). [[1]](#source-1)

## How it engages

The tracking radar feeds target range, elevation and bearing to the fire-control computer, which lays the guns or sends course corrections to the missile. The gunner can track with a stabilised sight instead. [[1]](#source-1) The missile boosts to 900 m/s and sustains 600 m/s; the original 9M311 has a laser [proximity fuze](term:proximity-fuze), replaced by a radio [fuze](term:fuze) on the Tunguska-M1's 9M311-M1. [[1]](#source-1) The 1998 Army handout lists radar command guidance for the missile and a 4,000 m range for the 30 mm guns. [[2]](#source-2) The cannon fire alternately at a combined 3,900–5,000 rounds a minute. [[1]](#source-1)

## Envelope

The 9M311 engages at 1.5–8 km (0.8–4.3 NM) and 5–3,500 m (16–11,500 ft); the Tunguska-M1's 9M311-M1 reaches 2.5–10 km and 15–3,500 m. [[1]](#source-1) The 1998 Army handout gives 8 km and a 15 m minimum engagement altitude. [[2]](#source-2) Figure 1 uses the original 9M311.

## On the radar warning receiver

In the DCS [F-14](record:f-14), the AN/ALR-67 shows the Hot Shot search and track radar as **19**. [[3]](#source-3)

## History

The Tunguska has served Soviet and Russian forces since 1984, and was used by Russia in the 2008 South Ossetia war. Russian and Ukrainian forces both use it in the war that began in 2022. Export users include India, Morocco and Myanmar. [[1]](#source-1)
