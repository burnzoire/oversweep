## Overview

The FIM-92 Stinger is the American shoulder-fired surface-to-air missile that replaced the Redeye. [[2]](#source-2) All US services have deployed it to protect forces, forward bases and high-value sites. [[2]](#source-2) Besides the shoulder launcher it is fired from the [Avenger](record:avenger), the Bradley Linebacker, the LAV-AD and helicopters such as the [OH-58D](record:oh-58d) and [AH-64](record:ah-64d), and an air-to-air version arms helicopters. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2) For allied aircrew it is also a friendly weapon close to the front, so keep [IFF](term:iff) working: the shooter can interrogate a target before firing. [[2]](#source-2)

## How it engages

The Stinger is fire-and-forget: after launch the missile needs no further help from the operator, so the operator can take cover or engage another target. [[2]](#source-2) The seeker is passive infrared, so it emits nothing an aircraft can detect, and it steers by proportional navigation. [[2]](#source-2) Near the target its threat-adaptive guidance shifts the aim from the hottest point towards more vulnerable parts of the aircraft. [[2]](#source-2) Stinger POST added a dual infrared and ultraviolet seeker that better tells a target from countermeasures and background, and Stinger RMP made the missile reprogrammable as threats changed. [[2]](#source-2) It has an all-aspect engagement capability. [[2]](#source-2)

## Envelope

Wikipedia gives a range of up to 4,800 m (2.6 NM) and engagements up to 3,800 m (12,500 ft). [[1]](#source-1) The Marine Corps fact file on the FAS page gives 1–8 km and a 10,000 ft (3,046 m) ceiling. [[2]](#source-2) Figure 1 uses the Wikipedia figures. The sources disagree on the minimum range: the Wikipedia infobox gives about 0.1 miles (160 m) and the fact file 1 km. Neither gives a minimum altitude, so Figure 1 leaves both inner edges dashed.

## On the radar warning receiver

A passive infrared missile gives no radar warning. Look for the launch and missile warning cues, and use flares and terrain.

## History

Stinger's combat debut was in the 1982 Falklands War, with British special forces. [[1]](#source-1) The CIA supplied it to the Afghan mujahideen in the Soviet–Afghan War. [[1]](#source-1) Large numbers have been sent to Ukraine since 2022, and the United States ordered 1,468 more to replace them. [[1]](#source-1)
