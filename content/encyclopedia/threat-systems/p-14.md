## Overview

The P-14, NATO reporting name **Tall King**, is a two-dimensional VHF early-warning radar: it gives a target's range and bearing but not its height. [[1]](#source-1) Design began in 1955, and it was the first high-power VHF radar the Soviet Union developed; it was accepted into service in 1959. [[1]](#source-1) It was built in three versions, the static 1RL113 Lena and 44Zh6 Furgon and the transportable 5N84A Oborona-14, with similar performance; 731 Lenas were built from 1959 to 1976. [[1]](#source-1) It was superseded by the [Nebo](record:nebo) family.

## What it does

All P-14 versions were built for long-range detection and tracking of aircraft, [[1]](#source-1) the kind of radar an [IADS](term:iads) relies on for [early warning](term:early-warning). Its single antenna is a huge open-frame parabola 33 m across. [[1]](#source-1) Against jamming it can hop between four preset frequencies, cancel clutter and passive interference, and take bearings on a jammer with five extra direction-finding antennas. [[1]](#source-1) It can scan with a high beam to see higher, a low beam for more range at low and medium altitude, or alternate between them. [[1]](#source-1)

## Coverage

The source gives a range of 400 km and an altitude of 30 km, or 65 km on the high beam, with a range accuracy of 1.2 km and a bearing accuracy of 1.2°. [[1]](#source-1) As with any surface radar, an aircraft below the [radar horizon](term:radar-horizon) is hidden from it, so these figures apply to high targets.

## On the radar warning receiver

The DCS F-14's AN/ALR-67 table lists no symbol for the P-14. [[2]](#source-2)

## History

The P-14 has served in conflicts in the Middle East, Europe and Asia, and has been seen in use in the Russo-Ukrainian War. [[1]](#source-1) Upgraded versions still serve in military and air traffic control roles; operators have included Bulgaria, China, Egypt, Hungary, Iraq, Romania, Serbia, Ukraine, Vietnam and Russia.
