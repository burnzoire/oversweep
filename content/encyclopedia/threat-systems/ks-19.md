## Overview

The KS-19 is a towed 100 mm anti-aircraft gun, designed in 1947 to replace the 85 mm guns of the Second World War; 10,151 were built between 1948 and 1957. [[1]](#source-1) It was adopted by all the Warsaw Pact armies and fought with communist forces in Korea and Vietnam. [[1]](#source-1) Surface-to-air missiles have since pushed it out of most front-line service. [[1]](#source-1)

## How it engages

The gun has a power rammer and an automatic fuze setter, and a trained crew of 15 can fire up to 15 rounds a minute. [[1]](#source-1) Fire control is the PUAZO-6/19 director with a SON-9 or SON-9A radar, NATO name **Fire Can**; the gun's own sights can engage aircraft, but the radar makes it much more accurate. [[1]](#source-1) Its anti-aircraft shells are high-explosive and fragmentation, with time or proximity fuzes. [[1]](#source-1)

## Envelope

The KS-19 reaches far higher than the light guns: 15 km vertically with proximity fuzes and 12.7 km with time fuzes, and 21 km horizontally. [[1]](#source-1) These are maximum ranges, not effective ones, and the source gives no effective figure, so Figure 1 draws the maximum reach and overstates where it is likely to hit. Even so, it reaches far higher than any other gun in this section.

## On the radar warning receiver

The DCS F-14's AN/ALR-67 table lists no symbol for the KS-19 or for the Fire Can. [[2]](#source-2) In the DCS F-16C, the HARM targeting pod shows the SON-9 Fire Can as **FC** in threat class 3. [[3]](#source-3) Falcon BMS training mission 28, which practises SEAD and DEAD, includes a KS-19 among its targets. [[4]](#source-4) See the [F-16 HARM threat tables](record:f-16-harm-threat-tables).

## History

Iraq had an estimated 200 KS-19s before 1990, many around Baghdad from 1980 to 2003; in 1991 and 2003, coalition electronic warfare and precision weapons released from beyond gun range made them obsolete. [[1]](#source-1) In Ukraine, guns captured back from Russian forces at Balakliia in 2022 have been used against ground targets. [[1]](#source-1)
