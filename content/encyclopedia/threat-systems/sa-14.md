## Overview

The 9K34 Strela-3, NATO reporting name **SA-14 Gremlin**, was developed to replace the poorly performing 9K32 Strela-2 ([SA-7](record:sa-7)). It borrows heavily from the [Strela-2](record:sa-7) and was accepted into Soviet Army service in January 1974. It has been exported to more than 30 countries and was followed by the Igla ([SA-16](record:sa-16) and [SA-18](record:sa-18)). [[1]](#source-1)

## How it engages

The big change from the SA-7 is the seeker. It uses FM (con-scan) modulation, which is harder to [jam](term:electromagnetic-jamming) or [decoy](term:decoy) with [flares](term:decoy-flare) than the SA-7's AM spin-scan seeker. A pressurised nitrogen bottle on the launcher cools its lead sulphide detector, which then senses longer wavelengths (up to about 4.3 µm instead of 2.8 µm), so it tracks cooler targets at longer range. That gives it a limited forward-hemisphere capability against jets in favourable conditions. [[1]](#source-1) The 1998 Army handout lists passive, cooled infrared guidance and all-aspect capability. [[2]](#source-2)

The missile weighs 10.3 kg, flies at 470 m/s and carries a 1.17 kg blast-fragmentation warhead. [[1]](#source-1)

## Envelope

The article gives a 4.5 km (2.4 NM) maximum range and a 30–3,000 m (100–9,800 ft) engagement altitude; its summary lists a ceiling of 1,800 m against jets and 3,000 m against slow targets. [[1]](#source-1) The 1998 Army handout gives a 6 km range and a 0 m minimum engagement altitude. [[2]](#source-2) Neither source gives a minimum range, so Figure 1 draws 4.5 km and 30–3,000 m with the inner edge dashed.

## On the radar warning receiver

The SA-14 has no radar, so a radar warning receiver gives no warning of it.

## History

In the War in Abkhazia, a Georgian SA-14 shot down a Russian [Mi-8](record:mi-8mtv2) carrying refugees on 14 December 1992, and an SA-14 shot down a Georgian Air Force [Su-25](record:su-25) on 4 July 1993. [[1]](#source-1) Northern Alliance SA-14s are credited with eight Taliban [MiG-21](record:mig-21) and Su-22 fighters in 2000. In Iraq, a Strela-3 hit an Airbus A300 cargo aircraft after take-off from Baghdad in November 2003, which landed despite losing hydraulic power, and one shot down a Royal Navy Lynx over Basra in May 2006. [[1]](#source-1) The Strela-3 article attributes the loss of a British Sea Harrier over Bosnia on 16 April 1994 to an SA-14 [[1]](#source-1); the Igla article credits the same loss to an Igla (see [SA-18](record:sa-18)).
