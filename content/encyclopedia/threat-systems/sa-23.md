## Overview

The S-300VM Antey-2500, NATO reporting name **SA-23 Gladiator/Giant**, is a Russian development of the army's S-300V, the [SA-12](record:sa-12). [[1]](#source-1) It is built first against short- and medium-range ballistic missiles, and also engages cruise missiles, aircraft, stand-off jamming aircraft and precision-guided munitions. [[1]](#source-1) Like the SA-12, it is carried on tracked vehicles, unlike the wheeled [SA-10](record:sa-10) and [SA-20](record:sa-20). [[1]](#source-1)

## The system

The Antey-2500 has a command post, circular and sector scan radars, and a multichannel missile guidance station that can illuminate 24 targets. [[1]](#source-1) Two launcher types carry their own illumination and targeting radar: the 9A82M with typically eight missiles and the 9A83M with typically twelve; loader-launchers carry more. [[1]](#source-1) The missiles fly on inertial guidance with radio command updates and home by semi-active radar at the end. [[1]](#source-1)

The 9M82M missile is meant for tactical, theatre and medium-range ballistic missiles as well as aircraft at up to 200 km. [[1]](#source-1)

## Envelope

Figure 1 draws 200 km (108 NM) and 30 km (98,000 ft). [[1]](#source-1) The variant list in the same source credits the S-300VM with 250 km, so treat the range as approximate. [[1]](#source-1) The source gives no minimum range or altitude, so those edges are dashed.

## On the radar warning receiver

The cited AN/ALR-67 threat table lists no S-300V-family radar, so this page gives no RWR symbol. That is a gap in one table, not evidence that a warning receiver would stay silent.

## History

A Russian battery of the later S-300V4 was deployed to Tartus in Syria in October 2016. [[1]](#source-1) In the war in Ukraine, Ukrainian forces have attacked S-300V4 and S-300VM systems with HIMARS rockets and drones. [[1]](#source-1) Egypt bought four Antey-2500 batteries, delivered by the end of 2017, and Venezuela operates one battalion. [[1]](#source-1)
