## Overview

The M48 Chaparral is an American self-propelled surface-to-air missile system that fires the MIM-72, a ground-launched version of the [AIM-9 Sidewinder](record:aim-9). [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2) It was a stop-gap after the radar-guided Mauler failed: the Army chose an infrared missile because it would not be confused by ground clutter. [[1]](#source-1) It worked alongside the [M163](record:m163) Vulcan, which covered short, quick engagements while Chaparral took longer ones. [[1]](#source-1) The first battalion deployed in May 1969, and it was the Army's standard short-range, low-altitude system until it was retired in the 1990s. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2)

## How it engages

The gunner aims the launcher by eye or with a forward-looking infrared sight, the seeker locks onto the target's heat, and the missile is fired and forgotten. [[2]](#source-2) Early seekers could lock only onto a target's tail; the final MIM-72G used the Stinger POST rosette-scan seeker, which resists infrared jammers. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2) The launcher carries four ready missiles, with eight more in the vehicle. [[2]](#source-2) The separate Forward Area Alerting Radar gave the fire units early warning and tentative identification. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2)

## Envelope

The FAS page gives a maximum range of about 4,800 m (2.6 NM) and a maximum effective altitude of about 2,500 m (8,200 ft), with a Mach 2.5 missile. [[2]](#source-2) Wikipedia gives 180 m as the minimum arming distance and 5 km as the range. [[1]](#source-1) Figure 1 uses the FAS figures with the 180 m minimum; no minimum altitude is given, so that edge is dashed.

## On the radar warning receiver

A passive infrared missile fired from an optically aimed launcher gives no radar warning. Look for the launch and missile warning cues.

## History

US forces never fired Chaparral in combat. [[2]](#source-2) An Israeli Chaparral shot down a Syrian MiG-17 over the Golan Heights on 16 May 1974, and Israel used it in the 1982 Lebanon War. [[1]](#source-1) Taiwan adopted the naval Sea Chaparral. [[1]](#source-1)
