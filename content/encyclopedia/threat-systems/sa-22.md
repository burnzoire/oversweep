## Overview

The Pantsir-S1, NATO reporting name **SA-22 Greyhound**, is a Russian self-propelled system that carries both missiles and anti-aircraft guns. [[1]](#source-1) It was built by KBP of Tula as the successor to the [SA-19](record:sa-19) Tunguska, to defend military, industrial and government sites against aircraft, helicopters, precision munitions, cruise missiles and drones, especially at low and very low altitude. [[1]](#source-1)

## How it engages

The combat vehicle carries up to twelve 57E6 missiles in two groups of six and two 2A38M 30 mm cannon. [[1]](#source-1) The acquisition radar detects targets at 32–36 km; the tracking radar follows both the target and the missile, at 24–28 km against a 2 m² target. [[1]](#source-1) An electro-optical channel, with a thermal imager and infrared direction finder, gives a second, passive way to track; the radar and optical channels can engage two targets at once, or four on later versions. [[1]](#source-1)

The 57E6 has no seeker. The vehicle tracks target and missile and sends steering commands over a radio link, which keeps each missile cheap. [[1]](#source-1) A booster burns for about 2 seconds and drops away; the sustainer carries a 20 kg continuous-rod and fragmentation warhead with contact and radar [proximity fuzes](term:proximity-fuze). [[1]](#source-1) Two missiles are normally fired at each target. [[1]](#source-1)

## Envelope

The 57E6 reaches 18 km (9.7 NM) and 15 km (49,000 ft). [[1]](#source-1) The source gives no minimum for the missile, so those edges are dashed. The guns fire 2,500 rounds a minute each, out to 4 km and up to 3 km, and can engage targets at ground level. [[1]](#source-1)

## On the radar warning receiver

In the DCS F-16C, the HARM targeting pod shows the Pantsir's “Trick Shot” radar as **22**, in threat class 1. [[2]](#source-2) See the [F-16 HARM threat tables](record:f-16-harm-threat-tables). The DCS [F-14](record:f-14)'s AN/ALR-67 table has no Pantsir entry.

## History

A Syrian Pantsir-S1 is reported to have scored the type's first kill by shooting down a Turkish RF-4E [Phantom](record:f-4) off Latakia on 22 June 2012. [[1]](#source-1) Russian Pantsirs were deployed to Syria from 2015, and a Russian display in 2017 claimed kills of several drones and rockets there. [[1]](#source-1) In Libya, the United Arab Emirates deployed Pantsirs for the Libyan National Army in 2019, and a US MQ-9 Reaper was shot down by one on 22 August 2022. [[1]](#source-1)
