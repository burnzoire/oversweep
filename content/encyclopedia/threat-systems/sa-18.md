## Overview

The 9K38 Igla, NATO reporting name **SA-18 Grouse**, is the full-capability version of the Igla. It is an all-new design rather than an improved Strela, and its 9M39 missile was accepted into Soviet Army service in 1983, two years after the simplified Igla-1 ([SA-16](record:sa-16)). [[1]](#source-1) It is in service with more than 30 countries. The naval version is SA-N-10, and the later Igla-S of 2004 is SA-24 Grinch. [[1]](#source-1)

## How it engages

The main gains over the Igla-1 are much better resistance to [flares](term:decoy-flare) and jamming and a more sensitive seeker. Its 9E410 seeker works in both infrared and ultraviolet, which makes it harder to decoy with flares and effective against lamp-type infrared jammers. It widens forward-hemisphere engagement and can, in favourable conditions, engage a fighter approaching head-on. [[1]](#source-1) The rocket burns harder and shorter for a higher peak speed (600 m/s average, 800 m/s peak), and a needle-like spike on the seeker dome cuts drag; *igla* means needle. [[1]](#source-1) The 1.17 kg warhead uses delayed impact, magnetic and grazing fuzes. [[1]](#source-1)

## Envelope

The article gives the Igla a maximum range of 5.2 km (2.8 NM) and lists a 3.5 km (11,000 ft) flight ceiling without saying which variant it applies to. It gives no minimum range or altitude. [[1]](#source-1) Figure 1 uses 5.2 km and 3.5 km with the inner edge and floor dashed.

## On the radar warning receiver

The SA-18 has no radar, so a radar warning receiver gives no warning of it.

## History

On 16 April 1994 a Bosnian Serb Igla shot down an RAF Sea Harrier over Goražde; the pilot ejected and was rescued (the Strela-3 article credits the same loss to an [SA-14](record:sa-14)). On 30 August 1995 an Igla brought down a French Mirage 2000D over Pale, and both crew were captured. [[1]](#source-1) On 13 May 2016 PKK militants shot down a Turkish Army AH-1W SuperCobra with an SA-18. [[1]](#source-1) Ukrainian forces have reported shooting down Russian [Su-25s](record:su-25) and an Su-34 with Igla systems since 2022. [[1]](#source-1)
