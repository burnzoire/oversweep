## Overview

The 9K333 Verba (“willow”) is a Russian fourth-generation shoulder-fired missile developed to replace the Igla family, including the [SA-24](record:sa-24) Igla-S. [[1]](#source-1) The list of NATO reporting names gives it as **SA-29 Gizmo**. [[2]](#source-2) Russian ground and airborne units have received it since 2014, and it officially entered service in 2015. [[1]](#source-1)

## How it engages

Its main advance is a multispectral seeker with three sensors, ultraviolet, near-infrared and mid-infrared, against the Igla-S's two. [[1]](#source-1) Cross-checking the three helps it tell the target from [decoy flares](term:decoy-flare) and makes it harder to disrupt, including by lasers meant to blind it. [[1]](#source-1) The maker says it can engage aircraft and helicopters, drones and cruise missiles with a low infrared signature, and that it can be linked to an external [IFF](term:iff) unit. [[1]](#source-1)

Expect a heat-seeking missile that is harder to decoy with flares alone than older types.

## Envelope

The source gives 6.5 km (3.5 NM) range and a 4.5 km (14,800 ft) ceiling, with a missile speed of up to 500 m/s. [[1]](#source-1) No minimum range or altitude is given, so those edges are dashed.

## On the radar warning receiver

A passive optical missile gives no radar warning. Look for missile warning cues and the launch itself.

## History

Russian forces deployed Verba near Damascus in 2017 to protect the capital against drones flown by Hayat Tahrir al-Sham. [[1]](#source-1) Armenia operates it, and Iran is reported to have bought it for delivery from 2027. [[1]](#source-1)
