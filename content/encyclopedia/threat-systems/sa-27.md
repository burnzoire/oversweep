## Overview

The 9K317M Buk-M3 is the latest production version of the Buk family that began with the [SA-11](record:sa-11) and continued with the [SA-17](record:sa-17). [[1]](#source-1) The US Department of Defense calls it **SA-27**, and NATO's reporting name is **Gollum**. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2) It is built on new hardware, with 36 target channels per battalion, and its launcher carries six missiles in tubes instead of four on rails. [[1]](#source-1) Trials began in 2015 and the first brigade set was delivered in 2016. [[1]](#source-1) Rosoboronexport offers an export version, the Buk-M3 “Viking”. [[1]](#source-1)

## How it engages

The missile can fly on commands from the ground, home on its own active radar, or combine the two, and it reaches 1,550 m/s. [[1]](#source-1) The radar covers ±60° and detects a target flying 7–10 m above the ground at up to 35 km, or a cruise missile such as the JASSM at 17–18 km. [[1]](#source-1) Launches can follow each other one second apart, and the source claims a single-shot hit probability of 0.95 against aircraft. [[1]](#source-1) The system also has a thermal (infrared) channel for day and night tracking. [[1]](#source-1)

## Envelope

The source gives 2.5–70 km (1.3–38 NM) and 15 m to 35 km (50 to 115,000 ft), against targets up to 3,000 m/s; it also says the missile can hit targets as low as 5 m. [[1]](#source-1) These are manufacturer-style claims rather than tested figures. The Viking export version can also fire the Antey-2500's longer-range missiles, reported to extend its range from 65 to 130 km. [[1]](#source-1)

## On the radar warning receiver

The DCS [F-14](record:f-14)'s AN/ALR-67 table has no Buk-M3 entry; it lists the SA-11's radars. Whether a real warning receiver labels a Buk-M3 as an earlier Buk depends on its threat library, which the public sources do not give.

## History

Ukraine reported two Russian Buk-M3 systems destroyed in February 2024, and Ukrainian drones destroyed two Buk-M3 and one Buk-M2 launchers in Kursk in April 2025. [[1]](#source-1)
