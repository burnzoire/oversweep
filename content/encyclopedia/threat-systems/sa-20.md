## Overview

The S-300PMU-1 and S-300PMU-2 are export developments of the S-300P air defence system, and NATO calls them **SA-20A** and **SA-20B Gargoyle**. [[1]](#source-1) They grew out of the [SA-10](record:sa-10) family: the S-300PMU (SA-10C) was the last export version under the old name. [[1]](#source-1) The PMU-1, introduced in 1993, was the first land-based S-300 to use the larger 48N6 missile and mixed missile types in one system; it also brought the 30N6E **Tomb Stone** fire-control radar. [[1]](#source-1) The PMU-2 Favorit, introduced in 1997, added the 48N6E2 missile and is described as able to engage short- and medium-range ballistic missiles as well as aircraft. [[1]](#source-1) China built its own version of the PMU-1, the HQ-15. [[1]](#source-1)

## The system

A PMU-2 system uses the 83M6E2 command system, made up of the 54K6E2 command post and the 64N6E2 surveillance radar, with the 30N6E2 radar for fire control, illumination and guidance. [[1]](#source-1) Like the PMU-1, it controls up to 12 launchers, any mix of self-propelled and towed, each with four missiles. [[1]](#source-1) It can also use the 96L6E all-altitude radar and the 76N6 low-altitude radar. [[1]](#source-1) Figure 3 shows one system as the source describes it; real sites vary.

## The missiles

Because a PMU-1 or PMU-2 site can mix missiles, the envelope depends on which one is fired. [[1]](#source-1)

- **5V55R**: carried over from the SA-10. [[1]](#source-1)
- **48N6E**: the larger missile, introduced with the PMU-1; its warhead is 143 kg. [[1]](#source-1)
- **48N6E2**: the PMU-2's missile, with a range of 195 km (105 NM). [[1]](#source-1)
- **9M96E1 and 9M96E2**: much smaller missiles with 24 kg warheads and gas-dynamic control, ranging 1–40 km and 1–120 km respectively; a launcher still carries four. [[1]](#source-1)

## Envelope

Figure 1 draws the PMU-2's 48N6E2 out to 195 km (105 NM) [[1]](#source-1) and up to 27 km (89,000 ft), the ceiling the S-400 article gives the same missile; that article puts its range at 200 km. [[2]](#source-2) Neither source gives a minimum range or altitude, so those edges are dashed. The smaller 9M96 missiles reach 40 or 120 km. [[1]](#source-1)

## On the radar warning receiver

The DCS [F-14](record:f-14)'s AN/ALR-67 table lists no SA-20 radar; it shows the SA-10's Flap Lid as **10**, Big Bird as **BB** and Clam Shell as **CS**. [[3]](#source-3) Whether a real warning receiver labels an SA-20 site as an SA-10 depends on its own threat library, which the public sources do not give.

## Operators

Users include China (the first PMU-2 customer, in 2004), Azerbaijan (two PMU-2 battalions, 2010) and Vietnam. [[1]](#source-1)
