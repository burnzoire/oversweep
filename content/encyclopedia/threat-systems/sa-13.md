## Overview

The 9K35 Strela-10, NATO reporting name **SA-13 Gopher**, is a highly mobile, short-range SAM that is visually aimed and uses optical/infrared guidance. It is mainly meant for low-altitude threats such as helicopters. It succeeded the 9K31 Strela-1 ([SA-9](record:sa-9)) and can also fire that system's missiles. [[1]](#source-1) After disappointing trials in 1973–74 it was accepted into service on 16 May 1976. [[1]](#source-1)

## The vehicle

It rides on a modified MT-LB tracked vehicle, with more room for equipment and reloads than the SA-9's BRDM-2. [[1]](#source-1) The 1998 Army handout describes four container-launcher boxes on a pivoting pedestal with a range-only radar, and four launcher vehicles in each SAM platoon of the air defence battery. [[2]](#source-2) Two launcher versions exist: the 9A34 with the 9S86 **Snap Shot** range-only radar between the missile pairs, and the 9A35 with the 9S16 **Flat Box-B** passive detector. A separate MT-LBU can carry the 9S80 **Dog Ear** acquisition radar. [[1]](#source-1)

## How it engages

The 9M37 missile is about 2.2 m long, weighs 40 kg and carries a 3.5 kg warhead at up to nearly [Mach](term:mach-number) 2. [[1]](#source-1) Its two-channel seeker combines a cooled photocontrast channel with an infrared channel, and the photocontrast channel lets it engage head-on. [[1]](#source-1) The Army handout lists passive, cooled infrared guidance and all-aspect capability. [[2]](#source-2) The system was chosen partly because it reacts quickly and is immune to heavy radio-frequency jamming. [[1]](#source-1)

## Envelope

The 9M37 intercepts targets at 800 m to 5 km (0.4–2.7 NM) and 10 to 3,500 m (33 to 11,500 ft). Those figures bound the intercept zone: launch distances are longer against an approaching target and shorter against a receding one. [[1]](#source-1) The 1998 Army handout gives a 7 km range and a 10 m minimum engagement altitude. [[2]](#source-2) Figure 1 uses 0.8–5 km and 10–3,500 m.

## On the radar warning receiver

The missile homes passively. The Snap Shot and Dog Ear radars emit, but the cited AN/ALR-67 table gives no symbol for either, so this page lists none.

## History

On 20 February 1988 a Cuban Strela-10 shot down a South African Mirage F1AZ over southern Angola. In the 1991 Gulf War, at least two US [A-10A](record:a-10) losses on 15 February are believed to have been Strela-10s, and a Strela-10 hit a US Air Force A-10 over Kosovo on 11 May 1999. [[1]](#source-1) A published video shows a Strela-10 missile fired at a Russian Ka-52 helicopter being decoyed by [flares](term:decoy-flare). [[1]](#source-1)
