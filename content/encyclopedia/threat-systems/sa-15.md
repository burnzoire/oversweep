## Overview

The Tor, NATO reporting name **SA-15 Gauntlet**, is an all-weather, low-to-medium-altitude, short-range SAM against aircraft, helicopters, cruise missiles, drones and short-range ballistic threats. Development began on 4 February 1975 as the successor to the 9K33 Osa ([SA-8](record:sa-8)), and the land system was accepted into service on 19 March 1986. [[1]](#source-1) The Tor-M1 of 1991 introduced the 9M331 missile, greater accuracy and two targets at once. [[1]](#source-1)

## The vehicle

Like the SA-8, each vehicle is self-contained. Its turret carries a search radar on top, a tracking radar on the front and eight missiles stored vertically between them. [[1]](#source-1) The F-band pulse-Doppler search radar reaches 25 km, detecting an F-15-size aircraft at 6 km altitude with a probability of 0.8 at that range; the fire-control system detects up to 48 targets, tracks ten and includes [IFF](term:iff). The G/H-band (later K-band) phased-array engagement radar reaches 20 km. NATO calls the two radars together **Scrum Half**. [[1]](#source-1) The original Tor engaged one target with two missiles; the Tor-M1 engages two targets with up to four missiles. [[1]](#source-1)

A battery of four Tor vehicles is usually accompanied by a Ranzhir-M mobile command centre, which shares out targets and links the battery into a wider air defence network. [[1]](#source-1) The 1998 Army handout lists SAM regiments of five batteries of four SA-6, SA-8 or SA-15 each. [[2]](#source-2)

## How it engages

The missiles are cold-launched: ejected from the vehicle before the motor fires, then turned toward the target by thrust vectoring. They fly on command guidance with radar proximity fuzes, manoeuvre at up to 30 g and can engage targets flying at up to [Mach](term:mach-number) 2. The 9M330 carries a 15 kg warhead and peaks at about Mach 2.8. [[1]](#source-1)

## Envelope

The article gives an engagement range of up to 12 km (6.5 NM), a minimum range of 1,500–2,000 m depending on version and an effective altitude of 6–10,000 m, but flags these as needing a citation. [[1]](#source-1) For the Tor-M1 it gives a 1.5 km minimum range and a 10 m minimum height, and its 9M331 data gives a 6,000 m ceiling. [[1]](#source-1) Figure 1 uses 1.5–12 km and 10–6,000 m.

## On the radar warning receiver

In the DCS F-14, the AN/ALR-67 shows the Scrum Half search and track radar as **15**. [[3]](#source-3)

## History

In January 2020 Iran shot down Ukraine International Airlines Flight 752; Iran's Civil Aviation Organisation said two Tor-M1 missiles were fired at it. [[1]](#source-1) Israel reportedly destroyed an Iranian-supplied Tor at Syria's T-4 air base on 9 April 2018, before it was operational. [[1]](#source-1) Operators include Russia, Iran, Egypt, Greece, Cyprus and Belarus. [[1]](#source-1)
