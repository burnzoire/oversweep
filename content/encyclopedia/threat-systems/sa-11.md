## Overview

The 9K37 Buk, NATO reporting name **SA-11 Gadfly**, succeeded the 2K12 Kub ([SA-6](record:sa-6)). Development began on 17 January 1972, and the modernised 9K37M1 Buk-M1 was adopted in 1983 with better radars, kill probability and resistance to electronic countermeasures. [[1]](#source-1) Later versions with the 9M317 missile carry a different name, [SA-17 Grizzly](record:sa-17). [[1]](#source-1)

## The battalion

Each component of the Buk (acquisition and targeting radars, command, launchers and reloads) rides on its own tracked vehicle, so the system moves with the army and is harder to find than a fixed site. [[1]](#source-1) A standard battalion has a command vehicle, a target acquisition radar (TAR), six transporter-erector-launcher-and-radar vehicles (TELARs) and three transporter-erector-launchers (TELs); a battery is two TELARs and one TEL. [[1]](#source-1)

Each TELAR carries four ready-to-fire missiles and its own 9S35 **Fire Dome** H/I-band tracking and engagement radar. The TELs look like TELARs but carry a crane instead of a radar; they can fire their missiles, but only with a TELAR guiding them. [[1]](#source-1) The 9S18M1 **Snow Drift** acquisition radar detects out to 85 km and sees an aircraft at 100 m from 35 km away. [[1]](#source-1)

## How it engages

The command element sorts friend from foe ([IFF](term:iff)), prioritises targets and passes them to the launchers. The missile needs a radar lock to steer it at first, until its own seeker takes over, and a [proximity fuze](term:proximity-fuze) sets off the 70 kg fragmentation warhead. [[1]](#source-1) The 9M38 is a single-stage solid-fuel missile with semi-active radar homing and a top speed of [Mach](term:mach-number) 3. [[1]](#source-1)

## Envelope

The article's 9M38 data gives an operational range of 30 km (16 NM) and a flight altitude of 14,000 m (46,000 ft), with no minimum range or altitude. [[1]](#source-1) Figure 1 uses those figures, and its inner edges are drawn dashed.

## On the radar warning receiver

In the DCS F-14, the AN/ALR-67 shows the Fire Dome track radar as **11** and the Snow Drift search radar as **SD**. [[3]](#source-3)

## History

In the 2008 South Ossetia war, US officials said a Georgian Buk-M1 caused the loss of a Russian Tu-22M and contributed to the losses of three [Su-25s](record:su-25). A Buk shot down Malaysia Airlines Flight 17 over eastern Ukraine on 17 July 2014, killing 298 people. [[1]](#source-1) Ukraine has fitted some Buk-M1 launchers to fire US RIM-7 Sea Sparrow missiles.
