## Overview

The ZSU-23-4 Shilka is a lightly armoured, radar-directed self-propelled anti-aircraft gun with four 23 mm cannon. Development began in 1957 and it entered service in 1965, replacing the [ZSU-57-2](record:zsu-57-2) in the air defence of motor rifle and tank regiments. [[1]](#source-1) It was succeeded by the 2K22 Tunguska ([SA-19](record:sa-19)). [[1]](#source-1) Afghan soldiers called it the "sewing machine" for the sound of its guns. [[1]](#source-1)

## How it engages

The turret carries four liquid-cooled 2A7 cannon linked to the RPK-2 Tobol radar, NATO name **Gun Dish**. [[1]](#source-1) The Ku-band radar detects aircraft out to 20 km and tracks well, but picks up much ground clutter below about 60 m and struggles to track automatically inside 7–8 km, where the target's angular rate is high. [[1]](#source-1) An electro-mechanical computer works out shell flight time and lead from the radar's data and lays the guns; each burst of 3–10 rounds is aimed afresh. [[1]](#source-1) The Army handout lists its guidance as optical or radar, for acquisition, tracking and ranging. [[2]](#source-2)

The source describes it as effective against attack aircraft and helicopters in any weather and light, with a very high density and accuracy of fire. [[1]](#source-1) Its 23 mm shells are small, though, and the source judges it less able than modern 30 mm systems against jet attack aircraft and cruise missiles. [[1]](#source-1)

## Envelope

The guns can throw a shell 7 km horizontally and 5.1 km vertically, but the effective height is 1.5 km at a slant range of 2.5 km against a target flying at 250 m/s. [[1]](#source-1) The Army handout gives 2,500 m. [[2]](#source-2) Figure 1 uses 2.5 km and 1,500 m.

## How it is deployed

From the 1970s a Soviet regiment's anti-aircraft battery had one platoon of four ZSU-23-4s and one of four [SA-9](record:sa-9) or later [SA-13](record:sa-13) missile vehicles, covering the low-level gaps of the division's [SA-6](record:sa-6)s. [[1]](#source-1) The 1998 Army handout gives the same battery of four ZSU-23-4s and four SA-9 or SA-13s. [[2]](#source-2) So where there is a Shilka, expect short-range missiles too.

## On the radar warning receiver

In the DCS F-14, the AN/ALR-67 shows the Gun Dish track radar as **A**, the same symbol it uses for the [Gepard](record:gepard) and the [M163](record:m163). [[3]](#source-3) In the DCS F-16C, the HARM targeting pod shows it as **A** in threat class 3, and the default HARM table 2 lists it as ALIC code 121. [[4]](#source-4) In Falcon BMS, AAA as a whole has HARM code 692, symbol **AAA**. [[5]](#source-5) See the [F-16 HARM threat tables](record:f-16-harm-threat-tables).

## History

In the 1973 Yom Kippur War, Israeli pilots who flew low to avoid the [SA-6](record:sa-6) were often shot down by ZSU-23-4s. [[1]](#source-1) It served in the Iran–Iraq War and the 1991 Gulf War, and in Afghanistan and Chechnya its guns' high elevation made it useful against positions on mountainsides. [[1]](#source-1) The source credits its appearance with significant changes in NATO's low-level tactics over the battlefield. [[1]](#source-1) It remains in service with many armies, including in Ukraine. [[1]](#source-1)
