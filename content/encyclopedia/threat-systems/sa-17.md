## Overview

The 9K37M1-2 Buk-M1-2, which entered service in 1998, introduced the 9M317 missile to the Buk ([SA-11](record:sa-11)) family. With it the system received a new NATO reporting name, **SA-17 Grizzly**, and the GRAU designation 9K317, which covers all later versions. [[1]](#source-1) The new missile let the system engage ballistic missiles and surface targets and enlarged its envelope against aircraft and helicopters; the Buk-M1-2 can still fire the older 9M38. [[1]](#source-1) The export name is Ural. The Buk-M2 adds a third-generation phased-array fire-control radar. The later Buk-M3 is SA-27. [[1]](#source-1)

## The battalion

The organisation is the same as the [SA-11](record:sa-11)'s: a command vehicle, a target acquisition radar, six TELARs and three TELs. Each Buk-M1-2 TELAR, on the GM-569 chassis, has a crew of four, a fire-control radar at the front and four ready-to-fire missiles, and can guide up to three missiles at one target. It can also be fitted with a thermal camera and laser rangefinder for passive tracking. [[1]](#source-1)

The Buk-M2 TELAR uses a passive electronically scanned array instead of the original mechanically scanned dish. Its 9S36 vehicle carries a radar on a mast but no missiles and can guide two TELs against up to four targets, for use in forested or hilly ground. [[1]](#source-1)

## How it engages

The 9M317 uses inertial correction with semi-active radar [homing](term:homing) and proportional navigation. [[1]](#source-1) The missile weighs 715 kg against the 9M38's 690 kg. [[1]](#source-1) The system's kill probability is estimated at 70 to 93% per missile against an aircraft. [[1]](#source-1)

## Envelope

The article's Buk system data, which does not name a variant, gives aircraft engagement at 3–42 km (1.6–23 NM) and 15 m to 25 km (49 to 82,000 ft). It describes the 9M317's envelope as up to 45 km in range and 25 km in altitude. [[1]](#source-1) Figure 1 uses the 3–42 km system figures.

## On the radar warning receiver

The cited AN/ALR-67 table names the Fire Dome and Snow Drift radars only as SA-11 (**11** and **SD**; see [SA-11](record:sa-11)), and has no separate entry for the SA-17.

## History

Russia has used the Buk-M1-2 and later versions in Ukraine: on 27 February 2022 a Ukrainian TB2 drone destroyed a Russian Buk-M1-2 TELAR and TEL near Malin. [[1]](#source-1) Syria received Buk-M2E systems in 2011, and Venezuela operates the Buk-M2E. [[1]](#source-1)
