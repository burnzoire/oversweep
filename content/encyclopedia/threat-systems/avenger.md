## Overview

The AN/TWQ-1 Avenger is an American self-propelled short-range air defence system: a turret on a heavy HMMWV carrying two pods of four [FIM-92 Stinger](record:stinger) missiles and a .50 calibre machine gun. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2) It defends ground units against cruise missiles, drones, low-flying aircraft and helicopters. [[1]](#source-1) Boeing developed it as a private venture in the 1980s; the Army ordered it in 1987 and took the first unit in November 1988. [[1]](#source-1) The Marine Corps also used it, in low-altitude air defence units. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2)

## How it engages

The gunner sits in the turret and finds targets by eye, or with a forward-looking infrared sight, a laser rangefinder and a video autotracker at night and in poor weather. [[2]](#source-2) A driven reticle shows where the missile seeker is looking, so the gunner can confirm lock before firing; the fire sequence, including lead and super-elevation, is automatic. [[2]](#source-2) The turret is stabilised, so it fires on the move, and covers 360° and −10° to +70° in elevation. [[2]](#source-2) The machine gun covers the area inside the Stinger's minimum range. [[2]](#source-2)

With the slew-to-cue upgrade, a radar picture from the air defence network can swing the turret onto a target automatically. [[2]](#source-2) The FAS page gives a 10 km acquisition range. [[2]](#source-2)

## Envelope

The Avenger's reach is the Stinger's. Figure 1 draws the Stinger figures from the [Stinger](record:stinger) record's source: up to 4,800 m (2.6 NM) and 3,800 m (12,500 ft). [[3]](#source-3) That source's minimum range is about 0.1 miles (160 m), while the Marine Corps fact file gives 1 km, and no minimum altitude is given; Figure 1 leaves both inner edges dashed. [[3]](#source-3)

## On the radar warning receiver

Avenger has no radar of its own, so an aircraft's warning receiver will not detect it. Look for the launch and missile warning cues, and remember that a network radar may be cueing it.

## History

Avenger first deployed in the build-up to the 1991 Gulf War and later supported NATO operations in Bosnia. [[1]](#source-1) The Army had 26 Avenger battalions in 2004 but only nine by 2017; the Stryker-based IM-SHORAD is replacing it. [[1]](#source-1) In 2017 Avenger-fired Stingers with proximity fuzes shot down drones in a test. [[1]](#source-1) The United States sent Avengers to Ukraine from late 2022 to defend against cruise missiles and Shahed drones. [[1]](#source-1)
