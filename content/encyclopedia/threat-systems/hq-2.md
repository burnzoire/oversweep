## Overview

The HQ-2 (Hongqi, “Red Flag”) is China's development of the Soviet S-75, the [SA-2](record:sa-2). [[1]](#source-1) China first built the S-75 under licence as the HQ-1, with extra electronic counter-countermeasures against the jammers on Taiwanese U-2s; the HQ-2 added more. [[1]](#source-1) The IISS reporting name is **CH-SA-1**. [[3]](#source-3) It was China's primary air defence system for over forty years and has been replaced by the HQ-22 since 2016. [[2]](#source-2) Iran's Sayyad-1 is an HQ-2 variant. [[2]](#source-2)

## How it engages

Like the S-75, the HQ-2 guides its missile by radio commands from a ground radar. [[1]](#source-1) Upgraded HQ-2s remain in service, and the latest versions use the SJ-202 passive phased-array radar, which can track and engage several targets at once at 115 km and 80 km and does the work of several older single-function radars. [[1]](#source-1)

## Envelope

The cited sources give no HQ-2 engagement limits, so this page draws no envelope. The [SA-2](record:sa-2) page gives the S-75's limits; the HQ-2 is a modified S-75 and is likely to be broadly similar, but the sources do not say so. The HQ-3 development raised the ceiling to 30 km and the range to 42 km, but only 150 were built before it was withdrawn. [[1]](#source-1)

## On the radar warning receiver

The DCS [F-14](record:f-14)'s AN/ALR-67 table has no HQ-2 entry. It lists the SA-2's Fan Song track radar as **2** and the Flat Face search radar as **FF**. [[4]](#source-4) Whether a real warning receiver labels an HQ-2 as an SA-2 depends on its threat library.

## History

Chinese S-75s shot down a Taiwanese RB-57D near Beijing on 7 October 1959, the type's first kill, and went on to down more Taiwanese reconnaissance aircraft and drones. [[1]](#source-1) Operators have included Albania, Iran and Pakistan, whose air force retired its HQ-2B in 2020. [[1]](#source-1)
