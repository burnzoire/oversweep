## Overview

The P-19 Dunai, NATO reporting name **Flat Face B**, is a mobile two-dimensional UHF radar developed from the P-15 and accepted into service with the Soviet air defence forces in 1974. [[1]](#source-1) Like the P-15, it was built to detect aircraft flying at low altitude, and it is closely associated with the S-125, the [SA-3](record:sa-3). [[1]](#source-1) It was widely exported and is still in service, although the Kasta series has superseded it. [[1]](#source-1)

## What it does

The P-19 is a surveillance and target-acquisition radar, best known as the search radar of SA-3 batteries. [[1]](#source-1) It is highly mobile: the antennas ride on one ZiL-131 truck and the electronics on another. [[1]](#source-1) It scans in azimuth mechanically, and a PRV-11 **Side Net** height finder is usually set up with it to measure elevation. [[1]](#source-1) Against jamming it can jump to one of four preset frequencies, and a coherent Doppler filter removes passive interference. [[1]](#source-1) Czech and French evaluations, the French one of a radar captured in Chad in 1987, describe it as sturdy, reliable and good at low altitude. [[1]](#source-1)

## Coverage

The source gives a range of 260 km and a frequency of 830–875 MHz, but no altitude limit. [[1]](#source-1) Low-flying aircraft are its purpose, but it cannot see below its [radar horizon](term:radar-horizon) either.

## On the radar warning receiver

In the DCS F-14, the AN/ALR-67 shows the Flat Face as **FF**, listed as the search radar of the SA-2, SA-3 and SA-5. [[2]](#source-2) In the DCS F-16C, the HARM targeting pod shows the P-19 as **S** in threat class 2, and the default HARM table 3 lists it as ALIC code 122. [[3]](#source-3) In Falcon BMS, the Flat Face is the SA-3's early-warning radar, with HARM code 403, symbol **F**. [[4]](#source-4) See the [F-16 HARM threat tables](record:f-16-harm-threat-tables).
