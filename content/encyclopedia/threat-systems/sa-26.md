## Overview

The Pechora-2M is a Russian modernisation of the S-125, the [SA-3](record:sa-3), which the list of NATO reporting names gives the designation **SA-26**; no reporting name is listed. [[2]](#source-2) It keeps the S-125's command-guided missile concept but replaces the search radar and adds optical and thermal tracking. [[1]](#source-1) Egypt's refurbished S-125s were reintroduced as the S-125 Pechora 2M under a separate overhaul contract. [[1]](#source-1)

The public detail on this version is thin. Most of the performance figures in the source carry a “citation needed” tag, so treat the numbers on this page as unverified.

## How it engages

The Kasta 2E2 radar replaces the original early-warning radar, and launchers can be sited up to 10 km from the control centre. [[1]](#source-1) The source credits the system with day and night optical tracking and a thermal imager, so it can track without radiating, and with resistance to active jamming and [anti-radiation missiles](term:arm). [[1]](#source-1) It can also fire at cruise missiles. [[1]](#source-1)

## Envelope

The source gives 2.5–32 km (1.3–17 NM) and 20 m to 20 km (66 to 66,000 ft) with the 5V27DE missile, against targets up to 1,000 m/s, but flags these figures as needing a citation. [[1]](#source-1) It also gives a detection range of about 100 km against a 2 m² target, falling to about 50 km against 0.15 m² and 40 km under active jamming. [[1]](#source-1)

## On the radar warning receiver

The DCS [F-14](record:f-14)'s AN/ALR-67 table has no Pechora-2M entry. It lists the original SA-3's Low Blow track radar as **3** and the Flat Face search radar as **FF**. [[3]](#source-3) Whether a real warning receiver shows a Pechora-2M as an SA-3 depends on its threat library.

## Operators

The source lists Pechora-2M batteries with Egypt (40 S-125-2M batteries in 2024), Mongolia, Myanmar, Syria, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan. [[1]](#source-1)
