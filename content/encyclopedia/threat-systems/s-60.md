## Overview

The S-60 is a towed 57 mm anti-aircraft gun designed by Vasiliy Grabin's bureau; it passed field tests in 1946 and was accepted into Soviet service in 1950, replacing the 37 mm guns of the divisions. [[1]](#source-1) It was sold to at least 37 countries and built under licence in Poland, Hungary and China, where the copy is the Type 59. [[1]](#source-1) Its twin version arms the [ZSU-57-2](record:zsu-57-2). [[1]](#source-1)

## How it engages

The S-60 is normally directed from a battery fire-control post. The PUAZO-5A director, developed from a German wartime computer, was teamed with Grom-2 radars of 10 cm wavelength; the whole system was called the SON-9, NATO name **Fire Can**. Later the RPK-1 Vaza replaced the director. [[1]](#source-1) The Army handout gives each battery of six guns one fire control centre with the **Flap Wheel** radar. [[2]](#source-2) The gun fires 105–120 rounds a minute, with a muzzle velocity of 1,000 m/s. [[1]](#source-1)

## Envelope

The effective firing range is 6,000 m under radar control. [[1]](#source-1) The Army handout gives 4,000 m and 6,000 m for the gun on and off its carriage, with optical or radar guidance. [[2]](#source-2) Neither source gives an effective altitude, so Figure 1 draws the range only. In Vietnam the S-60 was most effective between 460 and 1,500 m. [[1]](#source-1)

## How it is deployed

A Soviet divisional anti-aircraft regiment had two batteries of six guns; air defence forces regiments had four batteries, 24 guns in all. [[1]](#source-1) The 1998 Army handout gives a light infantry division an anti-aircraft gun regiment of four batteries, each of six S-60s and three shoulder-launched SAMs. [[2]](#source-2) Iraq deployed them in battalions of 36 guns to defend divisional headquarters and artillery. [[1]](#source-1)

## On the radar warning receiver

The gun itself has no radar. The DCS F-14's AN/ALR-67 table lists no symbol for it or for the Fire Can. [[3]](#source-3) In the DCS F-16C, the HARM targeting pod shows the SON-9 Fire Can as **FC** in threat class 3. [[4]](#source-4) See the [F-16 HARM threat tables](record:f-16-harm-threat-tables).

## History

The source calls the S-60 the keystone of North Vietnam's low-altitude air defence. [[1]](#source-1) The Soviet Union had begun replacing its divisional guns with missiles in the mid-1960s, but Vietnam showed what guns could do against low-flying aircraft, and many were brought back from storage to back up SAMs that were weak at low altitude. [[1]](#source-1) It served in the Middle East wars, the Iran–Iraq War, both Gulf Wars and Georgia in 2008, and in Ukraine both sides have used it, mostly against ground targets, and against drones. [[1]](#source-1)
