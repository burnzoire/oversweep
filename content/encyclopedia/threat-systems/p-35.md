## Overview

The P-35 Saturn, NATO reporting name **Bar Lock**, is a two-dimensional E/F-band radar for early warning and for ground-controlled interception: guiding fighters onto targets. [[1]](#source-1) Developed by the VNIIRT institute from the earlier P-30, it was accepted into service in 1958 by the Soviet air defence forces, air force and navy. [[1]](#source-1) The P-35M of 1961 improved the antennas, and the Mech-35 of 1971 scanned faster, saw better below 300 m and could vary its pulses and frequency against jamming. [[1]](#source-1) It was succeeded by the P-37. [[1]](#source-1)

## What it does

The P-35 has two horizontal open-frame antennas, each fed by a stacked beam of six horns, and an [IFF](term:iff) array on the upper antenna to identify the aircraft it detects. [[1]](#source-1) Unlike the P-30, it does not measure height with a V-beam. [[1]](#source-1) 

## Coverage

The source gives a range of 350 km and an altitude of 25,000 m, with a range accuracy of 500 m, a beam 0.7° wide and six 1 MW transmitters. [[1]](#source-1) Like every surface radar, it cannot see an aircraft below its [radar horizon](term:radar-horizon).

## On the radar warning receiver

The DCS F-14's AN/ALR-67 table lists no symbol for the Bar Lock. [[2]](#source-2) In Falcon BMS, the Bar Lock is the [SA-5](record:sa-5)'s early-warning radar, with HARM code 405, symbol **5A**. [[3]](#source-3) See the [F-16 HARM threat tables](record:f-16-harm-threat-tables).

## History

The P-35 passed to the Soviet Union's successor states and was exported; Czechoslovakia operated it in the 1980s, and some still serve. [[1]](#source-1)
