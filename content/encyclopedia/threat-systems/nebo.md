## Overview

The Nebo ("sky") radars are a family of Soviet and Russian VHF surveillance radars from the Nizhny Novgorod Research Institute of Radio Engineering (NNIIRT), which has developed VHF radars since 1948. [[1]](#source-1) The 55Zh6 Nebo, NATO name **Tall Rack**, passed acceptance trials in 1982 as the first Soviet VHF radar that measures height as well as range and bearing. [[1]](#source-1) The 1L13 also dates from 1982, and the 1L13-3 Nebo-SV, NATO name **Box Spring**, from 1985. [[1]](#source-1) They succeeded the [P-14](record:p-14). [[3]](#source-3)

## What it does

These are surveillance radars that find aircraft at long range for the air defence network; they do not guide weapons. The Nebo-M system, built around the Nebo-U's successor, claims to detect fifth-generation aircraft such as the F-22 and F-35. [[2]](#source-2)

The 55Zh6U Nebo-U of 1992 kept the Tall Rack name; it has been integrated with the SA-21 system around Moscow. [[1]](#source-1) Now the VHF element of the Nebo-M system, it is easy to tell from the older 1L13 because its polarisation is vertical rather than horizontal. [[2]](#source-2) The Nebo-UM has been delivered to Russian air defence regiments since 2018. [[2]](#source-2)

## Coverage

The Nebo-U has a range of 600 km and can track objects flying at up to Mach 6.4. [[2]](#source-2) The sources give no altitude limit, and no figures for the 1L13.

## On the radar warning receiver

In the DCS F-14, the AN/ALR-67 shows the 1L13 and 55G6 early-warning radars as **S**. [[4]](#source-4)
