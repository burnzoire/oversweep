## Overview

The ZU-23-2 is a Soviet towed anti-aircraft gun with two 23 mm cannon, developed in the late 1950s to engage low-flying aircraft and helicopters out to 2.5 km and to defend troops and key sites. [[1]](#source-1) It entered Soviet service in 1960; some 140,000 were built in the Soviet Union, and Bulgaria, Poland, Egypt and China built it under licence. [[1]](#source-1) It is one of the most widespread air defence weapons in the world, and a pilot should expect it anywhere ground forces are.

## How it engages

Two crew aim and fire the guns by hand. The ZAP-23 optical-mechanical sight uses target data entered by hand to give limited automatic aiming, [[1]](#source-1) and the 1998 Army handout lists its guidance simply as optical. [[2]](#source-2) Each cannon fires at up to 2,000 rounds a minute cyclic and 400 a minute in practice, from 50-round belts. [[1]](#source-1)

Because it has no radar, it gives no warning on a radar warning receiver: the first sign is usually tracer. It can only engage what its crew can see, so it is a daylight, clear-weather weapon unless fitted with one of the many later sights, some of which add laser rangefinders, television and thermal channels. [[1]](#source-1)

## Envelope

The source gives an effective range of 2–2.5 km and an effective altitude of 1,500–2,000 m; [[1]](#source-1) the Army handout gives 2,500 m. [[2]](#source-2) Figure 1 uses 2.5 km and 2,000 m. Its main targets are helicopters and low, slow aircraft; in the 1991 Gulf War the source describes it as ineffective against fast jets. [[1]](#source-1)

## How it is deployed

The gun rides a two-wheeled trailer that is lowered onto its mount to fire, but it can fire from its wheels in an emergency. [[1]](#source-1) It is just as often bolted onto trucks, pick-ups and tracked carriers such as the MT-LB, which is why it appears on "technicals" in many recent wars. [[1]](#source-1) In the 1998 Army handout, a light motorized infantry brigade has an anti-aircraft gun battery of two platoons of four ZU-23s, sometimes mixed with four [ZSU-23-4s](record:zsu-23-4). [[2]](#source-2)

## History

In Vietnam the ZU-23, with the 37 mm M1939, was the anti-aircraft gun US crews met most often; with 83 percent of USAF losses caused by ground fire, it probably shot down hundreds of aircraft. [[1]](#source-1) Georgian forces shot down a Su-25 with one in 1993, and in Syria truck-mounted ZU-23s are credited with several helicopters. [[1]](#source-1) In 2020 a ZU-23 manned by Ethiopian troops in Somalia shot down an EMB 120 on approach that arrived from an unexpected direction at low level, killing all six aboard. [[1]](#source-1) It has also been used against drones, by India in 2025. [[1]](#source-1)
