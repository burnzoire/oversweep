## Role today

Bombora, outside Gudauta in Abkhazia, is a Russian military airfield, part of Russia's 7th military base as of 2014. [[2]](#source-2) Its 3,000 m runway can take all combat and military transport aircraft, and its sea end lies only 60–70 m from the shore, so aircraft can take off straight out over the water at very low level, where radar finds them harder to see. [[2]](#source-2)

## History

Black Sea Fleet bombers were based at the airfield in the Second World War. [[2]](#source-2) The 171st Fighter Regiment flew from it from 1950 to October 1982, with La-7s, MiG-15s, MiG-17s, Yak-28Ps and finally Su-15TMs, before moving to Ugolny on Chukotka; the Air Defence Forces' 529th Fighter Regiment followed from 1982 to 1992, with Su-27s in the last years of the Cold War. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2) Between 1963 and 1969 it also took passenger flights to Moscow and Leningrad, with Il-18s and Tu-104s. [[2]](#source-2)

The 345th Independent Guards Airborne Regiment was later based at the airfield; after the Soviet collapse it became Russia's 50th military base and then a peacekeeping regiment. [[1]](#source-1) At the 1999 OSCE Istanbul summit Russia agreed to close the base and withdraw, but Abkhaz authorities block OSCE inspection visits and no date for withdrawal has been set. [[1]](#source-1)

## The airfield

A single concrete runway, 15/33, 3,000 m long, lengthened twice, in the early 1960s and the early 1970s. [[2]](#source-2) [[3]](#source-3)
