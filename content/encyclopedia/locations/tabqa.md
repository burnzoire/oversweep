## Role today

Tabqa was the Syrian government's last stronghold in Raqqa Governorate when the Islamic State took it in August 2014. [[4]](#source-4) The Syrian Democratic Forces captured it from the Islamic State in March 2017. [[1]](#source-1) OurAirports lists it as closed and OpenStreetMap tags its runway as disused. [[2]](#source-2) [[3]](#source-3) Who holds it now is not described in the sources consulted.

## History

The Islamic State began attacking the base around 10 August 2014. [[4]](#source-4) The army reinforced it, and its garrison beat off assaults on 19–22 August that began with suicide bombings at the gate, in part with an ambush and minefield laid by special forces. [[4]](#source-4) On 24 August, with most of the army already withdrawing towards Ithriya and a small garrison left behind, the Islamic State broke in and took the base. [[4]](#source-4) Fifteen MiG-21s and all the helicopters had been flown out; one MiG-21 was destroyed. [[4]](#source-4) About 200 soldiers were killed in the battle, and the Islamic State executed at least 160 of the soldiers it captured on 27–28 August. [[4]](#source-4)

On 22 March 2017 US aircraft airlifted Syrian Democratic Forces fighters and US troops across the Euphrates and Lake Assad to open the battle for Tabqa, its dam and its air base, with US Marine artillery in support. [[1]](#source-1) The SDF announced that it had stormed the air base and, after a 24-hour fight, captured it completely; Islamic State fighters withdrew to Tabqa city, and the SDF repelled a counter-attack near the base on 2 April. [[1]](#source-1)

## The airfield

One concrete runway, 09/27, listed at 9,842 ft by 131 ft. [[2]](#source-2) OpenStreetMap tags it as disused. [[3]](#source-3)
