## Role today

Beirut–Rafic Hariri is the only working commercial airport in Lebanon, the hub of Middle East Airlines and, with the Port of Beirut, the country's main port of entry; it took the name of the former prime minister Rafic Hariri after his assassination in 2005. [[1]](#source-1) The Directorate General of Civil Aviation runs it and Lebanon's air traffic control. [[1]](#source-1) Its security has long been entangled with Hezbollah: in 2008 the government found a hidden camera network in the terminals and fired the security chief, Hezbollah fighters took to the streets in response, and in June 2024 *The Telegraph* reported that Hezbollah stored Iranian weapons at the airport. [[1]](#source-1)

## History

The airport opened on 23 April 1954 with two asphalt runways, replacing Bir Hassan Airfield. [[1]](#source-1) On the night of 28 December 1968, in reprisal for an attack on an El Al airliner in Athens, Israeli commandos raided it and destroyed 14 Lebanese airliners. [[1]](#source-1) The civil war from 1975 cost it almost all its airlines; Israeli shelling badly damaged the terminal in the 1982 invasion, and in 1983 the airport was the site of the Beirut barracks bombing, in which 241 American servicemen were killed. [[1]](#source-1)

A ten-year rebuilding began in 1994, adding a new terminal and two concrete runways: 17/35, built out into the sea to keep landing traffic away from the city, and a new 03/21 alongside the old one, which became a taxiway. [[1]](#source-1) In 2004 the runways were renamed after their headings were measured more accurately: the sea runway became 16/34 and the old 18/36 became 17/35. [[1]](#source-1) On 13 July 2006, at the start of the 2006 Lebanon War, Israeli missiles struck all three runways, saying Hezbollah received weapons through the airport; it reopened on 17 August, and Israel's air blockade ended on 7 September. [[1]](#source-1)

## The airfield

Three runways: 03/21 for take-offs, the longest at 12,467 ft; 16/34 for landings, built out into the sea; and the old 17/35, still open but rarely used. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2)
