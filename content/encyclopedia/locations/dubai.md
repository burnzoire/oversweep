## Role today

Dubai International was the world's busiest airport by international passengers in 2025, handling more than 95 million passengers, and it is the main hub of Emirates and the base of flydubai. [[1]](#source-1) It is due to close once the expanded Al Maktoum International Airport at Dubai World Central is fully working. [[1]](#source-1) The Dubai Royal Air Wing has had its own VIP pavilion and hangars at the airport since July 2008. [[1]](#source-1)

The Iran war of 2026 hit it directly. Its airspace closed on 28 February 2026 when Iran launched strikes on Dubai, and Iranian strikes later damaged the airport and injured four people there. [[1]](#source-1) It reopened with a few flights on 2 March, stopped again after Iranian drone strikes on 7 March, and was still in limited operation at the end of March. [[1]](#source-1)

## History

Imperial Airways flying boats began calling at Dubai in 1937. [[1]](#source-1) Sheikh Rashid bin Saeed Al Maktoum ordered an airport in 1959, and it opened on 30 September 1960 with a compacted-sand runway; an asphalt runway followed in May 1965, and the first jets, Middle East Airlines and Kuwait Airways Comets, visited in May 1966. [[1]](#source-1) Terminal 1 opened in 1971 and a second, parallel runway in April 1984. [[1]](#source-1) The original runway was closed by the 2000s to make way for Concourse C, and Terminal 3, built for Emirates, opened in October 2008. [[1]](#source-1)

## The airfield

Two parallel asphalt runways, 12L/30R, 4,351 m long, and 12R/30L, 4,447 m. [[1]](#source-1)
