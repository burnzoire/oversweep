## Role today

RAF Akrotiri is a large Royal Air Force base in the Western Sovereign Base Area, one of the two parts of Akrotiri and Dhekelia, a British Overseas Territory on Cyprus. [[1]](#source-1) Its only permanent flying unit is No. 84 Squadron's [search and rescue](term:sar) helicopters, but it is the RAF's base for operations across the eastern Mediterranean and [Middle East](record:middle-east): Typhoons flying for Operation Shader, tankers and transports, and surveillance aircraft and drones that come and go with each operation. [[1]](#source-1) Two US Air Force U-2s are stationed there, still monitoring the Egypt–Israel ceasefire and flying missions in Central Command's area. [[1]](#source-1)

## History

The station was built in the mid-1950s and was a base for the Suez Crisis in 1956, when its Meteor night fighters, Canberra photo-[reconnaissance](term:reconnaissance) aircraft and Venom ground-attack aircraft were reinforced by more Canberras. [[1]](#source-1) From 1957 to 1969 four Canberra squadrons gave Britain's Baghdad Pact, later CENTO, allies first a conventional and from November 1961 a nuclear [strike](term:strike) force, and two Vulcan squadrons followed until 1975. [[1]](#source-1) After 1966 Akrotiri was the only RAF flying station left on the island. [[1]](#source-1) The Turkish invasion of Cyprus in 1974 led the UK to withdraw most of its aircraft. [[1]](#source-1)

US U-2s arrived in August 1970 to watch the Suez Canal ceasefire, and after the Yom Kippur War of 1973 the operation, OLIVE HARVEST, became permanent. [[1]](#source-1) Akrotiri received American casualties from the 1983 [Beirut](record:beirut) barracks bombing, was used in the US strike on Libya in 1986, evacuated people from Lebanon in 2006 and staged support aircraft for the 2011 Libya campaign. [[1]](#source-1) Tornados flew from it over Iraq from August 2014 and RAF aircraft struck the Islamic State from September; it supported the 2018 missile strikes on Syria, and on 12 January 2024 four of its Typhoons struck Houthi targets in Yemen. [[1]](#source-1)
