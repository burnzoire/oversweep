## Role today

Senaki is the home of the Georgian Land Forces' 2nd Infantry Brigade, whose base, built to NATO standards for 3,000 troops under a US military aid programme, opened on 30 April 2008 at a cost of about $17 million. [[1]](#source-1) No published runway or elevation data was found for the airfield, so the figures on this page are measured from community mapping and satellite terrain data. [[4]](#source-4) [[5]](#source-5)

## History

From December 1945 the Soviet 56th Guards and 176th Fighter Regiments of the 283rd Fighter Division were based at the airfield; the 56th was disbanded in September 1960. [[1]](#source-1) In 1989 a MiG-29 of the 176th Regiment was flown from Senaki to Turkey and returned a day and a half later. [[1]](#source-1)

Construction of the brigade base began in April 2005. [[1]](#source-1) On 11 August 2008, during the Russo-Georgian War, Russian forces entered Senaki and took the military base; they seized 1,728 firearms there and later brought Georgian prisoners and US-supplied Humvees from Poti to it, before withdrawing from Senaki on 13 September 2008. [[2]](#source-2) A Russian assessment held that Russian air losses would have been higher had Georgian forces not left part of their Buk-M1 air-defence systems near Senaki. [[2]](#source-2)

## The airfield

A main runway of about 2,350 m running east–west, with a shorter gravel strip to its north-east, as mapped in OpenStreetMap. [[4]](#source-4)
