## Role today

Aleppo International is a secondary hub for Syrian Air. [[1]](#source-1) The first commercial flight since the fall of the Assad government landed there on 18 March 2025, and the terminal has been refurbished with Qatari help since the war. [[1]](#source-1)

## History

The airport dates back to the French Mandate; KLM's first flight from Amsterdam to Batavia came through it in 1924. [[1]](#source-1) Its current terminal opened in 1999. [[1]](#source-1)

The civil war shut it in January 2013, with flights suspended from December 2012; it reopened briefly on 22 January 2014 and properly only on 19 February 2020, after the government retook eastern Aleppo. [[1]](#source-1) On 1 March 2020 Turkish drones and artillery struck it during Operation Spring Shield. [[1]](#source-1) Israel then hit it repeatedly, saying it was targeting Iranian weapons transfers: strikes in September 2022 and through 2023 damaged the runway and closed the airport several times, and a strike on 1 May 2023 on an ammunition depot at the military airport killed a Syrian soldier. [[1]](#source-1)

On 30 November 2024, as government forces withdrew from Aleppo, the Syrian Democratic Forces took the airport, followed by Hayat Tahrir al-Sham fighters, who captured ten military aircraft there. [[1]](#source-1)

## The airfield

One asphalt runway, 09/27, 3,110 m long. [[1]](#source-1)
