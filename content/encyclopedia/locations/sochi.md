## Role today

Sochi is one of Russia's ten largest airports and was the main gateway for the 2014 Winter Olympics. [[1]](#source-1) It is a civil airport shared with the Ministry of Defence, the FSB and the Emergencies Ministry. [[2]](#source-2) Because mountains rise to the north and north-east, aircraft land only from the sea and take off back towards it. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2)

## History

The airfield was built for the Second World War: from 9 July 1941 the people of Sochi cleared a grass landing ground in 50 days, and it took its first combat aircraft on 1 September. [[2]](#source-2) The Black Sea Fleet air force's transport detachment flew from it, and from 1942 regiments of the 5th Air Army and other Transcaucasian Front units were based there. [[2]](#source-2)

It became Adler civil airport on 23 November 1945, replacing Sochi's old airfield in the river valley; the first runway and terminal followed in 1956, and it was renamed Sochi in 1967. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2)

On 25 December 2016 a Russian military Tu-154 bound for Latakia in Syria, with members of the Alexandrov Ensemble military choir aboard, crashed into the Black Sea two minutes after taking off from Sochi; all 92 people on board were killed. [[1]](#source-1)

## The airfield

Two paved runways, 06/24 and 02/20. [[1]](#source-1) [[3]](#source-3) The authority planned to extend 06/24 to 3.5 km, part of it over the Mzymta River. [[1]](#source-1)
