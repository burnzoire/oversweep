## Role today

Khmeimim is the air base Russia built beside Latakia's civil airport in 2015 to run its intervention in the Syrian civil war, and it shares the airport's airfield. [[1]](#source-1) A treaty signed in [Damascus](record:damascus) on 26 August 2015 gives Russia its use free of charge and with no time limit; the Syrian military guards the perimeter while Russia handles [air defence](term:air-defense). [[1]](#source-1) At the end of 2017 Russia made it part of a permanent military grouping in Syria, with its naval facility at Tartus. [[1]](#source-1)

When the Assad government fell in December 2024, transports arrived and Russia moved its S-400 and [Tor](record:sa-15) air defence systems to Tartus, but it kept the base; Bashar al-Assad stopped there on his way into exile. [[1]](#source-1) On 9 August 2026 Syria's foreign ministry said the Syrian civil administration would take back the airport and the base would become a joint training centre over three months, under an agreement between the two countries. [[1]](#source-1) Talks in 2025 on reopening the civil airport stalled when Russia refused to hand it over, and it was still closed to civil traffic in March 2026. [[2]](#source-2)

## History

Latakia's airport opened its terminal in 1983 and its tower by 1989, and until 2024 it was named after Bassel al-Assad. [[2]](#source-2) In mid-2015 Russia built accommodation for about 1,000 people, a control tower, runway extensions, storage and fuelling points beside it; the US revealed the build-up in early September, and the base began operations on 30 September 2015. [[1]](#source-1) Su-24Ms, [Su-25s](record:su-25) and Su-34s flew [strikes](term:strike) from it, with Il-20M [reconnaissance](term:reconnaissance) aircraft and Mi-24, Mi-28, Ka-52 and [Mi-8](record:mi-8mtv2) helicopters. [[1]](#source-1) After Turkey shot down a Su-24M on 24 November 2015, Russia installed an S-400 system covering the air from southern Turkey to northern Israel, and Su-35s arrived at the end of January 2016. [[1]](#source-1) Some of the *Admiral Kuznetsov*'s MiG-29Ks and [Su-33s](record:su-33) flew from Khmeimim in November 2016 after the carrier lost a MiG-29K to an arresting-wire failure. [[1]](#source-1)

The base was a target for most of the war. Shelling on 31 December 2017 killed two Russian servicemen and, by press accounts, damaged several aircraft. [[1]](#source-1) On 6 January 2018 Russia repelled an attack by a swarm of 13 armed fixed-wing drones, said to be the first of its kind; air defences and [electronic warfare](term:ew) at the base had stopped more than 100 drones by September 2019. [[1]](#source-1) An An-26 crashed landing there on 6 March 2018, killing all 39 on board, and on 19 September 2018 Syrian air defences firing at Israeli [F-16s](record:f-16) shot down a Russian Il-20 coming in to land. [[1]](#source-1)

## The airfield

Two parallel asphalt runways, 17R/35L and 17L/35R, each published as 2,797 m long. [[1]](#source-1)
