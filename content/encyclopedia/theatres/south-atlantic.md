## The South Atlantic and the Falkland Islands

### Historical significance

Argentina invaded the Falkland Islands on 2 April 1982 and South Georgia the next day. On 5 April the British government sent a naval task force to retake them. The ten-week war ended with the Argentine surrender on 14 June. In all, 649 Argentine and 255 British service personnel and three Falkland Islanders were killed. [[1]](#source-1)

The war was fought at the limit of both sides' reach. The British had 42 aircraft, including 28 Sea Harriers and 6 Harrier GR.3s, available for air combat. [[1]](#source-1) In the seven Black Buck missions, RAF Vulcan bombers flew from Ascension Island, refuelled again and again in the air by Victor tankers. Five of them completed attacks: three on Port Stanley's airfield and two on its radar with Shrike missiles. [[2]](#source-2) On 4 May HMS Sheffield became the first Royal Navy ship sunk in action since the Second World War, hit by an Exocet from the Argentine Navy's 2nd Naval Air Fighter/Attack Squadron, whose Étendards were based at Río Grande in Tierra del Fuego. [[1]](#source-1) The United States supplied the British with 200 Sidewinder missiles for the Harriers, Stinger and Harpoon missiles, and fuel for the task force at Ascension. [[1]](#source-1)

### Forces deployed

- **British task force**: the carrier HMS Hermes was made its flagship. [[3]](#source-3) Sea Harriers of No. 801 Naval Air Squadron fought Argentine Mirage IIIs, downing one with an AIM-9L Sidewinder. [[1]](#source-1)
- **Ascension Island**: by mid-April 1982 the RAF had set up a base at Wideawake Airfield, in the mid-Atlantic, with Vulcan bombers, Victor tankers and Phantom fighters to protect them. [[1]](#source-1)
- **Stanley**: the Argentine strongpoint throughout the war. Despite the Black Buck and Harrier raids its airfield was never put entirely out of action; no fast jets were based there, and it was defended by Roland and Tigercat missiles and 35 mm guns. [[1]](#source-1)
- **RAF Mount Pleasant**: opened on 12 May 1985 to replace the RAF's post-war facilities at Stanley, it is the newest purpose-built RAF station, home to between 1,000 and 2,000 British military personnel, and part of British Forces South Atlantic Islands. [[4]](#source-4)
