## Built to catch the Valkyrie

The R-40 exists because of the American XB-70 Valkyrie, a Mach 3 bomber that threatened to make the Soviet air defence force's interceptors and missiles obsolete at a stroke. The answer was the MiG-25 and a new long-range missile for it, which the Bisnovat bureau began in 1962. [[1]](#source-1) The result is one of the largest production air-to-air missiles ever made, at 475 kg and more than 6 m long. [[1]](#source-1)

## Firing doctrine and combat

Soviet procedure was to fire a pair: an infrared R-40T first, then a radar-guided R-40R, so that the heat-seeker would not lock on to the radar missile. [[1]](#source-1) In Soviet hands the R-40 was never fired outside training and tests, but MiG-25s exported to the Middle East used it in combat. [[1]](#source-1) On the first night of the 1991 Gulf War an Iraqi MiG-25 shot down a US Navy [F/A-18 Hornet](record:fa-18c) of VFA-81, flown by Scott Speicher, with an R-40. [[1]](#source-1)
