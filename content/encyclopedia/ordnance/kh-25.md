## One missile, many heads

The Kh-25 is a family of light Soviet air-to-ground missiles built round a modular choice of guidance systems. [[1]](#source-1) It grew out of the [laser-guided](term:lgw) version of the Kh-23 Grom: work on a semi-active laser seeker began in 1971, and the Kh-25 entered production in 1975. [[1]](#source-1) Its predecessor, the beam-riding Kh-66, had been hard to use because the launch aircraft had to dive at the target. [[1]](#source-1)

## The modular Kh-25M

From 1973 the design bureau combined the Kh-23M, Kh-25 and the Kh-27 anti-radar missile into one modular system, to cut costs and add tactical flexibility. Completed by the end of 1978, it produced the anti-radar Kh-25MP, the laser-guided Kh-25ML and the radio-guided Kh-25MR, which could be changed simply by changing the seeker head. [[1]](#source-1) NATO kept calling them AS-12 and AS-10. [[1]](#source-1)

## In service

The original Kh-25 equipped the MiG-23, MiG-27 and Su-17M from the mid-1970s and has since been cleared for many more aircraft. [[1]](#source-1) It remains in wide use, though a successor, the Kh-38, has been developed. [[1]](#source-1)
