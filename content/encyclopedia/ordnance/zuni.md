## Replacing the HVAR

In the early 1950s, engineers at the Naval Ordnance Test Station at China Lake set out to replace the Second World War's 5 in High Velocity Aircraft Rocket with a new rocket of the same calibre. [[1]](#source-1) They designed the Zuni as a modular system, so that one motor could take many different warheads and [fuzes](term:fuze). Because it was first meant to shoot down aircraft, one warhead had a [proximity fuze](term:proximity-fuze), and the Zuni's airframe became the starting point for the AIM-9 Sidewinder missile. [[1]](#source-1) It was approved for production in 1957 and named after the Zuni people of New Mexico. [[1]](#source-1)

The Navy's ordnance manual lists only two rockets in its service: the 2.75 in "Mighty Mouse" and the 5 in Zuni. [[2]](#source-2) The Zuni's Mk 71 motor has spring-loaded fins that open once the rocket leaves the tube and a fluted nozzle that spins it in flight, so the same rocket can be launched from fast jets, slow aircraft and helicopters. [[3]](#source-3) It is carried in the four-tube LAU-10 launcher, which weighs 500 to 550 lb when loaded with four complete rounds. [[1]](#source-1) [[4]](#source-4)

## Vietnam

The Zuni was first fired in combat by Pakistan Air Force [F-86F](record:f-86) Sabres in the Indo-Pakistani War of 1965, and it was widely used for ground attack in Vietnam. [[1]](#source-1) Its best-remembered moment came in the air-to-air role it was first designed for. On 1 May 1967, during a strike on Kép air base in North Vietnam, Lieutenant Commander Theodore R. Swartz of VA-76, flying an A-4 Skyhawk from USS *Bon Homme Richard*, shot down a MiG-17 with Zuni rockets: the only MiG downed by a Skyhawk in the war. He was awarded the Silver Star. [[1]](#source-1)

The rocket also has a darker place in naval history. Later in 1967, the improper handling of a Zuni and other munitions caused the fire aboard USS *Forrestal* that killed 134 men, and in 1969 a similar accident aboard USS *Enterprise* killed 27. [[1]](#source-1)

## Last rounds

A [laser-guided](term:lgw) Zuni, using a WGU-58/B guidance section on a standard rocket, was tested successfully in 2009 and appears on the Marine Corps aviation weapons roadmap, for any aircraft cleared to fire Zunis from the LAU-10. [[1]](#source-1) In January 2023 the United States announced it would send 4,000 Zuni rockets to Ukraine, transferring its last stocks, and by mid-2024 Ukraine was reported to have used them all. [[1]](#source-1)
