## An aircraft built around a gun

The GAU-8 was developed alongside the A-X competition that produced the [A-10](record:a-10), from a specification of 1970. Both A-X prototypes were designed for it, although they first flew with an [M61](record:m61) as a stand-in. [[1]](#source-1) The complete gun system, properly the A/A 49E-6, makes up about 17% of the A-10's empty weight. The gun matters so much to the aircraft's balance that a jack must go under the tail whenever it is taken out, to stop the A-10 tipping backwards. [[1]](#source-1)

The gun alone weighs 281 kg. With its drum, feed and a full ammunition load, the system weighs about 1,828 kg and extends roughly 5.8 m from muzzle to the rear of the ammunition system. The drum holds up to 1,174 rounds. Those are installation figures: the barrel assembly is only part of what the aircraft carries. [[1]](#source-1)

## How seven barrels work

The Avenger is an externally powered rotary cannon. Each barrel has its own breech-bolt assembly and fires once in a complete rotation. Seven barrels share the firing loads; General Dynamics also attributes reduced component impact loads to the mechanism's continuous rotation. The arrangement is intended to sustain rapid fire while extending barrel and parts life. [[2]](#source-2) [[3]](#source-3)

The A-10 installation uses hydraulic drive and a linkless ammunition supply. [[1]](#source-1) The manufacturer's broader gun specification lists hydraulic, electric or pneumatic drive and linked or linkless feed. Those options describe the weapon family, not three drive systems fitted to an A-10. [[2]](#source-2)

## The cartridge matters

The ammunition family is 30 × 173 mm, but its projectiles do different jobs. General Dynamics distinguishes three principal rounds: [[4]](#source-4)

| Round | Role | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PGU-13/B HEI | High-explosive incendiary | Uses fragmentation and incendiary effects against targets including personnel, trucks and ammunition stores. |
| PGU-14/B API | Armour-piercing incendiary | Uses a dense penetrator to penetrate armour and produce effects behind it. |
| PGU-15/B TP | Target practice | Provides ballistically matched ammunition for training, weapon tests and aircraft-system checks. |

The manufacturer describes the practice round as ballistically matched to both combat types. That makes it relevant to training the weapon system, rather than simply providing a cheaper projectile of the same diameter. The listed roles do not establish a universal combat ammunition mix or guarantee penetration of a particular target. [[4]](#source-4)

## Rate of fire and dispersion

The A-10 rate quoted in the existing reference is about 3,900 rounds per minute. [[1]](#source-1) General Dynamics quotes a maximum of 4,200 for the gun. [[2]](#source-2) The two figures should not be treated as interchangeable installation settings.

The manufacturer's dispersion specification is an 80-percent circle, five milliradians in diameter. That is an angular spread, not a promise that every projectile strikes the aiming point. [[3]](#source-3)

**Illustrative calculation:** at a range of 1,000 m, a diameter of 0.005 radians gives approximately **1,000 m × 0.005 = 5 m**. This is a geometric conversion of the published angle, not a predicted combat grouping: it does not model aiming error or aircraft motion. [[3]](#source-3)

## Integrating the weapon

The manufacturer lists average recoil force as 44.5 kN. [[3]](#source-3) This is another installation consideration alongside the ammunition mass and feed system, rather than just a characteristic of the visible barrels.

The GAU-8 also equips the Goalkeeper ship-defence system, where General Dynamics identifies a missile-piercing discarding-sabot round. The same gun designation therefore does not imply identical ammunition or a complete A-10 installation transplanted to a ship. [[3]](#source-3) Some Avenger technology also went into the smaller 25 mm [GAU-12](record:gau-12) used by the [AV-8B Harrier II](record:av-8b). [[1]](#source-1)

For the aircraft's wider role, see the [A-10](record:a-10) article and the definition of [close air support](term:cas). The Manual's [weapons-delivery module](https://www.oversweep.com/manual/air-to-ground-operations/weapons-delivery/) covers the associated delivery concepts.
