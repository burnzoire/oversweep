## Successor to the Kh-28

The Raduga bureau developed the Kh-58 in the early 1970s as a solid-fuel successor to the liquid-fuelled Kh-28 anti-radiation missile, to arm the new Su-24M attack aircraft. [[1]](#source-1) It was designed to work with the Su-24's Fantasmagoria target acquisition system, and like other Soviet missiles of its time it could take seeker heads tuned to particular air defence radars, such as those of the Nike-Hercules or Patriot. [[1]](#source-1) Its range depends heavily on launch altitude: 36 km from low level, 120 km and more from altitude. [[1]](#source-1)

## In combat

Iraqi MiG-25BMs first fired the Kh-58U in November 1987 against Iranian Hawk batteries, disabling at least one radar, and in July 1988 Iraqi Kh-58Us and [Kh-31Ps](record:kh-31) destroyed an Iranian early-warning radar site. [[1]](#source-1) In 2007 a Kh-58 fired at a Georgian radar missed and failed to explode; during the 2008 war Russia did not use anti-radiation missiles, likely because Georgian operators kept their radars off until Russian aircraft were in range. [[1]](#source-1) The Kh-58 is being superseded by the Kh-31. [[1]](#source-1)
