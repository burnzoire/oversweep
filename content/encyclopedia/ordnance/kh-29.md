## A big warhead

The Kh-29 carries a 320 kg warhead and a choice of laser, TV, infrared or radar guidance, giving tactical aircraft such as the Su-24, Su-30, [MiG-29](record:mig-29) and [Su-25](record:su-25) a longer standoff. [[1]](#source-1) It is meant mainly for large battlefield targets and infrastructure, such as factories, depots and bridges, but can also attack ships of up to 10,000 tonnes, hardened aircraft shelters and concrete runways. [[1]](#source-1)

## Borrowed parts

The Molniya bureau, better known for air-to-air missiles, began the design in the late 1970s, and Vympel finished it when Molniya moved to space work. It was first fired in 1976 and entered service in 1980. [[1]](#source-1) Its layout follows the [R-60](record:r-60) air-to-air missile; its [laser](term:lgw) seeker came from the [Kh-25](record:kh-25) and its TV seeker from the [Kh-59](record:kh-59). [[1]](#source-1) It is often compared with the [AGM-65 Maverick](record:agm-65), but it is a much bigger missile, more than twice as heavy, with a warhead of 320 kg against 136 kg. [[1]](#source-1)

## In combat

Russian Su-34s and Su-24s used the Kh-29L in Syria from 2015. [[1]](#source-1)
