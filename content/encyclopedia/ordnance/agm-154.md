## Outside the point defences

The JSOW came from a joint Navy and Air Force programme for a standard, medium-range precision weapon that could attack defended targets from outside the reach of ordinary anti-aircraft defences, keeping the aircraft and crew safer. [[1]](#source-1) It is a glide bomb, not a missile, despite its AGM designation. [[1]](#source-1) The FAS gives a standoff of 15 NM from a low-altitude launch and 40 NM from high altitude [[2]](#source-2); Wikipedia gives 12 and 70 NM. [[1]](#source-1)

## How it is used

Before launch the weapon is given a destination, either a preplanned waypoint or a point marked with a targeting pod. It then glides on two pop-out wings and, in the baseline version, dispenses its submunitions in a short, roughly linear pattern over soft targets such as parked aircraft, trucks, armoured personnel carriers and SAM sites. [[1]](#source-1) It is a fire-and-forget weapon guided by tightly coupled GPS and inertial navigation, usable day, night and in bad weather. [[1]](#source-1) The AGM-154C adds an imaging infrared seeker and a two-way data link for point targets, with aimpoint refinement and man-in-the-loop guidance. [[2]](#source-2)

## Variants

All versions share one air vehicle, the "truck", with different payloads. [[1]](#source-1) The AGM-154A carries 145 BLU-97/B bomblets, each with a shaped charge against armour, a fragmenting case and a zirconium incendiary ring. [[2]](#source-2) The anti-armour AGM-154B, carrying sensor-fuzed submunitions, was developed but never bought once the Air Force left the programme. [[1]](#source-1)
