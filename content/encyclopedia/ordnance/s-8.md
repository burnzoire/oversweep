## Eighty millimetres

The S-8 is an 80 mm rocket developed in the 1970s for fighter-bombers and helicopters, and it entered service in 1984. [[1]](#source-1) It is roughly one and a half metres long and weighs between 11 and 15 kg depending on the warhead, and it is carried in the B series of pods, which hold either seven or 20 rockets. [[1]](#source-1)

## A warhead for every target

The S-8 comes with a wide range of warheads. The S-8KO and S-8KOM carry a 3.6 kg shaped charge able to penetrate about 400 mm of armour; the S-8KOM's improved low-smoke motor burns twice as long and pushes the rocket to about 700 m/s. [[1]](#source-1) The S-8B and S-8BM are penetrating rockets for attacking runways, and the S-8D and S-8DM carry fuel-air explosive. Others carry illumination flares, radar-reflecting chaff or flechettes. [[1]](#source-1) The rockets reach between 2 and 4 km. [[1]](#source-1)

## Still developing

In 2018 the Russian Aerospace Forces tested the S-8OFP Broneboishchik, a successor with longer range, a heavier warhead and a digital fuze that can be set to burst in front of an obstacle or behind it. It is meant for Su-25 attack aircraft and Mi-8 helicopters, and has been seen fired from the Mi-35M; it entered series production in May 2023. [[1]](#source-1)
