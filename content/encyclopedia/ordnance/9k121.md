## Riding the beam

The Vikhr is a Soviet anti-tank missile that rides a laser beam to its target. It can be launched from the [Ka-50](record:ka-50) and Ka-52 helicopters, the [Su-25T](record:su-25) and warships, and was first shown in public at the 1992 Farnborough Airshow. [[1]](#source-1) The Vikhr-M system pairs the missile with an automatic sight that has TV and infrared channels, a laser channel to control the missile, a laser rangefinder and automatic target tracking, so it can find and track targets by day or night. [[1]](#source-1) Because the receiver faces back toward the launcher, the guidance is hard to jam. [[1]](#source-1)

## One missile, three jobs

Its warhead combines a two-stage shaped charge with a fragmentation sleeve and both contact and proximity fuzes, so one missile can attack armour, including tanks with explosive reactive armour, aircraft and area targets. [[1]](#source-1) Wikipedia notes this as an advantage over the 9M120 Ataka-V complex, which needs three different missiles for the same jobs. [[1]](#source-1)
