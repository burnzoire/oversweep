## Flown by hand

Saab developed the RB 05 in the 1960s as a ground-attack missile for the Saab 37 [Viggen](record:ajs-37) and the Saab 105 trainer, and it entered service in 1972. [[1]](#source-1) It was guided by manual command to line of sight: the pilot followed the flares on the missile and steered it to the target with a joystick, the commands going out over a jam-resistant radio link. [[1]](#source-1) That meant aiming separately from flying, which needed either a second crew member, as in the Saab 105, or a computer to fly the aircraft during guidance, as in the Viggen. [[1]](#source-1)

## Supersonic and smokeless

The missile was usually launched after a high-speed run at very low altitude and a climb to 400 m. It flew at supersonic speed to cut the threat from surface-to-air missiles, and because it was roll-stabilised the aircraft did not need to point straight at the target and could descend again at once. [[1]](#source-1) Its rocket engine was smokeless so as not to hide the target from the pilot. [[1]](#source-1)
