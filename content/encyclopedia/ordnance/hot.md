## High subsonic, optical, tube

HOT stands for the French "Haut subsonique Optiquement Téléguidé Tiré d'un Tube": high subsonic, optically remote-guided, tube-launched. [[1]](#source-1) Nord Aviation and Bölkow developed it to replace the older SS.11 wire-guided missile. Compared with the SS.11 it flies farther and faster, and it is guided semi-automatically instead of by hand. [[1]](#source-1) Mass production began in 1976 and the first missiles were fielded in 1978. [[1]](#source-1)

## Anti-tank helicopters

Soon after fitting HOT to ground vehicles, France and West Germany both introduced dedicated anti-tank helicopters to fire it: the French chose the [Gazelle SA 342M](record:sa342), carrying four missiles in two dual launchers, and the Germans the Bo 105 PAH-1, carrying six. [[1]](#source-1) The Viviane night sight for firing from helicopters followed in the early 1980s. [[1]](#source-1) HOT is reaching the end of its life in Europe, where France has bought Hellfire II for its Tiger helicopters, but it remains in wide use elsewhere. [[1]](#source-1)
