## Two barrels that work each other

The GSh-23 uses the Gast principle, devised by the German engineer Karl Gast in 1916: in a twin-barrel gun, the firing of one barrel operates the mechanism of the other. [[1]](#source-1) That gives a much faster rate of fire than a single barrel, with less wear. It cannot match the sustained rate of an electrically driven rotary cannon such as the [M61](record:m61), but it needs no outside power, because the recoil of its floating barrels drives it. [[1]](#source-1) The principle has been little used in the West, but widely in the Soviet Union. [[1]](#source-1)

## Where it flies

The GSh-23 entered service in 1965. The GSh-23L, with a muzzle brake to cut recoil, became standard on late-model [MiG-21s](record:mig-21) and on all MiG-23s, and it arms the [JF-17](record:jf-17) and the [L-39ZA](record:l-39). Earlier MiG-21s could carry it in the GP-9 gondola under the fuselage, with 200 rounds. [[1]](#source-1) It is also the defensive gun in the tail turrets of the Tu-22M and some Tu-95MS bombers.
