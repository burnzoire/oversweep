## Looking off the nose

The R-73 replaced the [R-60](record:r-60). Work began in 1973, the missile was operational in 1982 and it formally entered service in 1984. [[1]](#source-1) Its cooled infrared seeker can detect targets up to 40° off the missile's centreline, and the pilot can point it with a helmet-mounted sight, designating a target just by looking at it. [[1]](#source-1) That combination let a Soviet pilot shoot at an aircraft well off his nose without first pointing his fighter at it. Minimum engagement range is about 300 m and maximum aerodynamic range nearly 31 km at altitude. [[1]](#source-1)

## In service

The R-73 arms the [MiG-29](record:mig-29) and the [Su-27](record:su-27) family. [[1]](#source-1) In February 1996 a Cuban MiG-29UB shot down two Brothers to the Rescue Cessna 337s over international waters with R-73s. [[1]](#source-1) In the Eritrean–Ethiopian War of 1998–2000 both Ethiopian Su-27s and Eritrean MiG-29s fired it, and in 2008 a Russian MiG-29 destroyed a Georgian Hermes 450 drone over Abkhazia with one. [[1]](#source-1) Later versions, the R-74 family, look up to 75° off the nose. [[1]](#source-1)
