## The heavy end of the scale

The Soviet Union used aircraft rockets from as early as the 1930s, and the S-24 sits at the heavy end of that tradition. It is named for its diameter, 24 cm, and it is one of a handful of successors to the BETAB-750DS rockets of the Second World War. [[1]](#source-1) At 2.33 m long and 235 kg at launch, it is carried singly on weapon pylons rather than in pods. [[1]](#source-1)

## One big warhead

Where the smaller rockets rely on numbers, the S-24 relies on a single 123 kg blast-fragmentation warhead. [[1]](#source-1) With the RV-24 proximity fuze it bursts about 3 m above the ground, and its scored body breaks into some 4,000 fragments able to penetrate up to 30 mm of armour (some sources say 25 mm), over a casualty radius of 300 to 400 m. [[1]](#source-1) Its range is short, about 2 to 3 km. [[1]](#source-1)

The S-24B version uses a low-smoke propellant, so that its flight leaves less of a trail. [[1]](#source-1) The rocket remains in service with the Russian Aerospace Forces, the Ukrainian Air Force and many export customers, and Iran builds it under licence as the Shafaq. [[1]](#source-1)
