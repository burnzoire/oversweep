## From Mauser to Enfield

During the Second World War Mauser developed a revolver cannon with a motorised firing mechanism, in 20 mm as the MG 213 and in 30 mm as the MK 213. Only five prototypes were finished by the end of the war. [[1]](#source-1) British engineers at the Armament Development Establishment, Enfield, took the idea forward to meet an Air Ministry demand for more lethal aircraft guns; the name ADEN stands for Armament Development, Enfield. [[1]](#source-1) The French [DEFA](record:defa-550) and the American [M39](record:m39) come from the same German design.

## On every British jet

The ADEN entered service on the Hawker Hunter in 1954 and was used on every British gun-armed aircraft until the Tornado in the 1980s. [[1]](#source-1) The Hunter carried four in a removable pack. [[1]](#source-1) A 25 mm version, the ADEN 25, was chosen for the Harrier GR.5, but problems with weight and with integrating the gun pod led to its cancellation in 1999; Fleet Air Arm Sea Harriers kept the 30 mm gun.
