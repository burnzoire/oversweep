## Four for one

The Small Diameter Bomb is a small, accurate glide bomb designed so that one aircraft can carry more weapons and strike more targets: most US Air Force aircraft can carry a pack of four on a BRU-61/A rack in place of a single 2,000 lb [Mk 84](record:mk-84). [[1]](#source-1) It entered service in 2006, reaching initial operational capability on the [F-15E](record:f-15e) in October that year and seeing combat the same month. [[1]](#source-1)

## Small but sharp

The original SDB uses GPS-aided inertial navigation against fixed targets such as fuel depots and bunkers. [[1]](#source-1) Its "DiamondBack" wings deploy after release and stretch its glide. Its small size and accuracy make for an effective weapon with less collateral damage, and its warhead can penetrate more than 3 ft of steel-reinforced concrete. [[1]](#source-1) Accuracy can be sharpened before release by differential GPS corrections sent to the aircraft over Link 16 from fixed receivers in theatre. [[1]](#source-1)
