## The small laser bomb

The GBU-12 is a 500 lb [Mk 82](record:mk-82) with a nose-mounted laser seeker and guidance fins added. [[1]](#source-1) Its roots are in the Vietnam War, when the Air Force wanted a lighter, more manoeuvrable laser-guided bomb able to hit moving targets on the Ho Chi Minh trail. [[1]](#source-1) It entered service in 1976, and "Paveway II" names the guidance kit, not the bomb. [[1]](#source-1)

## Bang-bang guidance

The Paveway II steers with "bang-bang" guidance: when the seeker sees that a correction is needed, the fins deflect fully until the bomb overcorrects, then deflect back the other way, so the bomb flies a sinusoidal path to the spot. It is less efficient, but cheaper and simpler. [[1]](#source-1) The FAS gives the GBU-12 a range of 8 NM and a circular error probable of 9 m. [[2]](#source-2)

## Tank buster

The GBU-12 was the F-111F's tank-busting weapon of choice in Desert Storm: of the 4,493 dropped, over half came from F-111Fs, the rest from [F-15Es](record:f-15e) and A-6s, mostly against fixed armour, and the Air Force says it hit 88 per cent of its targets. [[2]](#source-2) Wikipedia credits the F-111s with 920 Iraqi tanks and armoured personnel carriers destroyed with it. [[1]](#source-1) It is among the most commonly used guided munitions and can be dropped by a very wide range of aircraft. [[1]](#source-1)
