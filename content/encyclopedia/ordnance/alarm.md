## Britain's anti-radiation missile

ALARM, the Air Launched Anti-Radiation Missile, was designed mainly to destroy enemy radars for the suppression of enemy air defences, [SEAD](term:sead). [[1]](#source-1) The Ministry of Defence chose it over the [AGM-88 HARM](record:agm-88) in 1983, a contested decision to keep the capability in British industry, and it entered service in 1990. [[1]](#source-1)

## Waiting for the radar

It is a fire-and-forget missile with an added loiter capability. In loiter mode it climbs to about 13,000 m after launch; if the target radar shuts down, it deploys a parachute and descends slowly until the radar transmits again, then fires a second motor to attack it. [[1]](#source-1) That answers the oldest defence against an [anti-radiation missile](term:arm): switching the radar off.

## In service

The RAF fired 121 in the 1991 Gulf War, 6 over Kosovo in 1999 and 47 in the 2003 invasion of Iraq, and used it over Libya in 2011; Saudi Arabia used it in Yemen in 2015. The UK retired it at the end of 2013. [[1]](#source-1)
