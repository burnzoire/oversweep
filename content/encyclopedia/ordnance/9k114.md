## For the Mi-24

The 9M114 Kokon missile of the Shturm system was developed by the Kolomna bureau, starting in 1967 with the aim of arming the [Mi-24](record:mi-24p). Testing was completed in 1974 and it entered service in 1976. [[1]](#source-1) In role it resembles the [TOW](record:bgm-71) and [HOT](record:hot), which entered service about the same time, but it is heavier, faster and longer-ranged. [[1]](#source-1)

## Radio, not wire

The Shturm is a SACLOS missile with a radio command link, which lets it fly much faster and farther than a wire-guided missile. The link uses five frequency bands and two codes to resist jamming. [[1]](#source-1) The gunner uses an 8× daylight sight with a built-in laser rangefinder and keeps the cross hairs on the target until impact. The missile flies above the line of sight to clear obstacles and drops onto the target just before it hits; it can even engage low, slow helicopters, though only a direct hit will do, since it has only a contact fuze. [[1]](#source-1)

## Afghanistan

The Shturm was first used late in the Soviet–Afghan War, when better anti-aircraft weapons in the Mujahideen's hands forced Mi-24 crews to use it from standoff range; sources report hit rates of 75–85 per cent. [[1]](#source-1)
