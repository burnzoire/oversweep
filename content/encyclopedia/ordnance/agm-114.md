## Heliborne, laser, fire and forget

The Hellfire began in 1974 with a US Army requirement for a tank-buster that helicopters could launch against armoured vehicles. [[1]](#source-1) Its working name, "Heliborne laser, fire-and-forget missile", gave it the nickname Hellfire, which became its formal name. [[1]](#source-1) It is now the main 100 lb class air-to-ground [precision weapon](term:pgm) of the US forces and many others, and has been adapted for drone strikes against high-value targets. [[1]](#source-1)

## Laser or radar

Most versions are [laser-guided](term:lgw). The designator can be the launcher itself, such as the nose sights of the [AH-64 Apache](record:ah-64d), another aircraft, or an observer on the ground; the last two let the launching helicopter break line of sight with the target and stay in cover. [[1]](#source-1) The AGM-114L Longbow Hellfire is different: its millimetre-wave radar seeker makes it fire-and-forget, able to lock on even after launch, and it works in weather, smoke and fog that would hide a target or stop a laser spot forming. [[1]](#source-1)

## Platforms

Helicopter gunships such as the Apache and the AH-1 Cobra are the most common Hellfire platforms, carrying up to 16 each, though the Predator and Reaper drones carry the Hellfire II too. [[1]](#source-1) The Hellfire II, a modular family of warheads for different targets, entered Army service in 1996. [[1]](#source-1)
