## A Sidewinder in Soviet service

The K-13 is a copy of the [AIM-9 Sidewinder](record:aim-9), and it looks like one. [[1]](#source-1) The Sidewinder was quickly reverse-engineered, the copy entered limited service in 1960, and the improved R-3S followed in 1962. NATO saw it in 1961 and named it AA-2 "Atoll". All versions share the Sidewinder's 5 in body, and when NATO later examined captured missiles it found that parts from an AIM-9 could be swapped into an AA-2 and either combination would still work. [[1]](#source-1) Its seeker has the early Sidewinder's limits: the R-3S needed about 22 seconds for its seeker to settle, and its minimum range was about a kilometre. [[1]](#source-1)

## Wide use

The K-13 armed the [MiG-21](record:mig-21) in many air forces. North Vietnamese MiG-21 pilots, few in number, would race in at high speed, fire their missiles in volleys at an American formation and leave at full speed before they could be engaged. [[1]](#source-1) Israel captured some in the Six-Day War, and India used them on MiG-21s in 1971. [[1]](#source-1)
