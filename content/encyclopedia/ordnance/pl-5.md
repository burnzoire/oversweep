## A long road into service

The PL-5 follows the PL-2, which was based on the Soviet [K-13](record:r-3) and so, at one remove, on the [Sidewinder](record:aim-9). [[1]](#source-1) Work began in 1966 but production started only in 1982. The designers first considered radar guidance, like the US Navy's AIM-9C, before settling on a heat-seeker, the PL-5B. The PL-5C followed in the mid-1980s and the PL-5E-II in the early 1990s. [[1]](#source-1)

China's newest fighters now use the PL-8 and PL-10, but the PL-5 remains useful on lighter aircraft that cannot carry them. [[1]](#source-1) The [JF-17](record:jf-17) carries the PL-5E alongside the radar-guided [PL-12](record:pl-12). [[2]](#source-2)
