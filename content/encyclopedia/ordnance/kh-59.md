## A longer reach

Raduga developed the Kh-59 in the 1970s as a longer-ranged relative of the [Kh-25](record:kh-25), a precision standoff weapon for the Su-24M and late MiG-27s. [[1]](#source-1) After release a solid-fuel booster fires, the fins and wings unfold, and a sustainer motor drives it on, while a radar altimeter holds it about 7 m over water or 100–1,000 m over land. [[1]](#source-1) The Kh-59M Ovod-M adds a bigger warhead and a turbofan engine. [[1]](#source-1)

## Man in the loop

The target's coordinates are loaded before launch and the missile flies the first part of its flight on inertial guidance. About 10 km from the target its television guidance switches on; an operator in the aircraft identifies the target and locks the missile onto it. [[1]](#source-1) Its electro-optical sensors came from the same Krasnogorsk design bureau as those of the [Kh-29](record:kh-29) and the [KAB-500Kr](record:kab-500) bomb. [[1]](#source-1)
