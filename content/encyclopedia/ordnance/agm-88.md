## Homing on radar

The HARM is a [supersonic](term:supersonic) missile that [homes](term:homing) on the transmissions of surface-to-air radars, built to replace the [AGM-45 Shrike](record:agm-45) and the AGM-78 Standard ARM. [[1]](#source-1) It can detect, attack and destroy a radar antenna or transmitter with little input from the crew, and can pick a single target out of many emitters. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2) A fixed antenna and seeker in the nose feed a proportional guidance system, and a smokeless dual-thrust rocket motor drives it at more than [Mach](term:mach-number) 2. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2) It is a weapon for the suppression of enemy air defences, [SEAD](term:sead).

## Three modes

The missile has three operating modes: Pre-Briefed, Target of Opportunity and Self-Protect. [[1]](#source-1) The aircraft passes target parameters to the missile before launch. [[2]](#source-2) In the Air Force, the [F-4G](record:f-4) Wild Weasel paired it with its APR-47 radar attack and warning system [[2]](#source-2); later, [F-16s](record:f-16) carried the HARM Targeting System pod, which detects and targets radars automatically rather than relying on the missile's own sensors. [[1]](#source-1)

## Magnum

The HARM was first used in March 1986 against a Libyan [S-200](record:sa-5) site in the Gulf of Sidra, and widely by all three US air arms in Desert Storm. [[1]](#source-1) "[Magnum](term:magnum)" is spoken over the radio when one is launched; in the Gulf War a bogus Magnum call was often enough to make radar operators switch off. [[1]](#source-1) Its dangers show in the same war: an F-4G pilot mistook a B-52's tail-gun radar for an Iraqi gun site and fired; the B-52 survived with shrapnel damage. [[1]](#source-1)
