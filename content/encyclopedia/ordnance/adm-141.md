## A target that is not there

The Tactical Air-Launched Decoy is an unpowered glider, released from a strike aircraft to confuse and saturate enemy air defences as part of a [SEAD](term:sead) plan, so that the real attackers and weapons are more likely to get through. [[1]](#source-1) Its flight controls can hold set speeds and fly set manoeuvres, so it can imitate a particular aircraft, and a Luneburg lens reflects radar energy straight back to make it look much larger, like a bomber. [[1]](#source-1)

## From Samson to TALD

It grew out of the Samson, a winged unpowered decoy developed in the United States and built in Israel, which did well in Israeli service in the 1982 Lebanon War. The US Navy bought about 2,000 Samsons in the mid to late 1980s, and the improved TALD entered US service in 1987. [[1]](#source-1)

## The opening night of Desert Storm

More than 100 TALDs were launched on the opening night of the 1991 Gulf War. They made Iraqi air defences switch on many of their radars, most of which were then destroyed by [anti-radiation missiles](term:arm). [[1]](#source-1) Strikes that night had decoys and drones in their escort both to "take the hit" for the manned aircraft and to make the Iraqis reveal their positions when they tracked or fired on the lures. [[2]](#source-2)
