## The heavyweight

The Mk 84 is the biggest bomb of the Mark 80 series: nominally 2,000 lb, actually between 1,972 and 2,083 lb depending on how it is fitted, with 945 lb of explosive inside a streamlined steel case. [[1]](#source-1) When it entered service during the Vietnam War it was the third-heaviest bomb in the American inventory. [[1]](#source-1) A Mk 84 can dig a crater about 50 ft across and 36 ft deep and can throw lethal fragments out to about 400 ft. [[1]](#source-1)

The FAS explains why the whole series looks the way it does. The Mk 80 bombs were developed in the 1950s in response to the need for bombs with less aerodynamic drag; they are cylindrical, about 45 per cent explosive by weight, carry conical fins or retarders for high-speed external carriage, and are fitted for both nose and tail fuzes to make sure they go off. [[2]](#source-2)

## Desert Storm

The Gulf War shows how widely the Mk 84 was carried. More than 12,000 were expended in Desert Storm, most of them dropped by Air Force F-15Es, F-16s and F-111Fs, while Marine Corps tactical aircraft dropped fewer than 1,000. [[2]](#source-2) Across the whole series the FAS notes that Mk 80 bombs were dropped from "literally every fixed-wing aircraft" that supported the ground offensive, against artillery, trucks, bunkers, Scud launchers, missile and gun sites, radars and supply points. [[2]](#source-2)

## A ballute for low level

A 2,000 lb bomb released at low level is dangerous to the aircraft that drops it, so the Mk 84 has its own retarder. The Mk 84 AIR is fitted with the BSU-50/B tail, a canister holding a "ballute", a combination of balloon and parachute made of strong nylon. On release a lanyard opens the canister, the airflow pulls the bag out and ram air inflates it through four inlets, slowing the bomb so that the aircraft can escape the blast. The pilot can also keep the canister shut and drop the bomb in low-drag form. [[2]](#source-2)

## A warhead today

Most Mk 84s dropped now carry a guidance kit. The Air Force's 2021 ammunition budget describes the BLU-117/Mk-84 as a 2,000 lb class bomb producing blast and fragmentation, used either unguided in high- or low-drag form or as the warhead of the GBU-10 Paveway and the GBU-31 JDAM. [[3]](#source-3) Practice versions match its flight exactly: the 2,000 lb BDU-56/B and the inert Mk 84 both simulate the drop trajectory of a live bomb for aircrew training. [[4]](#source-4) The Navy also converts the Mk 84 into the Mk 41 Destructor mine, which is dropped with a conical fin only. [[5]](#source-5)
