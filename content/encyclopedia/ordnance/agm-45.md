## A Sparrow with a radar seeker

The Shrike was developed at the Naval Weapons Center, China Lake, in 1963 by mating a radar-homing seeker head to the rocket body of an [AIM-7 Sparrow](record:aim-7). [[1]](#source-1) It homes on hostile anti-aircraft radar. The Navy first fired it over Vietnam in 1965 from A-4 Skyhawks, and the Air Force adopted it the next year on F-105F and G Wild Weasels, later on the [F-4](record:f-4). [[1]](#source-1)

## Against the Fan Song

The Shrike's nominal range was shorter than that of the [SA-2](record:sa-2) missiles it was used against, though it was a great improvement on the earlier method of attacking SAM sites with rockets and bombs from [F-100F](record:f-100) Super Sabres. [[1]](#source-1) A Shrike was typically lofted about 30° above the horizon at a Fan Song radar some 15 miles away, for a flight of 50 seconds. [[1]](#source-1) Even after the faster, longer-ranged AGM-78 Standard ARM arrived, the Weasels kept carrying Shrikes because a Standard cost about $200,000 and a Shrike $7,000; a typical F-105G load was two of each. [[1]](#source-1)

## Its limits

Radar operators learned to switch off. In the Falklands War, RAF Vulcans fired Shrikes at an Argentine TPS-43 surveillance radar, whose crew simply turned it off during the attacks; it survived the war, though the Shrikes hit two secondary fire-control radars. [[1]](#source-1) The US phased the Shrike out in 1992; the [AGM-88 HARM](record:agm-88) replaced it. [[1]](#source-1)
