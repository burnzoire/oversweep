## One missile, many seekers

The R-27 comes in versions with different seekers on a common design. The radar-guided R-27R needs the fighter's radar on the target, like the [Sparrow](record:aim-7); the R-27T uses an infrared seeker; and the extended-range "E" versions are heavier, at 348 kg for the R-27ET, and reach further. [[1]](#source-1) Anti-radiation and active-radar versions followed. [[1]](#source-1) The type entered service in 1983 and arms the [Su-27](record:su-27) and [MiG-29](record:mig-29) families. [[1]](#source-1)

## What range means

Wikipedia's figures for the R-27R show why a single range number misleads. Its maximum range is 73 km, but against a target at the same altitude its effective kill range is 2 to 42.5 km head-on and only 0.7 to 7.5 km against a target flying away. [[1]](#source-1) A target running from the missile makes it fly much further to catch up.

## Combat

In the 1999 Eritrean–Ethiopian War, Ethiopian Su-27s fought Eritrean MiG-29s; only one R-27 fired by a Su-27 [fuzed](term:fuze) close enough to a MiG to bring it down, and that aircraft crashed on landing. [[1]](#source-1)
