## Filling a gap

In the 1970s Soviet aircraft had 80 mm and 240 mm rockets but nothing in between. The S-13 was developed to fill that gap: a 122 mm rocket able to crater runways and penetrate hardened aircraft shelters, bunkers and pillboxes, in a role similar to the American 127 mm [Zuni](record:zuni). [[1]](#source-1) It is conventional in layout, a solid rocket motor with folding tail fins that steady it after launch. Its first trials were in 1973, but it entered service only in 1983. [[1]](#source-1)

## Built to dig

The basic S-13 is 2.54 m long and weighs 57 kg. Its 21 kg warhead holds only 1.82 kg of explosive and can penetrate 3 m of earth or 1 m of reinforced concrete; against a runway it leaves a damaged area of about 20 square metres. [[1]](#source-1) The later S-13T uses two warheads in tandem to go deeper, while the S-13OF of 1993 trades penetration for 450 heavy fragments that can pierce lightly armoured vehicles, and the S-13D carries fuel-air explosive. [[1]](#source-1)

## Five at a time

S-13s are fired from five-tube launchers: the B-13L, carried by most Soviet and Russian attack aircraft and newer fighters from the Su-17 family to the [MiG-29](record:mig-29), and the B-13L1 for helicopters such as the [Mi-24](record:mi-24p), Mi-28 and Ka-52. [[1]](#source-1)
