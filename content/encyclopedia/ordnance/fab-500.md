## Ninety years of the half-tonne bomb

The FAB-500 is the Soviet and Russian 500 kg high-explosive bomb, and its line is older than the Second World War. Work on the first model, the FAB-500sv, began in 1929 alongside a smaller 250 kg bomb; it passed its trials in 1931 and went straight into series production. [[1]](#source-1) Through the 1930s it was refined year by year, with stronger lifting lugs and joints and a little weight saved, and before the war it was exported to the Spanish Republic, where it was known as the R-500. [[1]](#source-1)

Wartime development brought the long welded M-43 and the short, stubby M-44, followed after the war by the M-46. [[1]](#source-1)

## Bombers and fighter-bombers

Like the FAB-250, the FAB-500 split into two lines in the jet age. The M-54 of 1954 was shaped to fit inside heavy bombers. The M-62 of 1962 was a low-drag bomb for the external pylons of fighter-bombers. [[1]](#source-1) The M-62 is 400 mm across and nearly two and a half metres long, and carries 200 kg of explosive in its 500 kg. [[2]](#source-2) Its export description gives a release envelope from 570 m to 12,000 m and from 500 to 1,900 km/h; the top of that speed range is well past the speed of sound. [[1]](#source-1)

## Still in use

The FAB-500 was used widely by Soviet and Afghan forces in Afghanistan in the 1980s and by Russian and Syrian aircraft in Syria's civil war. [[1]](#source-1) The M-62 has been used in Russia's invasion of Ukraine, and in March 2023 wreckage showed that Su-35s had dropped M-62s fitted with UMPK kits, pop-out wings that turn a free-fall bomb into a glide bomb released from further away. [[1]](#source-1)
