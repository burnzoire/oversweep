## Blast or fragments

Soviet bomb designers drew a clear line between two kinds of explosive bomb. A FAB is built for blast: about half its weight is explosive. An OFAB, a high-explosive fragmentation bomb, trades some of that explosive, down to about 30 to 35 per cent, for a case designed to burst into fragments in a controlled way, with a sawtooth inner surface or a grid of grooves cut into the body. [[1]](#source-1) Its intended targets are military facilities, troops and combat vehicles. [[1]](#source-1)

## A short, heavy bomb

The name follows the Soviet code: OFAB for the type, 250 for the calibre in kilograms, and 270 for the class of its real weight, which is 266 kg. [[1]](#source-1) It is a short, thick bomb, 325 mm across but less than a metre and a half long, and it carries 97 kg of explosive. [[1]](#source-1) Its smaller relative, the OFAB-100-120, weighs 133 kg and carries 42 kg of explosive. [[1]](#source-1)

The OFAB family also shows how one calibre was adapted for different tactics. Alongside the OFAB-250-270 there were a heat-resistant OFAB-250T and two longer "assault" models, the OFAB-250ShL and OFAB-250ShN, the second of them marked for low-altitude delivery. [[1]](#source-1)
