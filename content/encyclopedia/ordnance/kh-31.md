## Fast enough to survive

As surface-to-air missiles spread, [suppression of enemy air defences](term:sead) became a priority, and anti-radiation missiles needed enough range to keep the launch aircraft outside the SAMs, enough speed to avoid being shot down, and a seeker that could find many kinds of radar. [[1]](#source-1) The Zvezda bureau, which had built the anti-radar [Kh-25MP](record:kh-25), started work on a long-range ARM; the Kh-31 was first launched in 1982 and entered service in 1988. [[1]](#source-1) It reaches Mach 3.5. [[1]](#source-1)

## Two jobs

The Kh-31 is best known as an anti-radiation missile, the Kh-31P, but the Kh-31A is an anti-ship missile, and it was the first supersonic anti-ship missile that tactical aircraft could launch. [[1]](#source-1) It has been fired in anger since the Iran–Iraq War, when Iraqi forces used Kh-31Ps with [Kh-58Us](record:kh-58) against Iranian early-warning radars in July 1988. [[2]](#source-2)
