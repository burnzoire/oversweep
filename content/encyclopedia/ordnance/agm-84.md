## Sea-skimmer

The Harpoon is an all-weather, over-the-horizon anti-ship missile that flies just above the water to evade defences and finds its ship with active radar. [[1]](#source-1) The Navy accelerated it after a Soviet-built Styx sank the Israeli destroyer Eilat in 1967, and the first missiles were delivered in 1977. [[1]](#source-1) The FAS describes the attack: once targeting information is sent to the missile and it is fired, it flies to the target location, turns on its seeker, finds the target and strikes it with no further action from the launcher, which is free to deal with other threats. [[2]](#source-2) Air-launched Harpoons need no booster, and the engine usually starts only after the missile drops from the wing. [[2]](#source-2)

## SLAM

The AGM-84E Standoff Land Attack Missile took the Harpoon's airframe and added a GPS receiver, the imaging infrared seeker of the [AGM-65F Maverick](record:agm-65) and a Walleye data link, for precision attacks on high-value land targets and ships in port. [[3]](#source-3) An aircraft carrying a data-link pod could steer it to the aimpoint. [[3]](#source-3) Its combat debut came on 18 January 1991: an A-6E fired two SLAMs, two minutes apart, at a hydroelectric plant in Iraq while an A-7E guided them by data link, the second missile flying through the hole the first had made. [[3]](#source-3)
