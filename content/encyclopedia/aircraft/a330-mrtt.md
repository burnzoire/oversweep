## An airliner that carries fuel in its wings

The A330 MRTT is a military A330-200. Its big airliner wing already holds a great deal of fuel, 111,000 kg without extra tanks, which leaves the main deck free for passengers, pallets and military equipment, and needs little change to hang refuelling pods on its hardpoints. [[1]](#source-1) Each customer picks its refuelling kit: an Airbus boom for receptacle aircraft, Cobham 905E pods under the wings and a Cobham 805E fuselage unit for probe aircraft, and a receptacle so the tanker can itself take fuel. [[1]](#source-1)

## One airframe, many names

Australia was the launch customer. Its KC-30As carry both a boom and two wing pods, and the first reached RAAF Base Amberley in May 2011, two years late, partly because of boom development problems; in 2013 the drogue system was found to be passing too much fuel. [[1]](#source-1) From 2014 KC-30As refuelled coalition aircraft over Iraq. [[1]](#source-1) Britain leases 14 as the Voyager through the AirTanker consortium; they have pods but no boom, so they cannot refuel the RAF's own boom-only types, such as the C-17, the RC-135 and the P-8. [[1]](#source-1) France calls it the Phénix, and it replaced the French C-135FR and the Singapore and Dutch tankers. [[1]](#source-1)

## The tanker the US nearly bought

A version of the aircraft, the KC-45, won the US Air Force tanker competition in 2008, before a successful protest led to the contest the [KC-46](record:kc-46) won. [[1]](#source-1) Orders now come from Australia, France, Britain, the United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia, Singapore, South Korea, Spain, Canada and NATO's multinational fleet. [[1]](#source-1)
