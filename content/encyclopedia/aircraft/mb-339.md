## Improving on a classic

In 1972 the Italian Air Force asked Aermacchi to study a replacement for its MB-326, effectively the standard advanced jet trainer of the 1960s. The company drew up seven all-new designs and compared them with an improved MB-326. The improved version, the MB-339, met every requirement and was cheaper than any clean-sheet design, so in 1975 it was chosen to replace both the MB-326 and the Fiat G.91T. [[1]](#source-1)

The biggest change was a redesigned front fuselage that raised the instructor's seat behind the student, giving a clear view over the student's head. [[1]](#source-1) Much of the rest came from the MB-326: a low, straight wing with tip tanks, air intakes in the wing roots, and a Rolls-Royce Viper turbojet; later models such as the MB-339C have the more powerful Viper 680. [[1]](#source-1) Under the wings it can carry gun pods, rockets and bombs for weapons training or light attack. [[1]](#source-1) The prototype flew on 12 August 1976, and MB-339As reached the Italian Air Force from 1979. [[1]](#source-1)

## The Frecce Tricolori

For decades the MB-339 has been the Italian Air Force's main trainer, and it is the aircraft of the *Frecce Tricolori*, the national aerobatic team, which flies a special version, the MB-339PAN, with smoke generators and no tip tanks. In 1988 three of the team's aircraft were lost in a display accident in Germany. [[1]](#source-1) The M-345 is planned to replace the MB-339 in Italian training and with the team. [[1]](#source-1)

## A trainer at war

Twice the trainer has gone to war as an attack aircraft. [[1]](#source-1) In the 1982 Falklands War, Argentine Naval Aviation based six MB-339As at Port Stanley, the only attack jets to fly from the islands. On 21 May, Lieutenant Owen Crippa, on a [reconnaissance](term:reconnaissance) flight, came upon the British amphibious force and attacked it alone, lightly damaging a Royal Navy frigate. Six days later another MB-339 was shot down by a Blowpipe missile over Goose Green, and three were captured by the British at the end of the war. [[1]](#source-1)

In June 1998, at the start of its war with Ethiopia, Eritrea sent its new MB-339s against targets inside Ethiopia, including the city of Mekelle; one was shot down the next day. [[1]](#source-1)

Roughly half of all MB-339s were built for Italy, and the rest went to export customers, including New Zealand and the United Arab Emirates, whose Al Fursan aerobatic team flies them. [[1]](#source-1)
