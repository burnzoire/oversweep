## Overview

The F-4 Phantom II is a large two-seat, twin-engine, all-weather supersonic fighter-bomber. It was developed for the US Navy, entered Navy service in 1961, and was soon adopted by the US Marine Corps and US Air Force. With 5,195 built, it is the most-produced American supersonic military aircraft. [[1]](#source-1)

## Design

The Phantom was designed as a fleet-defence interceptor: climb fast, fly high and fast, and shoot down attackers with radar-guided missiles at long range. Like other interceptors of its time it had no internal gun. [[1]](#source-1) In Vietnam it was used as a fighter instead, where the lack of a gun, poor rearward view and energy lost in hard turns all counted against it. [[2]](#source-2)

The **F-4E**, first delivered to the US Air Force in October 1967, answered some of this. It added a fuselage fuel tank, leading-edge slats for better manoeuvrability, an improved engine and an internal 20 mm multibarrel gun with a better fire-control system. [[2]](#source-2)

## Sensors and weapons

The F-4E carries four AIM-7 Sparrows semi-recessed under the fuselage and AIM-9 Sidewinders on the wing pylons. [[1]](#source-1) From 1973, F-4Es were fitted with a zoom-lens TV camera for long-range visual identification of air and ground targets, and with Pave Tack, which let the crew find, track and laser-designate ground targets by day or night. A digital intercept computer calculated launch solutions for the Sidewinder and Sparrow. [[2]](#source-2)

## In service

The F-4 was the principal air superiority fighter of the US Air Force, Navy and Marine Corps during the Vietnam War, and later took on ground attack and reconnaissance. [[1]](#source-1) US Air Force Phantoms flew air superiority, interdiction, close air support, reconnaissance and Wild Weasel missions. The **F-4G** Wild Weasel, an F-4E with electronic warfare equipment in place of the gun, hunted surface-to-air missile sites in Desert Storm, often paired with an F-16. The last US Air Force combat Phantoms, F-4Gs of the Idaho Air National Guard, retired after a deployment that ended in December 1995. [[2]](#source-2) Phantoms remain in limited service abroad.
