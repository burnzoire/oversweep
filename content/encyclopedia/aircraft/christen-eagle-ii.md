## A kit-built rival to the Pitts

The Eagle is not a warplane. It is a small aerobatic biplane designed by Frank Christensen to compete with the famous Pitts Special, and the Eagle II is marketed as a kit for owners to build themselves. [[1]](#source-1)

Its construction is traditional. The fuselage and tail are a welded frame of chrome-molybdenum steel tube, skinned in aluminium at the front and fabric at the back; the wings are Sitka spruce covered in fabric, braced with streamlined wires and joined by a single I-shaped strut on each side. Pilot and passenger sit one behind the other under a large bubble canopy, and the wheels are fixed, on sprung aluminium legs with streamlined spats. [[1]](#source-1)

## Small, light and strong

The two-seat Eagle II weighs about 1,025 lb empty and has a 200 hp Lycoming engine driving a constant-speed propeller. It is stressed to +9 and −6 g and rolls at 187 degrees a second. [[1]](#source-1)

## The Eagles Aerobatic Team

In 1979 three well-known display pilots, Charlie Hillard, Tom Poberezny and Gene Soucy, replaced the Pitts Specials of their "Red Devils" act with single-seat Christen Eagles. As the Eagles Aerobatic Team they flew close-formation aerobatics at air shows until 1995. All three of their aircraft now hang in the lobby of the EAA AirVenture Museum in Oshkosh, Wisconsin.
