## The Marines' own tanker

The KC-130 is the tanker version of the [C-130 Hercules](record:c-130j). The first, the KC-130F, flew in January 1960 as the GV-1 under the old Navy designation system and entered service in 1962 to refuel Marine Corps aircraft. [[1]](#source-1) The KC-130J is built on the Super Hercules and replaced the Marines' older F, R and T models; the last reserve squadron to fly legacy KC-130s converted in 2021. [[1]](#source-1)

## Slow enough for helicopters

A Hercules flies slowly enough to refuel helicopters and tiltrotors as well as jets, all by probe and drogue. [[1]](#source-1) Its two wing pods can each pass up to 300 US gallons a minute at the same time, which lets it refuel a typical four-aircraft formation in under 30 minutes. [[1]](#source-1) On the ground it can refuel helicopters, vehicles and fuel caches, which suits forward airstrips. [[1]](#source-1)

## Into combat

VMGR-252 at Cherry Point was the first squadron to fly the KC-130J, and in February 2005 it took six to Al Asad in Iraq, the J model's first combat deployment, flying refuelling, cargo, passengers and battlefield illumination. [[1]](#source-1) With the Harvest HAWK kit, first deployed in 2010, a KC-130J could also watch over troops and fire Hellfire or Griffin missiles; the Marines have since retired the kit. [[1]](#source-1)
