## Overview

The F/A-18 Hornet is a twin-engine, carrier-capable, supersonic multirole aircraft, designed as both a fighter and an attack aircraft, hence the F/A designation. It can fly fighter escort, fleet air defence, suppression of enemy air defences, air interdiction, close air support and reconnaissance. [[1]](#source-1)

## Design

The Hornet grew out of Northrop's YF-17, which lost the US Air Force Lightweight Fighter competition to the YF-16. The US Navy chose the YF-17 for its Air Combat Fighter programme, and McDonnell Douglas and Northrop developed it into the F/A-18. [[1]](#source-1)

It was designed around versatile avionics, multifunction cockpit displays and good handling at high angle of attack. [[1]](#source-1) The two-seat F/A-18D can carry a weapon systems officer in the rear cockpit, or controls for training. [[2]](#source-2)

## Sensors and weapons

The F/A-18C's APG-73 radar and the targeting pods it carries let one pilot switch between air-to-air and air-to-ground work on the same sortie. The gun is an M61A1 in the nose. [[1]](#source-1)

## In service

The Hornet entered US Navy service in 1983, replacing the A-7 Corsair II and the last F-4 Phantoms. It first saw combat in the 1986 US strikes on Libya, then in the 1991 Gulf War, over Bosnia and Kosovo in the 1990s and in the 2003 Iraq War. [[1]](#source-1) The US Navy replaced it with the larger F/A-18E/F Super Hornet and retired its last Hornets in 2019; the US Marine Corps and several other air forces still fly it. [[1]](#source-1) It also flew with the Navy's Blue Angels demonstration team. [[1]](#source-1)
