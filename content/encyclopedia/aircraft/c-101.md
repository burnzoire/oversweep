## Simple on purpose

In 1975 the Spanish Air Force needed a jet trainer to replace its ageing Hispano HA-200 Saetas, with a limited ability to attack ground targets, as most European trainers of the day had. CASA won the contract that October and looked abroad for help: engineers at Germany's MBB designed the rear fuselage and tail, and Northrop's team the wing and engine intakes. [[1]](#source-1) What came out was deliberately conventional. Simplicity and economy mattered more than high performance, and the C-101 has a straight, unswept wing where its contemporaries, the Hawk and the Alpha Jet, had more sophisticated aerodynamics. [[1]](#source-1)

Two crew sit in tandem, the rear seat raised so that the instructor can see past the student. [[1]](#source-1) The engine is a Honeywell TFE731 turbofan, a commercial engine reworked for military use, chosen for its good performance and fuel economy. [[1]](#source-1) The aircraft needed long legs: the requirement asked it to fly itself from mainland Spain to the Canary Islands. [[1]](#source-1)

Its one unusual feature is a large internal bay beneath the rear cockpit. It can hold a gun pod, other weapons or [reconnaissance](term:reconnaissance) equipment, adding to six underwing hardpoints. [[1]](#source-1)

## The Blackbird

The first of four prototypes flew on 27 June 1977, and the aircraft performed better than its designers had predicted. [[1]](#source-1) Spain ordered 88 as pure trainers, designated **E.25 Mirlo**, "Blackbird", and the first entered service on 17 March 1980. [[1]](#source-1) For more than four decades it was where Spanish military pilots learned to fly jets, and for forty years it was the mount of the *Patrulla Águila* aerobatic team. [[1]](#source-1)

Armed versions followed. Chile built its own under licence as the T-36 and A-36 Halcón ("Falcon"), and Honduras and Jordan also bought them. [[1]](#source-1) A final C-101DD, with a [head-up display](term:hud), hands-on-throttle-and-stick controls, a radar warning receiver and the ability to fire Maverick missiles, was demonstrated in 1985 but found no buyers. [[1]](#source-1)

Spain ended regular training flights in 2022. In June 2025, after 45 years of service and some 285,000 training flight hours, the Spanish Air and Space Force retired the C-101 after a final display at the Aire25 festival at San Javier. [[1]](#source-1)
