## Overview

The F-15 Eagle is a twin-engine, all-weather air superiority fighter. The US Air Force chose McDonnell Douglas's design in 1969; it first flew in July 1972 and entered service in 1976. It is among the most successful modern fighters, with more than 104 air-to-air victories and no losses in air combat. [[1]](#source-1)

## Design

The Eagle answered the US Air Force's need for a dedicated air superiority fighter after Vietnam. It gives the pilot all-round vision, high-speed manoeuvrability and a 20 mm gun, and it flies higher and faster than the F-4 it replaced, with much better climb and acceleration. Its radar was designed for look-down, shoot-down engagements. [[2]](#source-2) A secondary ground-attack capability was built in but largely unused on the single-seat fighter. [[1]](#source-1)

## Sensors and weapons

The F-15C carries up to eight air-to-air missiles: Sidewinders on the wing pylons and Sparrows or AMRAAMs on the fuselage stations, plus the internal M61 gun with 940 rounds. [[2]](#source-2) Later radar upgrades replaced the original APG-63 with active electronically scanned array (AESA) versions on some aircraft. [[1]](#source-1)

## In service

The F-15 was the principal air superiority fighter of the US Air Force and several allies during the late Cold War. Israel first used it in combat in 1979, and it saw extensive action in the 1982 Lebanon War. US Air Force Eagles fought in the 1991 Gulf War and over Yugoslavia. The F-22 began replacing the F-15C in the 2000s. [[1]](#source-1)

Its success led to the two-seat **F-15E Strike Eagle**, an all-weather strike derivative. [[1]](#source-1)
