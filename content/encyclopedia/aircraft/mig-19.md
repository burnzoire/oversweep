## Overview

The MiG-19 was the first Soviet production aircraft capable of supersonic speed in level flight. [[1]](#source-1) It was the Soviet Union's primary fighter in the late 1950s, until the MiG-21 replaced it in the early 1960s, and it served on for years with many other countries. [[2]](#source-2)

## Design

The MiG-19 has two turbojets fed from a single nose intake, which is divided inside so that each engine has its own duct, and a sharply swept wing with fences. [[2]](#source-2) The **MiG-19P** all-weather fighter added the RP-5 Izumrud radar, whose antennas sit in a small radome in the intake centre body and another on the top lip of the intake, and carried its cannon in the wing roots. [[1]](#source-1)

## In service

China built the aircraft as the J-6, its first supersonic fighter, and exported it widely. [[2]](#source-2) The J-6 was found more manoeuvrable than the MiG-21; although slower, its acceleration in a dogfight was considered adequate. [[1]](#source-1) MiG-19s served with North Vietnam, North Korea, Cuba, Iraq and most of the Warsaw Pact. [[2]](#source-2)
