## Back to the delta

In the early 1970s Dassault's big project was not a delta at all. It was the twin-engined Super Mirage G8A, meant to be France's future combat aircraft. It turned out ambitious, over-engineered and two and a half times the price of a [Mirage F1](record:mirage-f1), at the very moment that the cheap American [F-16](record:f-16) was winning orders across Europe. On 18 December 1975 France's National Defence Council cancelled it, and at the same meeting Dassault offered a smaller, single-engined design that had begun as a side project: the Mirage 2000. [[1]](#source-1)

It looked like a return to the classic Mirage III, a tailless delta, but it was an entirely new aircraft. [[2]](#source-2) A delta wing gives a large wing area, low wing loading and a high top speed, but it is awkward at low speed. Fly-by-wire controls fixed that, letting the 2000 manoeuvre well where earlier deltas struggled. [[1]](#source-1) The first prototype flew on 10 March 1978, just 27 months after the go-ahead. On that first flight Jean Coureau went [supersonic](term:supersonic) without [afterburner](term:afterburner), and by the end of May the aircraft had passed [Mach](term:mach-number) 2. At the Farnborough Airshow that September it showed the other end of the range, flying nose-high at 25 degrees [angle of attack](term:aoa) at just 190 km/h. [[1]](#source-1)

## The fighter

The **Mirage 2000C**, C for *chasseur* (fighter), first flew in production form on 20 November 1982, and the first [squadron](term:squadron) formed in 1984, the French Air Force's 50th anniversary. [[1]](#source-1) Its job was interception, with ground attack second. [[1]](#source-1) The first 37 aircraft carried the RDM radar and the M53-5 engine, a single-shaft [afterburning](term:afterburner) turbofan; from the 38th the more powerful M53-P2 was fitted. The RDI pulse-Doppler radar that entered service in 1987 could see about 150 km and was paired with the [Super 530D](record:super-530) radar-guided missile, with much better look-down, shoot-down performance. [Magic II](record:magic-2) infrared missiles and two 30 mm [DEFA cannon](record:defa-550) complete the air-to-air fit. [[1]](#source-1) Later variants include the two-seat 2000N nuclear strike, 2000D conventional attack and 2000-5 multirole aircraft. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2)

## In action

The 2000C went to war in 1991, flying [combat air patrols](term:cap) over Saudi Arabia and escorting strike and [reconnaissance](term:reconnaissance) aircraft over Kuwait and southern Iraq. [[1]](#source-1) French Mirage 2000s went on to fly over Bosnia, Kosovo, Afghanistan, Libya and Syria. [[1]](#source-1) In 2017, during the Barkhane operation in Africa, a pair answered a militant attack on US and African troops with low "intimidation" passes, because the fighting was too close to the friendly troops to use weapons safely. [[1]](#source-1) On 9 March 2024 two Mirage 2000-5Fs shot down Houthi drones over the Red Sea, France's first air-to-air victories since the Second World War. [[1]](#source-1)

The Mirage 2000 sold widely abroad. India, which bought it in answer to Pakistan's [F-16s](record:f-16) and named it *Vajra*, "thunderbolt" in Sanskrit, flew it in the Kargil War, and on 26 February 2019 twelve Indian Mirages struck a target at Balakot in Pakistan, the first time Indian fighters had entered Pakistani airspace since 1971. [[1]](#source-1) The UAE flew its Mirages in the Gulf War and over Yemen, Peru in the Cenepa War, and Ukraine has flown them against Russia. [[1]](#source-1)

France retired its last 2000Cs in June 2022, after their final years training pilots for the newer variants; the Rafale is replacing the Mirage 2000 family in French service. [[1]](#source-1)
