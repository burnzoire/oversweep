## Overview

The MiG-15 is a Soviet swept-wing jet fighter, developed as an interceptor to shoot down heavy bombers. The first version was powered by the RD-45, a copy of the Rolls-Royce Nene. [[2]](#source-2) More than 17,000 were built in the Soviet Union and under licence. [[1]](#source-1)

## Design

Second World War experience showed that guns larger than 20 mm were needed to bring down four-engine bombers, so the MiG-15 carries one 37 mm and two 23 mm cannon. [[2]](#source-2) Early combat with US aircraft led to the **MiG-15bis**, whose VK-1 engine gave about 1,000 lb more thrust, and which added hydraulically boosted ailerons. [[2]](#source-2)

## In service

MiG-15s appeared over Korea in late 1950. [[2]](#source-2) In combat they outclassed the straight-winged jet day fighters of the time, which were largely moved to ground attack, and the US Air Force rushed the F-86 Sabre to Korea to counter them. [[1]](#source-1) The MiG-15bis could climb faster and higher than the F-86, but poor turning performance and instability at high Mach limited it in a dogfight, and it suffered heavy losses to Sabres. Against B-29 bombers it was very effective. [[2]](#source-2)
