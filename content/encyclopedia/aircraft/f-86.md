## Overview

The F-86 Sabre is a transonic, swept-wing jet fighter, best known as the US fighter that could meet the Soviet MiG-15 in the Korean War, in some of the earliest jet-against-jet battles. It is the most-produced Western jet fighter, with 9,860 built across all variants. [[1]](#source-1)

## Design

North American Aviation, builder of the P-51 Mustang, first proposed a straight-winged jet fighter. The design was reworked with swept wings, and the XP-86 first flew on 1 October 1947. [[1]](#source-1)

The F-86F, introduced in 1953, has the more powerful J47-GE-27 engine. Its six 0.50 in machine guns are aimed through a gunsight ranged by the APG-30 radar. It could carry two or, from 1953, four drop tanks to extend its range, and the fighter-bomber versions carried bombs and rockets on the underwing pylons. [[1]](#source-1)

## In service

Over Korea the most intense air combat took place in **MiG Alley**, near the mouth of the Yalu River on the border with China. Once the F-86F arrived in 1953 the Sabre and MiG-15 were closely matched, and many experienced US pilots judged the F-86F slightly superior. Post-war reviews cut the claimed kill ratio against the MiG-15 by half, and later research puts it closer to 2:1. [[1]](#source-1) Two US fighter-bomber wings converted to the F-86F in spring 1953. [[1]](#source-1)

After Korea the Sabre served with many air forces; Taiwan alone had received 320 F-86Fs by 1958. [[1]](#source-1)
