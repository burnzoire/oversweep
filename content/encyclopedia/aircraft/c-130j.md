## Overview

The C-130J Super Hercules is the modernised version of the four-engine C-130 tactical transport. It keeps the classic airframe but has new engines and propellers, digital avionics and a smaller crew. [[1]](#source-1)

## Design

Compared with older C-130s, the C-130J climbs faster and higher, flies farther at a higher cruise speed, and takes off and lands in a shorter distance. [[2]](#source-2) The Rolls-Royce AE 2100D3 turboprops drive six-bladed composite Dowty propellers with scimitar tips, and each pilot has a head-up display. The cargo crew is two pilots and a loadmaster, with no navigator or flight engineer. [[1]](#source-1) The stretched C-130J-30 is 15 ft longer, using two fuselage plugs. [[2]](#source-2)

In the troop-carrying role a C-130 seats 92 combat troops or 64 paratroops, who exit through doors on either side behind the landing-gear fairings or off the rear ramp; for medical evacuation it carries 74 litters. [[2]](#source-2)

## In service

The US Air Force began buying C-130Js in 1996 to replace C-130Es and Hs, starting with combat delivery aircraft. [[2]](#source-2) Variants include the US Marine Corps KC-130J tanker, which refuels fixed-wing aircraft and helicopters and conducts ground refuelling, and the WC-130J that flies into hurricanes. [[2]](#source-2)
