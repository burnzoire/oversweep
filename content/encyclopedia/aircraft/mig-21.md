## Overview

The MiG-21 is a small, single-engine Soviet delta-wing fighter and interceptor. With 11,496 built, it is the most-produced supersonic jet aircraft in history, and it has served with dozens of air forces. [[1]](#source-1)

## Design

The MiG-21 pairs a tailed delta wing with a single turbojet fed by a nose intake with a shock cone. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2) It is light, which gives it a high climb rate: the MiG-21bis, with the more powerful R-25 engine and its emergency boost, has slightly better than 1:1 thrust-to-weight. [[1]](#source-1)

The **MiG-21bis** was the final major production version. It brought the R-25 engine, four wing hardpoints instead of two, and compatibility with the R-60 short-range missile. [[1]](#source-1)

## In service

Over Vietnam, the MiG-21 was a difficult opponent for US fighters when flown by experienced pilots in fast hit-and-run attacks under ground-controlled interception (GCI). [[1]](#source-1) China built it as the J-7 after a 1961 licence, and Chinese versions were exported widely, including the F-7 to Pakistan. [[2]](#source-2) US Navy adversary training programmes used the F-5 Tiger II among the aircraft that stood in for it. [[1]](#source-1)
