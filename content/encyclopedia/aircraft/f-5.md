## Overview

The F-5 is a small, supersonic light fighter built around two compact J85 engines, designed to be cheap to buy and maintain. The original F-5A/B Freedom Fighter went mainly to US allies; the much improved **F-5E Tiger II** followed in 1972. [[1]](#source-1)

## Design

Northrop began the design in the mid-1950s as a lightweight supersonic fighter for NATO and SEATO allies that could fly from short runways. The US Air Force first bought it as the T-38 Talon trainer, and in 1962 the Department of Defense chose the fighter for its Military Assistance Program. [[2]](#source-2)

The F-5A was optimised for ground attack and had no fire-control radar. [[2]](#source-2) The F-5E, built to win the International Fighter Aircraft competition for allies facing late MiG-21s, added more powerful engines, more fuel, a larger wing, improved leading-edge extensions for better turn rates, optional air refuelling and an air-to-air radar. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2)

## In service

In 1965 the US Air Force sent twelve F-5As to Vietnam for combat trials under the code name Skoshi Tiger, which gave the aircraft its nickname. [[2]](#source-2)

The US Navy Fighter Weapons School (Top Gun) and other Navy units flew F-5Es as adversary aircraft, because their size and performance resembled the MiG-21. In the 1990s the Navy moved more adversary training to the F-5, which cost about a third as much to operate as an F/A-18. [[2]](#source-2) The F-5 remains in US service for training and with many other air forces. [[1]](#source-1)
