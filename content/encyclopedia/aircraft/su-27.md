## Overview

The Su-27, NATO reporting name **Flanker**, is a large twin-engine Soviet air superiority fighter, designed to compete directly with US fourth-generation fighters such as the F-14 and F-15. [[1]](#source-1) Together with the MiG-29, it ended the advantage the F-15 and F-16 had given Western pilots in the mid-1970s. [[2]](#source-2)

## Design

The Su-27 has a fly-by-wire control system, a blended wing-body with large leading-edge extensions, twin tails mounted outboard of the engines, and can carry up to ten air-to-air missiles. [[2]](#source-2) Its long range, 3,530 km on internal fuel, comes from a very large internal fuel load. [[1]](#source-1) Test pilot Viktor Pugachev first demonstrated the **Cobra** manoeuvre, a sudden pitch-up to beyond vertical and back, in an Su-27 in 1989. [[1]](#source-1)

## Sensors and weapons

The N001 radar gives track-while-scan and look-down/shoot-down capability, and the OLS-27 infrared search and track lets the pilot find and track targets passively. [[1]](#source-1)

## In service

The Su-27 serves with Russia and other former Soviet states, and with China, Vietnam, Ethiopia and Indonesia among others. China bought Su-27SKs and a licence to build its own, the J-11. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2) The Su-27 has been the basis of a large family including the navalised Su-33 and the Su-30 and Su-35. [[1]](#source-1)
