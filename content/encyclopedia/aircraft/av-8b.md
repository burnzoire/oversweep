## Overview

The AV-8B Harrier II is a single-engine ground-attack aircraft that can take off and land vertically or on very short strips (V/STOL). It is the second generation of the Harrier family and flies light attack and multirole missions, from close air support to armed reconnaissance. It served with the US Marine Corps and serves with the Spanish and Italian navies. [[1]](#source-1)

## Design

The project began in the early 1970s as a joint US–UK effort to fix the shortcomings of the first-generation Harrier, centred on a bigger Pegasus engine. After the UK withdrew in 1975, McDonnell Douglas redesigned the AV-8A into the AV-8B: a larger composite wing with an extra hardpoint each side, a raised cockpit, a redesigned fuselage and an improved Pegasus. [[1]](#source-1)

The single Pegasus turbofan exhausts through four rotating nozzles. Pointing them down lifts the aircraft vertically; pointing them aft gives conventional thrust; in between, the aircraft makes short take-offs, which carry far more weight than a vertical lift. [[1]](#source-1)

## Sensors and weapons

Upgrades added a night-attack capability, the **AV-8B(NA)**, and then radar in the **Harrier II Plus**. [[1]](#source-1) The Night Attack uses an uprated Pegasus F402-RR-408 of about 22,200 lb thrust. [[2]](#source-2) The gun is a 25 mm GAU-12 carried in a pod under the fuselage, with ammunition in the opposite pod. [[1]](#source-1)

## In service

US Marine Corps Harriers operated from amphibious assault ships and forward sites ashore, close to the troops they supported. The Corps retired its last AV-8B on 3 June 2026, replacing it with the F-35B. [[1]](#source-1)
