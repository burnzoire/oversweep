## The first of a new kind

When it appeared in the mid-1930s, the I-16 was the first low-wing monoplane fighter with a retractable undercarriage to reach front-line [squadrons](term:squadron), and it "introduced a new vogue in fighter design". [[1]](#source-1) Nikolai Polikarpov designed it while still working on biplanes: a tiny, stubby fighter with a radial engine in a streamlined cowling, an enclosed cockpit and wheels that folded away. It was small, light and simple to build, with a wooden fuselage and partly fabric-covered wings. [[1]](#source-1) The famous test pilot Valery Chkalov first flew it on 30 December 1933. [[1]](#source-1)

It was not easy to fly. The early version was judged so sensitive to abrupt control inputs that aerobatics were forbidden. The pilot had to crank the undercarriage up and down by hand, which took real strength and often jammed, so many test flights were made with the wheels down. [[1]](#source-1) In service it rolled faster than 240 degrees a second, but it was unstable in pitch and could not be flown hands-off, and its canopy often fouled with oil or slammed shut in hard manoeuvres, so many pilots locked it open. [[1]](#source-1) By the time the Type 5 arrived it was both the lightest and the fastest production fighter in the world. [[1]](#source-1)

## Donkey, rat, fly and swallow

Soviet pilots called it **Ishak**, "donkey", because the word sounds like the Russian pronunciation of "I-16". In Spain the Republicans who flew it called it the *Mosca* ("fly") and the Nationalists who fought it the *Rata* ("rat"); the Chinese called it *Lastochka*, "swallow", and the Finns "flying squirrel". [[1]](#source-1)

## Spain, China and Khalkhin Gol

Stalin sent the Spanish Republic hundreds of I-16s in return for gold. On 13 November 1936 twelve of them intercepted a Nationalist bombing raid on Madrid, and the little monoplanes dominated the enemy's Heinkel and Arado biplanes until the [Bf 109](record:bf-109k-4) arrived. [[1]](#source-1) In China, Chinese aces flew I-16s against Japanese fighters, and in 1939 about 500 fought Japan at Khalkhin Gol on the Mongolian border. [[1]](#source-1) There, on 20 August 1939, Captain N. Zvonarev hit a Japanese Ki-27 with an RS-82 rocket fired from about a kilometre away, the first successful use of an air-to-air rocket in combat. [[1]](#source-1)

## 22 June 1941

When Germany invaded the Soviet Union on 22 June 1941, I-16s made up 1,635 of the 4,226 aircraft of the Soviet air force on the frontier. German attacks on their airfields destroyed hundreds in the first 48 hours. [[1]](#source-1) In the air the I-16 could turn with a Bf 109E, and skilled pilots who made use of that resisted switching to newer fighters, but the Messerschmitt was faster, climbed better and dived better, and the Germans held the initiative. [[1]](#source-1) Around half of all I-16s were still in service in 1943. Some were even carried under the wings of TB-3 bombers as "parasite" dive-bombers, released near the target with two 250 kg bombs. [[1]](#source-1)
