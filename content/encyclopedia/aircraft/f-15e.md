## Overview

The F-15E Strike Eagle is a two-seat, all-weather strike fighter derived from the F-15 Eagle. It was designed in the 1980s for long-range, high-speed interdiction without relying on escort or electronic warfare aircraft, and it became the US Air Force's primary strike fighter, gradually replacing the F-111. [[1]](#source-1)

## Design

US Air Force Strike Eagles are told apart from other Eagles by their darker camouflage, the conformal fuel tanks (CFTs) along the fuselage, LANTIRN pods under the engine intakes and the tandem cockpit. [[1]](#source-1) The pilot flies and fights the aircraft; the weapon systems officer in the rear seat works the radar, targeting pods and weapons. [[1]](#source-1)

## Sensors and weapons

The F-15E's radar maps the ground in high resolution for attacks at night and in bad weather, and LANTIRN gave it low-level navigation and laser targeting at night. Later aircraft carry the APG-82 AESA radar and newer targeting pods. [[1]](#source-1) Typical loads combine guided bombs or Mavericks with AIM-9 and AIM-120 missiles for self-defence. [[2]](#source-2) It is also the US Air Force's most capable tactical nuclear delivery aircraft, able to carry up to five B61 Mod 12 bombs. [[1]](#source-1)

## In service

The Strike Eagle has flown combat operations over Iraq, Afghanistan, Syria, Libya and Iran, among others, carrying out deep strikes on high-value targets, combat air patrols and close air support for coalition troops. It has been exported, and is expected to stay in US service into the 2030s; the F-15EX Eagle II continues the line. [[1]](#source-1)
