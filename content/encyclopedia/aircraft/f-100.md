## Overview

The F-100 Super Sabre was the first of the US Century Series fighters and the first US Air Force fighter that could fly faster than sound in level flight. Crews called it the **Hun**. [[1]](#source-1)

## Design

North American offered the F-100, first called the Sabre 45, as a faster successor to the F-86. The YF-100A flew on 25 May 1953, seven months ahead of schedule. Testing showed yaw instability and inertia coupling, and after six major accidents in its first weeks of service the F-100A was grounded from November 1954 to February 1955 while they were fixed. [[1]](#source-1)

Tactical Air Command wanted a fighter-bomber, which led to the F-100C and then the more capable **F-100D**, the definitive model. The two-seat F-100F was a supersonic trainer. [[1]](#source-1)

## In service

The F-100D entered service on 29 September 1956 with the 405th Fighter Wing at Langley. [[1]](#source-1)

In Vietnam, F-100s flew combat air patrol escort for F-105s, forward air control and Wild Weasel missions over North Vietnam, before being moved to close air support in South Vietnam. Two-seat F-100Fs flew as "Misty" fast forward air controllers over North Vietnam and Laos, spotting targets for other fighter-bombers. The F-100 was the US Air Force's main close air support aircraft over South Vietnam until the A-7 Corsair II replaced it. [[1]](#source-1)
