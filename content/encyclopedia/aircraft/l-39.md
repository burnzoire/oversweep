## Overview

The L-39 Albatros is a Czechoslovak two-seat jet trainer, successor to the L-29 Delfín. It became the standard jet trainer of the Soviet Union, Czechoslovakia and the other Warsaw Pact air forces, and was widely exported. [[2]](#source-2)

## Design

The L-39 is a straight-winged jet with a single AI-25TL turbofan in the rear fuselage, fed by shoulder intakes, and tandem seats with the instructor's rear seat slightly raised to watch the student. [[1]](#source-1) Armed versions added a weapons role: the **L-39ZO**, first flown in 1975, has four underwing hardpoints and a reinforced wing, and the **L-39ZA** of 1976 adds an underfuselage GSh-23 cannon and improved avionics. [[2]](#source-2)

## In service

Besides training, armed L-39s have flown light-attack combat missions with several air forces. [[1]](#source-1) The design led to the L-159 light combat aircraft, which entered production in 1997. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2)
