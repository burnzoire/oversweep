## Overview

The F-14 Tomcat is a carrier-based, twin-engine, two-seat fighter with variable-sweep wings. It was the US Navy's main maritime air superiority fighter, fleet defence interceptor and tactical reconnaissance aircraft from 1974 into the 2000s. [[1]](#source-1)

## Design

The Tomcat was developed for the Navy's VFX programme after the F-111B was cancelled. It was the first of the US "Teen Series" fighters, designed with the Vietnam War lessons of fighting smaller, more manoeuvrable MiGs. [[1]](#source-1) The wings sweep automatically with speed, from 64 ft span unswept to 38 ft fully swept, which gives a low carrier approach speed of about 125 kt and high speed in the dash. [[2]](#source-2)

The pilot sits in front and the radar intercept officer behind, running the radar and the long-range AIM-54 Phoenix missiles. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2) The F-14A's TF30 engines were replaced by the more powerful General Electric F110 in the F-14B and F-14D. [[2]](#source-2)

## Sensors and weapons

The AWG-9 radar and Phoenix gave the Tomcat its fleet-defence role: engaging bombers and missile carriers far from the carrier. It also carries Sparrow and Sidewinder missiles and an internal M61 gun. [[1]](#source-1) With the LANTIRN pod added in the 1990s, the Tomcat began flying precision ground-attack missions with laser-guided bombs. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2)

## In service

The F-14 replaced the F-4 Phantom in the fleet and was itself replaced by the F/A-18E/F Super Hornet, retiring from the US Navy on 22 September 2006. [[1]](#source-1)

Iran bought F-14s in 1976 and used them as land-based interceptors. In the Iran–Iraq War, Iran claimed its F-14s shot down at least 160 Iraqi aircraft, 55 of them confirmed. Iran is the only remaining operator. [[1]](#source-1)
