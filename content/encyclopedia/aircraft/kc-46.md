## A ten-year competition

The Air Force began trying to replace its oldest [KC-135s](record:kc-135) in 2001 with a lease of Boeing KC-767s. Criticism of the lease, and a corruption investigation that jailed a former Pentagon procurement executive who had applied to work for Boeing, killed that deal in 2006. [[1]](#source-1) In 2008 the Pentagon chose a version of the Airbus A330 tanker instead, but a protest upheld by the Government Accountability Office forced a new competition. In February 2011 Boeing's 767-based bid won, as the KC-46A. [[1]](#source-1)

## A boom operator who never looks out

Older tankers seat the boom operator in the tail, looking straight at the receiver. The KC-46 has two operator stations just behind the flight deck, fed by camera imagery from its Remote Vision System. [[1]](#source-1) That system's depth compression and curvature distortion, which affected how operators judged motion, and glare in some light, were among the deficiencies that followed the tanker into service; an upgraded RVS 2.0 with high-definition colour cameras is meant to fix them. [[1]](#source-1) The KC-46 carries a boom, a centreline drogue and wing pods, and first refuelled an F/A-18 by drogue in February 2016. [[1]](#source-1)

## Into service

The first aircraft reached the Air Force in January 2019, and the service plans 263 in all. [[1]](#source-1) Japan, which already flew four KC-767s, and Israel have bought KC-46s too. [[1]](#source-1)
