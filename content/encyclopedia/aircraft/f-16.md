## Overview

The F-16 is a single-engine, supersonic multirole fighter. It was designed as a lightweight daytime air superiority fighter and grew into an all-weather multirole aircraft, with more than 4,600 built. Crews call it the **Viper**. [[1]](#source-1)

## Design

The F-16 came out of the US Air Force's Lightweight Fighter programme of the early 1970s, which asked for a small, cheap fighter with very high performance below Mach 1.6, where air combat is actually fought. General Dynamics' YF-16 was chosen over Northrop's twin-engine YF-17. [[2]](#source-2)

It was the first fighter built with relaxed static stability and fly-by-wire controls, and the first purpose-built to pull 9 g. A frameless bubble canopy gives the pilot an unobstructed view, the control stick is mounted on the right console, and the seat is reclined 30 degrees to help the pilot tolerate g. [[1]](#source-1) The wing blends into the fuselage, and the space this creates holds fuel. [[2]](#source-2)

## Sensors and weapons

The F-16C and two-seat F-16D entered production in 1984. The first C/D, the Block 25, brought an improved cockpit and the AN/APG-68 radar, which gave it all-weather, beyond-visual-range capability with the AIM-7 and AIM-120. Later C/D blocks are the 30/32, 40/42 and 50/52; the second number of a pair marks the Pratt & Whitney-engined version. [[1]](#source-1)

The gun is an internal M61A1 Vulcan in the left wing root. Missiles, bombs, fuel tanks and pods hang on the wingtip rails and the underwing and underfuselage stations. [[1]](#source-1)

## In service

The F-16 became a mainstay of US Air Force tactical airpower, replacing the F-105, A-7 and F-4. It mainly flew strike and suppression of enemy air defences (SEAD) missions, and by 1996 it had replaced the F-4G Wild Weasel in the SEAD role. [[1]](#source-1) US Air Force F-16s flew air-to-ground missions in the 1991 Gulf War and every conflict after it, and Israel has also used the aircraft in combat. [[2]](#source-2)

US F-16s also fly with the Air Force Reserve and Air National Guard, the Thunderbirds demonstration team, and as adversary aircraft for the US Navy. [[1]](#source-1)
