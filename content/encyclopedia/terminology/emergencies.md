terms:
  - term: Critical action items
    definition: "The steps of an emergency procedure marked in the flight manual for immediate action, which the pilot must be able to recite word for word; the other steps, notes, warnings and cautions must be understood and explained from memory but not memorised verbatim. Also called boldface or critical memory items."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "1" }, { n: 2, page: "3" }]
  - term: Land as soon as possible
    definition: "Land at the first place at which a safe landing can be made, because the emergency (smoke and fumes, for example) needs the aircraft on the ground right now."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "2" }]
  - term: Land as soon as practical
    definition: "Extended flight is not recommended because of the nature of the emergency, but the duration of the flight and the landing site are at the discretion of the pilot in command."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "2" }]
  - term: PEL
    expansion: Precautionary emergency landing
    definition: "A landing flown from a power-off emergency landing pattern when the single engine might quit or may need to be shut down, such as for a chip light, low fuel pressure or an unconfirmed fire."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "2" }, { n: 3, page: "5" }]
  - term: ELP
    expansion: Emergency landing pattern
    definition: "The power-off pattern a single-engine aircraft flies to a runway for a precautionary emergency landing or after an engine failure, managed by height at checkpoints beginning with high key."
    cite: [{ n: 3, page: "5" }]
  - term: High key
    definition: "The first checkpoint of a power-off emergency landing pattern, reached at a set height over the landing field; each type's manual gives the height, which the T-6 training notes put at 3,000 ft AGL."
    cite: [{ n: 3, page: "5" }]
  - term: Low key
    definition: "The checkpoint of a power-off emergency landing pattern after high key, at a lower set height on the downwind side of the field; the T-6 training notes put it at 1,500 ft AGL."
    cite: [{ n: 3, page: "5" }]
  - term: Emergency fuel
    definition: "The point at which it is necessary to proceed directly to the airport of intended landing due to low fuel. Declaring emergency fuel is an explicit statement that priority handling by ATC is both required and expected."
    note: "Minimum fuel, by contrast, is not an emergency: the aircraft can accept little or no delay."
    cite: [{ n: 4, page: "Attachment 1" }]
  - term: IROK
    definition: "The post-ejection checklist under the parachute: Inflate the life preserver, Release the raft, Options (steering, visor, mask, gloves, seat survival kit) and the canopy release fittings (FROST fittings in the T-6), released on water entry over water or after landing over land."
    cite: [{ n: 5, page: "1–2" }]
  - term: PLF
    expansion: Parachute landing fall
    definition: "The way to land under a parachute over land, taking the impact on five points of contact in turn: balls of the feet, side of the calf, side of the thigh, side of the buttocks and the shoulder blade. The heels should never touch the ground."
    cite: [{ n: 5, page: "2" }]
  - term: ADR
    definition: "After a parachute water entry: Avoid the parachute, Disentangle from it, then Release the seat survival kit on one side and retrieve the survival items."
    cite: [{ n: 5, page: "2" }]

## The words of emergencies

These are the words a military pilot uses when something goes wrong: how the steps of an emergency procedure are learned, how soon to land, the power-off landing pattern of a single-engine aircraft, the fuel states that get priority from ATC, and what to do under the parachute after an ejection. Most come from Navy primary training squadrons' study aids for the T-6, so the heights quoted are that aircraft's; each type's flight manual gives its own. [[1]](#source-1) [[3]](#source-3) [[5]](#source-5)

Emergency fuel is the Air Force's definition. [[4]](#source-4) MAYDAY, PAN-PAN, minimum fuel and squawk are in the airfield and ATC terms, and autorotation in the flight terms.
