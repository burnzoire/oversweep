## What a brevity word is

A **brevity word** is a code word that provides no security: it serves only to shorten a message, not to hide what it means. [[3]](#source-3) The words on this page come from the multi-service brevity publication, which the Army, Marine Corps, Navy and Air Force publish together under four numbers (ATP 1-02.1, MCRP 3-30B.1, NTTP 6-02.1 and AFTTP 3-2.5), so a pilot from any service means the same thing by them. [[1]](#source-1)

The publication lists several hundred words. This page gives the ones a pilot meets most often on the radio: fuel and endurance, sighting calls, threat and radar-warning calls, weapons calls, and the directives that start or stop an engagement. Words printed in capitals inside a definition are themselves brevity words. [[1]](#source-1)

**Domains.** Unless a word is tagged otherwise, its meaning applies to general air operations. Tags such as air-to-air, air-to-surface, surface-to-air, maritime air, space operations and electromagnetic warfare limit a meaning to that kind of operation. [[1]](#source-1)

**NATO.** The publication marks words whose meaning may differ from the NATO brevity word, and words that are not NATO brevity words at all, against NATO's Joint Brevity Words Publication (APP-7). Those marks are repeated here in each word's note, so a pilot flying with NATO allies knows which calls to confirm in the brief. [[1]](#source-1)

**Edition.** The definitions are quoted from the May 2020 edition with its 2021 change. [[1]](#source-1) A 2025 edition has superseded it and may have changed some words; check the current edition before relying on a meaning. [[2]](#source-2)
