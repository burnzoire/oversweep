---
short: Air-to-air refuelling
usage: Tanker and receiver crews of every service and nation
terms:
  - term: AAR
    expansion: Air-to-air refuelling
    definition: Passing fuel from a tanker to a receiver aircraft in flight. Two systems are in use, the probe and drogue and the flyable boom, and they are not compatible with each other. Some tankers carry both.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "¶1.13" }]
  - term: Probe and drogue
    definition: The AAR system in which the tanker trails a hose ending in a reception coupling and a cone-shaped drogue, and the receiver pilot flies the aircraft's probe into the drogue. The receiver is responsible for positioning once cleared astern.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "¶1.14, ¶2.45.b" }]
  - term: Flying boom
    definition: The AAR system in which the tanker's telescopic boom, flown by a boom operator with its own small wings, is extended into the receiver's receptacle while the receiver holds steady formation. It offers the highest fuel flow, up to about 3,650 kg (8,000 lb) a minute.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "¶1.15, ¶1.17, ¶2.8.f" }]
  - term: Boom drogue adapter
    expansion: BDA
    definition: About 3 m (9 ft) of hose with a hard, non-collapsible drogue fitted to the end of a boom on the ground, so the boom can refuel probe-equipped receivers. It has no hose response system, so excessive closure can break the probe or hose, and the director lights are not used with it.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "¶1.16" }]
  - term: UARRSI
    expansion: Universal Aerial Refuelling Receptacle Slipway Installation
    definition: A modular receptacle and slipway on a receiver that guides the boom nozzle in, with boom interphone capability. Usually lit or marked for night refuelling.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "Annex 1A; ¶1.15.b" }]
  - term: Pilot director lights
    expansion: PDL
    definition: Two parallel light arrays under a boom tanker's forward fuselage telling the receiver which way to move to reach and hold the refuelling position. One array gives up and down, the other fore and aft; there are none for azimuth, which the yellow stripe on the tanker's belly gives instead.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "¶1.15.a, ¶1.18" }]
  - term: Echelon Left
    definition: The position where receivers join a tanker and wait before refuelling, normally on the left. On a tanker with a rearward observer it is stepped down, aft of the wingline and one receiver wingspan outboard; on one without, it is level and well forward, so the tanker's pilots can see you.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "¶2.5.a; Annex 1A" }]
  - term: Echelon Right
    definition: The area to the right of the tanker, level or slightly above, where receivers reform after refuelling, the first receiver closest to the wing. Receivers leave from it level or climbing.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "¶2.10.a; Annex 1A" }]
  - term: Astern position
    definition: The stable position behind the refuelling equipment with zero closure, held before contact. For probe and drogue it is about 5 ft directly aft of the drogue; for the boom, about 50 ft behind and slightly below the boom nozzle.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "Annex 1A" }]
  - term: Pre-contact
    definition: The name sim manuals give the boom astern position, where the receiver stabilizes behind the boom before being cleared to contact.
    cite: [{ n: 3, page: "162" }, { n: 4, page: "121" }]
  - term: Contact position
    definition: For boom refuelling, the stable position inside the refuelling envelope from which the boom can make contact. For probe and drogue, the receiver is in contact once the probe has engaged the coupling.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "Annex 1A; ¶1.14.a" }]
  - term: Wet contact
    definition: A contact during which fuel is transferred.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "Annex 1A" }]
  - term: Dry contact
    definition: A contact made for aircrew proficiency, with no fuel transferred.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "Annex 1A" }]
  - term: Breakaway
    definition: The emergency separation call, prefaced by the tanker's call sign and said three times, or the red light or flashing director lights when silent. The receiver disconnects at once, moves back and goes to a safe position clear of the tanker and its equipment; separation is the receiver's job.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "¶2.32, ¶2.35; Annex 2P" }]
  - term: Spokes
    definition: The call a receiver pilot makes after damaging the drogue. The drogue is not used again, and because pieces may be ingested, a training sortie normally diverts to the nearest suitable airfield and the engine is inspected after landing.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "¶2.28.f; Annex 1A" }]
  - term: Underrun
    definition: In AAR, a receiver closing so fast that it cannot stabilize in the astern position, or moving forward too far during contact or the approach to it. On a boom, a breakaway is initiated.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "Annex 1A; ¶2.28.h" }]
  - term: Overrun
    definition: A receiver passing, or about to pass, the tanker before or during its rendezvous turn. The receiver passes at least 1,000 ft below, calls "execute overrun" and decelerates; the tanker accelerates; either calls "terminate overrun" when it is no longer a factor.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "¶2.4" }]
  - term: ARIP
    expansion: AAR initial point
    definition: A planned point before the control point to which tanker and receiver time independently, to arrive at the control time; a climb or descent to the rendezvous level starts there if needed.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "Annex 1A" }]
  - term: ARCP
    expansion: AAR control point
    definition: The planned point over which the receivers arrive in the Echelon Left or astern position on their tanker.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "Annex 1A" }]
  - term: ARCT
    expansion: AAR control time
    definition: The planned time tanker and receiver arrive over the control point.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "Annex 1A" }]
  - term: Anchor
    definition: Refuelling while the tanker flies a set pattern tied to a geographical point, normally a left-hand racetrack with 50 NM legs 7 to 20 NM apart. In the anchor rendezvous (RV Alpha) a radar controller brings the receiver to 1 NM behind and 1,000 ft below the tanker.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "Annex 1A; Annex 2A" }]
  - term: Point parallel
    definition: The rendezvous (RV Delta) in which the receiver holds an agreed track and the tanker flies the reciprocal, offset, then turns at a computed range to roll out ahead of the receiver.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "¶2.11.d; Annex 2D" }]
  - term: Base AAR altitude
    definition: The reference altitude at which the lowest aircraft of a tanker formation flies, ideally at least 2,000 ft below the receiver's best altitude for refuelling.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "Annex 1A" }]
  - term: Hose response
    definition: The winding-in torque a hose drum applies against the drogue's drag, balanced so it absorbs the receiver's contact and damps hose whip, provided excessive closure is avoided.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "¶1.14.a" }]
  - term: Buddy store
    definition: A self-contained refuelling pod carried on a pylon of a combat aircraft, giving it an alternate tanker role. It needs only fuel and electrical power from the aircraft and usually drives its pump and hose drum with a ram air turbine.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "¶1.14.b" }, { n: 2 }]
  - term: Judy
    definition: In AAR, the receiver's call that it has the tanker on radar and takes responsibility for closing to visual range.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "Annex 2P" }]
  - term: HAAR
    expansion: Helicopter air-to-air refuelling
    definition: Probe and drogue refuelling of helicopters, normally at 110 to 115 KIAS, from a tanker that joins on the helicopters from behind. The receiver stays above the contact position on disconnect to keep its rotor clear of the drogue.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "¶3.2.c, ¶3.7, ¶3.10" }]

## The words of refuelling in the air

These are the words tanker and receiver crews share: the two refuelling systems, the positions around the tanker, the rendezvous points and patterns, and the emergency calls. They come from NATO's air-to-air refuelling publication, which the alliance's air forces use so that any tanker can refuel any compatible receiver. [[1]](#source-1)

EMCON, VISUAL and BINGO are in the brevity words and joint terms; the formation words are in the formation terms.
