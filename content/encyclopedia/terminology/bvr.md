usage: "Fighter aircrew of every service flying radar intercepts beyond visual range: the geometry, missile launch zones, control and section calls, and identification"
terms:
  - term: Angle-off
    expansion: AO
    definition: "The angle between the fighter's heading and the bearing to the bandit: how far off the fighter's nose the bandit is, left or right."
    note: "The US Air Force calls it antenna train angle (ATA). [1]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "8-1" }]
  - term: Antenna train angle
    expansion: ATA
    definition: "The US Air Force name for angle-off: the angle between the fighter's heading and the bearing to the bandit."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "8-1, 9-20" }]
  - term: Aspect angle
    expansion: AA
    definition: "The number of degrees from the bandit's tail to the fighter, measured at the bandit. Aspect angle plus target aspect is always 180 degrees, so 45 R target aspect is 135 R aspect angle."
    note: "Air Force aspect words are given in aspect angle, Navy words in target aspect; see Flank and Drag. [1]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "8-1" }]
  - term: Bandit reciprocal
    expansion: BR
    definition: "The heading 180 degrees from the bandit's. A fighter flying the bandit reciprocal (a zero cut) keeps its lateral separation constant as range closes."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "8-1, 10-11" }]
  - term: Lateral separation
    expansion: LS
    definition: "The horizontal distance from the fighter to the bandit's flight path: the fighter's turning room. Roughly, LS in feet = target aspect × slant range × 100, so 20 TA at 20 NM is 40,000 feet."
    note: "The rule of thumb approximates slant range × sin(target aspect). [1]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "8-1, 8-2, 9-2" }]
  - term: Cut
    definition: "The angle from the fighter's heading to the bandit reciprocal; 180 degrees minus the heading crossing angle. On a zero cut lateral separation does not change."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "8-1, 10-11" }]
  - term: Heading crossing angle
    expansion: HCA
    definition: "The angle between the fighter's and the bandit's headings at the point of intercept. Also called degrees to go."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "8-1" }]
  - term: Rate of closure
    expansion: VC
    definition: "The sum of the parts of both aircraft's speeds that close the range. It is highest with the bandit at 0 target aspect and on the fighter's nose."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "8-1, 8-5" }]
  - term: Collision course
    definition: "A straight course on which the bandit keeps a constant bearing as range decreases (constant bearing, decreasing range). Neither target aspect nor angle-off changes and the contact does not drift; with both aircraft at the same speed, angle-off equals target aspect in size, on the opposite side. It closes on the bandit in the fastest way."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "4-2, 9-2" }]
  - term: Collision bearing
    expansion: CB
    definition: "The angle-off at which the bandit would be on a collision course: half the cut, for aircraft at the same speed."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "9-2, 9-20" }]
  - term: Contact drift
    definition: "The movement of the radar contact across the display. Turn drift comes from the fighter's own turns, intercept drift from both aircraft moving through space; a contact that is not on collision always drifts away from collision bearing."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "9-1, 9-2" }]
  - term: Cutoff vector
    definition: "A heading that places the interceptor between the bandit and the force it defends."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "4-2" }]
  - term: Pure pursuit
    definition: "Keeping the nose pointed directly at the bandit. The primary way to reach its rear quarter; increasingly hard turns may be needed."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "4-3" }]
  - term: Positional advantage
    definition: "A fighter's ability to attack a bandit or defend a target from where it starts the intercept. A fighter seeing hot or flank aspect has it; beam is neutral or worse; drag is a disadvantage."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "8-9" }]
  - term: Stern conversion
    definition: "The intercept that ends behind a non-maneuvering bandit: build lateral separation at range, then turn in to arrive in its rear quarter within short-range missile or gun parameters. Also used to join a friendly aircraft."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "10-1, 13-10" }]
  - term: Counterturn
    expansion: CT
    definition: "The turn into the bandit's rear quarter at the end of a stern conversion, ideally from 40,000 feet of lateral separation at 10 NM, flown in pure pursuit."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "13-10" }]
  - term: Displacement turn
    expansion: DT
    definition: "A turn away to create lateral separation when there is too little for the counterturn."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "13-10" }]
  - term: Single side offset
    expansion: SSO
    definition: "Holding the contact a set angle off the nose on the hot side, such as 20 degrees, to stop target aspect growing while closing range efficiently."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "16-6" }]
  - term: LAR
    expansion: Launch acceptability region
    definition: "The three-dimensional volume around a hostile aircraft that a fighter must fly into to have a chance of a successful missile shot. It is largest against a 0 target aspect bandit at high speed and altitude, smallest in the rear quarter low and slow."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "15-1" }]
  - term: RMAX
    expansion: Maximum range
    definition: "The longest range from which an air-to-air missile can be fired and reach its target in the current conditions of closure, aspect and altitude."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "15-1" }]
  - term: RNE
    expansion: No-escape range
    definition: "The range inside which a launched missile has the energy and maneuverability to reach the target whatever it does after launch."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "15-1, 15-2" }]
  - term: RMIN
    expansion: Minimum range
    definition: "The shortest range at which a missile can be launched, acquire the target, be guided and fuze. Generally shorter for infrared missiles than radar missiles."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "15-1, 15-4" }]
  - term: ROPT
    expansion: Optimum range
    definition: "A shot range taught with tactics as a compromise: far enough not to be defensive to the bandit, close enough to leave the missile ample energy at the end."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "15-8" }]
  - term: WEZ
    expansion: Weapon engagement zone
    definition: "In air-to-air use, the volume around a fighter that a hostile aircraft must fly into to employ its weapons: the bandit's equivalent of the fighter's launch zone."
    note: "The joint air defence meaning, the airspace a friendly surface weapon system is responsible for, is in the SEAD terms. [1] [2]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "15-1, 15-2" }]
  - term: Kinematic defeat
    definition: "Defeating a missile by maneuvering so that it runs out of energy, easiest against a shot from maximum range."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "15-8" }]
  - term: Pk enhancing shot
    definition: "A second missile fired at the same bandit before the first times out, to raise the probability of a kill."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "15-8" }]
  - term: Commit criteria
    definition: "The briefed conditions, such as range and aspect, at which fighters leave their station to intercept a group. They follow from the mission: defending an asset needs tighter criteria than an offensive sweep."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "16-5" }]
  - term: Broadcast control
    definition: "Control information for every aircraft on the net, in bullseye format, with the group's track direction rather than its aspect to anyone."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "5-7, 16-1" }]
  - term: Tactical control
    definition: "Control information for one fighter element, in bullseye or BRAA format, with the group's aspect to that element."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "5-7, 16-1" }]
  - term: Declaration
    definition: "The controller's statement of a group's identity, which sets what fighters may do to it: hostile, bandit, bogey or friendly."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "5-8" }]
  - term: Picture label
    definition: "The controller's name for how groups are arranged relative to each other, such as single group, two groups azimuth, two groups range, three groups vic or champagne; each group then gets a name, such as east group."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "16-2, 17-20" }]
  - term: GROUP
    definition: "Radar targets within 3 NM of each other in azimuth and range."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "A-5" }]
  - term: COMMIT
    form: "(TED)"
    definition: "Fighter intent to engage or intercept groups of interest."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "A-3" }]
  - term: DECLARE
    definition: "A request for the identity of a correlated group, track or target."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "A-3" }]
  - term: MELD
    definition: "Bringing every fighter's radar onto the targeted group, with a call giving the group's name, bearing, range and, most important, altitude."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "16-7, 17-26 to 17-27" }]
  - term: SORT
    definition: "A directive to assign responsibility within a group, met visually, by radar or both; each fighter takes its own contact."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "17-26, A-9" }]
  - term: CRANK
    definition: "A turn after a shot that puts the target near the radar's gimbal limits, to stay out of the target's weapons envelope while supporting the missile."
    note: "The Navy training standard is at least 50 degrees angle-off on the cold side. [1]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "16-11, A-3" }]
  - term: TIMEOUT
    definition: "A call that a missile already fired has reached its expected target. It does not mean a kill."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "15-9" }]
  - term: TRASHED
    definition: "A call that a missile in flight has been defeated, by countermeasures, the bandit's maneuvers or radar problems."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "15-9, A-11" }]
  - term: SKATE
    definition: "Launch and leave: support any missile in flight to timeout, then turn away without merging."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "16-12, A-9" }]
  - term: BANZAI
    definition: "Launch and defend: support any missile in flight to timeout, then continue to the merge with the targeted group."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "16-12" }]
  - term: Reset
    definition: "Ending an intercept and returning to the station, handing the group back to the controller to watch, when the bandit has turned away or chasing it wastes fuel."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "16-9, A-8" }]
  - term: Beam
    form: "(ING)"
    definition: "A target maneuvering within 70 to 110 degrees aspect. Turning to put a radar threat on the beam is also a missile defense, because it puts the fighter in the threat radar's Doppler notch."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "16-12 to 16-13, A-1" }]
  - term: Flank
    form: "(ING)"
    definition: "A target with a stable aspect of 30 to 60 degrees (Navy, target aspect)."
    note: "The US Air Force definition is the same geometry as an aspect angle of 120 to 150 degrees. [1]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "A-4" }]
  - term: Drag
    form: "(ING)"
    definition: "A target maneuvering to 120 to 180 degrees target aspect, pointing away (Navy)."
    note: "The US Air Force definition is 0 to 60 degrees, because it counts aspect angle from the target's tail. [1]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "A-3" }]
  - term: Eyeball
    definition: "The fighter with the primary visual identification responsibility; its partner is the shooter."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "17-47, A-4" }]
  - term: Shooter
    definition: "The aircraft or unit designated to employ weapons, such as the fighter that shoots when the eyeball identifies a group as hostile."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "17-47, A-9" }]
  - term: Positive identification
    expansion: PID
    definition: "Identification from visual recognition, electronic support systems, non-cooperative target recognition, IFF or other physics-based techniques, never assumed from location or airspace behaviour alone. Rules of engagement normally require it for a shoot decision against an air threat."
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "III-13" }]
  - term: Procedural identification
    definition: "Identification by whether a track follows airspace control measures: friendly while it follows the rules, hostile if it does not and is not otherwise positively identified. Generally not enough for an engagement decision."
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "III-13 to III-14" }]
  - term: Combat identification
    expansion: CID
    definition: "Characterising detected objects accurately enough to support an engagement decision, using the most positive identification methods available."
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "III-17" }]

## The words of the intercept

These are the words of the fight before the merge: how fighter aircrew describe where a bandit is and where it is going, how they turn that into a collision course or a stern conversion, what a missile's launch zone is, and the calls a section and its controller use from the commit to the decision to merge or leave. Most come from the Navy's intercept training instruction, which also notes where the Air Force counts aspect from the other end of the target. [[1]](#source-1) The identification terms are joint.
