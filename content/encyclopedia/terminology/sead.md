short: SEAD and DEAD
usage: Aircrew of every service who suppress, avoid or destroy surface air defences, and those they protect
terms:
  - term: AOR/JOA-wide suppression
    definition: "SEAD conducted against specific enemy air defence systems throughout the area of responsibility or joint operations area, to degrade or destroy their major capabilities. It targets the high-payoff assets whose loss degrades the whole system most, and is normally a major effort against the whole IADS; destroying key command and control nodes usually has the most disruptive effect."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "IV-13" }]
  - term: Barrage jamming
    definition: "Noise jamming spread over a frequency band much wider than one radar's, often an amplitude-modulated signal covering about 10 percent of the centre frequency. It can cover every radar in that band, but spreads the jammer's power thinner than spot jamming."
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "11.2.2.6" }]
  - term: Burnthrough
    definition: "The range at which the radar echo from a target becomes equal to or greater than the jamming signal, so the target can be seen through the jamming. It happens to every jammed radar at some range, because the echo travels out and back while the jamming travels only one way."
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "11.2.2.4" }]
  - term: DEAD
    expansion: Destruction of enemy air defenses
    definition: "In some contexts, the physical destruction of air defence targets, as opposed to SEAD sorties that discourage the enemy from using air defence radars for fear of losing them."
    note: "Joint doctrine does not define DEAD: it treats destruction as the destructive means of SEAD. [1] [3]"
    cite: [{ n: 3, page: "lead" }]
  - term: Deception jamming
    definition: "Active electronic countermeasures that mimic the radar echo, so the radar responds as if it were seeing another aircraft or ship and measures a false range or bearing. Usually done with repeaters or transponders; it needs much less power than noise jamming."
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "11.2.2.8" }]
  - term: Destructive means
    definition: "SEAD that seeks to destroy the target system or its operating personnel."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "IV-13" }]
  - term: Disruptive means
    definition: "SEAD that temporarily denies, degrades, deceives, delays or neutralizes enemy surface air defences. Active means include electronic attack, jamming, deception, chaff, flares, decoys and tactics such as avoidance; passive means include emission control, camouflage, warning receivers and stealth."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "IV-13" }]
  - term: FEZ
    expansion: Fighter engagement zone
    definition: "In air defense, airspace of defined dimensions within which the responsibility for engagement of air threats normally rests with fighter aircraft."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "GL-11" }]
  - term: Frequency agility
    definition: "The ability of a single radar to change frequency to counter a spot jammer."
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "11.2.2.6" }]
  - term: HIMEZ
    expansion: High-altitude missile engagement zone
    definition: "In air defense, airspace of defined dimensions within which the responsibility for engagement of air threats normally rests with high-altitude surface-to-air missiles."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "GL-12" }]
  - term: HOJ
    expansion: Home-on-jam
    definition: "A guidance submode of nearly all radio-frequency homing weapons, in which the weapon homes on the jamming signal itself."
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "11.2.2.7" }]
  - term: IADS
    expansion: Integrated air defense system
    definition: "An enemy's air defences tied together into one network. SEAD targets its command and control nodes, SAM sites, anti-aircraft artillery, early-warning, fire-control and ground-controlled intercept radars, SAM carriers and storage, and its operators and maintainers."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "IV-12" }]
  - term: JRFL
    expansion: Joint restricted frequency list
    definition: "The list of friendly frequencies that should not be disrupted, because of friendly use or exploitation. It is updated constantly; during intense SEAD operations friendly forces have been attacked because their emitters were not recognized as friendly."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-24" }]
  - term: Localized suppression
    definition: "SEAD confined to the areas of specific targets or transit routes for a specific time. SEAD aircraft normally escort other aircraft to protect them from surface air defences in that sector. It may be planned or immediate."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "IV-13 to IV-14" }]
  - term: LOMEZ
    expansion: Low-altitude missile engagement zone
    definition: "In air defense, airspace of defined dimensions within which the responsibility for engagement of air threats normally rests with low- to medium-altitude surface-to-air missiles."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "GL-14" }]
  - term: MEZ
    expansion: Missile engagement zone
    definition: "In air defense, airspace of defined dimensions within which the responsibility for engagement of air threats normally rests with surface-to-air missile systems. It can include one or more HIMEZs, LOMEZs and SHORADEZs."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "V-11, GL-14" }]
  - term: Noise jamming
    definition: "Saturating a radar receiver with a continuous random signal so the echo is hidden. It needs a large average power, and the radar operator sees a strobe at the jammer's bearing: the jammer hides its range but shows its direction and presence."
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "11.2.2.5" }]
  - term: Opportune suppression
    definition: "Unplanned SEAD, including aircrew self-defense and attacks on surface air defence targets of opportunity. Any movement by air defence systems from targeted locations turns localized suppression into opportune suppression."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "IV-14" }]
  - term: Radar horizon
    definition: "The distance beyond which the earth's curve hides a low target from a radar. Allowing for normal refraction, it is calculated with an effective earth radius 4/3 of the real one; a radar at 1 mile altitude has a horizon of about 102 miles, and one on a 75 ft mast about 12."
    cite: [{ n: 4, page: "Definition" }]
  - term: SFJ
    expansion: Stand-forward jammer
    definition: "A jammer placed between the enemy sensors and the attacking aircraft. It uses its power most efficiently, but is a prime target, well within reach of home-on-jam and anti-radiation weapons."
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "11.2.2.7" }]
  - term: SHORADEZ
    expansion: Short-range air defense engagement zone
    definition: "In air defense, airspace of defined dimensions within which the responsibility for engagement of air threats normally rests with short-range air defense weapons. It may be established within a low- or high-altitude missile engagement zone."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "GL-16" }]
  - term: SOJ
    expansion: Stand-off jammer
    definition: "A jamming aircraft that stays just outside the range of the enemy's weapons, screening the attackers that penetrate. It is safe from home-on-jam weapons, but the attackers reach burnthrough sooner than with a self-screening jammer."
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "11.2.2.7" }]
  - term: Spot jamming
    definition: "Noise jamming with all the jammer's power in a narrow band, ideally matched to one radar's frequency. It is aimed at a specific radar and needs a receiver to find that radar's frequency."
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "11.2.2.6" }]
  - term: SSJ
    expansion: Self-screening jammer
    definition: "A jammer carried by an aircraft for its own protection. Jammer, target and victim radar are always in line, but the jammer takes weight and space from sensors, weapons and fuel."
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "11.2.2.7" }]
  - term: Sweep jamming
    definition: "Noise jamming with a frequency-modulated signal swept back and forth over a very wide band, covering many radars at once."
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "11.2.2.6" }]
  - term: Terrain masking
    definition: "Using terrain to block the line of sight between a radar and an aircraft, so the radar cannot detect it. Aircraft may use terrain or radar horizon shielding, and the reduced radar performance against low-elevation targets, to evade detection."
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "11.2.3.4" }]
  - term: WEZ
    expansion: Weapon engagement zone
    definition: "In air defense, airspace of defined dimensions within which the responsibility for engagement of air threats normally rests with a particular weapon system."
    note: "Joint doctrine defines it for friendly air defence planning. Sim manuals and tactics discussions use the same words for the airspace an enemy system can engage. [1]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "V-10, GL-18" }]
  - term: Wild Weasel
    definition: "The US Air Force name for aircraft tasked with SEAD missions using anti-radiation missiles, from the F-100F of the Vietnam War to the F-4G; the name is also used for SEAD operations generally, which the US first called Iron Hand."
    cite: [{ n: 3, page: "lead, Wild Weasel" }]

## The words of air defence suppression

These are the words for what a SEAD mission does and the air defences it faces: the categories and means of suppression from the joint counterair publication, the engagement zones an air defence is organised into, and the jamming terms a strike package lives by. The joint publication applies to all four US services; the jamming terms come from the Naval Academy's weapons systems text. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2)

SEAD itself, ARM, electronic attack and early warning are in the joint terms; chaff and decoy are in the weapons terms; MAGNUM is in the brevity words. The Manual's SEAD and DEAD module uses all of them.
