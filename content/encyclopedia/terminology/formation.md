---
short: Formation
usage: Pilots of every service flying as a section, division, display team or helicopter flight
terms:
  - term: Parade
    definition: The close, fixed formation position, held by lining up checkpoint pairs on the lead rather than by a number. An administrative formation for airshows and the break, with little flexibility; the wingman asks before crossing under.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "8" }, { n: 3, page: "Formation Types" }]
  - term: Cruise
    definition: An administrative formation in which the wingman moves between the 30° and 45° bearings on either side of lead, using radius of turn, up to about 0.2 NM in the T-44C. Used through controlled airspace to keep the formation tight and away from other aircraft.
    cite: [{ n: 3, page: "Formation Types" }]
  - term: Combat spread
    definition: A tactical formation with the wingman near abeam the lead, for the most lookout forward. In the T-44C it is within 10° of abeam, 0.3–2.0 NM away depending on conditions, and tactical formation maneuvers are called from it; in Army helicopter teams lead calls it with a side and the wingman moves toward lead's 3 or 9 o'clock.
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "Combat Spread" }, { n: 5, page: "230" }]
  - term: Step-down
    definition: The wingman's height below the lead in parade, the first checkpoint corrected (the A of A-B-C). On the T-6B, set with the lead's inboard exhaust stack fully visible and tangent to the bottom leading edge of the wing.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "8" }]
  - term: Bearing line
    definition: The line, angled aft from the lead's wingline, along which the wingman sits in parade; the second checkpoint corrected (B). On the T-6B, set with the lower UHF antenna over the inboard aileron cutout on the opposite wing.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "8" }]
  - term: Acute
    definition: Too far forward of the parade position, ahead of the bearing line. In the T-44C it shows as a gap between the lead's horizontal stabilizer and UHF antenna, with the vertical stabilizer outside the star. The opposite of sucked.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "8" }, { n: 3, page: "Starboard Parade: Acute" }]
  - term: Sucked
    definition: Too far aft of the parade position, behind the bearing line. In the T-44C the lead's horizontal stabilizer moves ahead of and covers the UHF antenna, with the vertical stabilizer inside the star. The opposite of acute.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "2, 8" }, { n: 3, page: "Starboard Parade: Sucked" }]
  - term: Running rendezvous
    definition: The join-up after an interval takeoff, with the lead holding a set power, speed and bank limit while the wingman closes with a limit on closure.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "3, 8" }]
  - term: Breakup and rendezvous
    expansion: B&R
    definition: Separating the section and joining it again. The wingman breaks as the lead approaches the 3/9 line abeam the wing's leading edge, then rejoins by radius of turn rather than by power, with both aircraft at the same speed.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "4" }]
  - term: Underrun
    definition: Passing under and ahead of the lead with too much closure during a rendezvous. The recovery is lower, level, idle, call - increase step-down, keep sight, level the wings, power to idle, transmit "underrunning" - then join in the assigned position, normally on the outside of the turn.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "4, 9" }, { n: 3, page: "RTB / Safety Procedures" }]
  - term: Crossunder
    definition: The wingman moving from one side of the lead to the other by stepping down and passing under the lead. Common errors are getting too far in trail and stopping the cross too soon.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "9" }, { n: 3, page: "Crossunder" }]
  - term: Lost sight
    definition: A wingman losing sight of the lead in cloud. Only the wingman can lose sight, since the lead is on instruments. The wingman goes to instruments, calls "lost sight" with altitude and reduces power about 10%, all nearly at once.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "7" }]
  - term: NORDO
    definition: An aircraft without radio communications. In formation it pats the oxygen mask ("can't talk") or the helmet ear ("can't hear"), then gives a thumbs down.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "6" }]
  - term: Formation lights
    definition: Exterior lights that show the shape of an aircraft to its formation at night. The DCS F/A-18C guide lists eight, on the wingtips, the outboard sides of the fins, the aft fuselage below the fins and the forward fuselage ahead of the leading-edge extensions.
    cite: [{ n: 4, page: "61" }]
  - term: Tac turn
    definition: A tactical turn of 60-120° from combat spread. The aircraft on the outside of the turn turns first; the inside aircraft turns when the other's nose points at it.
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "Tac-Turns" }]
  - term: Shackle
    definition: Both aircraft turn about 30-45° toward each other and cross, changing sides; used to let a sucked or wide wingman regain position. The aircraft with the most awareness calls "Resume".
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "Shackle" }]
  - term: Pump
    definition: A 90° turn by the flight to stop travel toward a threat, obstacle or weather at once. The wingman ends in trail and stays there until another pump.
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "Pump" }]
  - term: Hook turn
    definition: A 120-240° turn by both aircraft in the same direction at once; they change sides, and the aircraft on the inside loses sight of the other until the rollout.
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "Hook Turn" }]
  - term: Chalk
    definition: "An aircraft's numbered place in a helicopter flight: chalk 1 is lead, then chalk 2, 3 and so on. When lead leaves the flight, chalk 2 becomes lead, chalk 3 becomes chalk 2, and so on."
    cite: [{ n: 5, page: "212, 232" }]
  - term: Air mission commander
    expansion: AMC
    definition: "The officer in charge of all of a flight's resources, responsible for planning, organizing and briefing the mission, delegating tasks, flight integrity and discipline, and the debrief. A designated alternate takes over if the AMC must leave the flight."
    cite: [{ n: 5, page: "211-212" }]
  - term: Tactical lead
    definition: "Whoever, in any aircraft or position, first sees a threat or obstacle. The tactical lead announces it and calls the maneuver to engage, suppress or bypass it; flight lead takes control back when the threat is bypassed or neutralized."
    cite: [{ n: 5, page: "212" }]
  - term: Fixed formation
    definition: "A helicopter formation used when more control is needed: the flight acts as one aircraft and mirrors lead's movements. Staggered, echelon, trail and V are fixed formations, used for departures and arrivals, administrative transits and when tactical spacing is not possible."
    cite: [{ n: 5, page: "224" }]
  - term: Maneuvering formation
    definition: "A helicopter formation for tactical situations, with wider spacing and overlapping fields of view and fire; each wingman holds an area rather than a point and keeps its own clearance while lead maneuvers freely. Combat cruise, combat cruise left or right, combat trail and combat spread."
    cite: [{ n: 5, page: "227" }]
  - term: Staggered formation
    definition: "A fixed helicopter formation, staggered left or right: each aircraft about 45° astern of the one ahead, alternating sides, so chalk 3 and the other odd chalks fly in trail behind lead. Chalk 2's side names it."
    cite: [{ n: 5, page: "224" }]
  - term: Echelon formation
    definition: "A formation with every wingman on the same side of lead, echelon left or right. In a fixed-wing four-ship each flies the bearing line of the aircraft ahead; it is used in the traffic pattern and for pitchouts, and lead can turn only away from the flight. In helicopters each wingman sits about 30-45° off lead's 6 o'clock, suited to dust, sand and snow takeoffs and landings, but it severely limits maneuvering."
    cite: [{ n: 7, page: "10" }, { n: 5, page: "225" }]
  - term: Trail formation
    definition: "A formation with each aircraft following the one ahead. Fixed-wing flights fly close trail, extended trail or tail chase. In helicopters each aircraft stays within 10° of the track of the one ahead: the hardest fixed formation, used for takeoffs, landings and changing formation but not for long, and a poor choice in dust, sand or snow."
    cite: [{ n: 7, page: "10" }, { n: 5, page: "226" }]
  - term: V-formation
    definition: "A fixed helicopter formation of a leader and two wingmen, each about 45° astern of the leader, one left and one right. It needs a relatively small landing area."
    cite: [{ n: 5, page: "226-227" }]
  - term: Combat cruise
    definition: "The basic helicopter team formation, which replaced the term free cruise: the wingman may fly anywhere from about lead's 3 o'clock round the back to 9 o'clock, best at 45°, and lead maneuvers freely. Combat cruise left or right keeps the wingman on one side, from directly behind to abeam."
    cite: [{ n: 5, page: "228-229" }]
  - term: Combat trail
    definition: "A helicopter maneuvering formation that holds each wingman within 30° either side of the 6 o'clock of the aircraft ahead, for narrow terrain and narrow landing zones; not for long periods or at night."
    cite: [{ n: 5, page: "229-230" }]
  - term: Stepped-up
    definition: "Vertical separation in a helicopter formation with each aircraft 1-10 ft higher than the one ahead. Its opposite, stepped-down, can put trailing aircraft in the wake and costs them power."
    cite: [{ n: 5, page: "215" }]
  - term: Accordion effect
    definition: "Abrupt altitude or airspeed changes by the lead, copied ever more abruptly down a formation, so that aircraft at the back close dangerously fast."
    cite: [{ n: 5, page: "217" }]
  - term: Inadvertent IMC breakup
    definition: "A formation's briefed plan for flying into cloud. In the Army circular's suggestion for a staggered formation, lead flies straight ahead and each chalk turns 10° times its chalk number away from the flight and climbs 500 ft above the chalk ahead, holding it for at least 30 seconds."
    cite: [{ n: 5, page: "220-221" }]
  - term: LITECON
    definition: "Army. A numbered lighting condition for multi-aircraft flight, setting the anti-collision, position, formation and covert IR lights; the circular's sample runs from 1, FAA day, to 5, total blackout."
    cite: [{ n: 5, page: "216" }]
  - term: Division
    definition: "Three or four aircraft flying as one maneuver element, usually two sections. Five or more aircraft, or two or more maneuver elements, make a flight."
    cite: [{ n: 3, page: "Formation Description" }]
  - term: Element
    definition: "A leader and a wingman, never more than two aircraft; every flight is built of elements, so a four-ship has two. The element stays together: #4 flies on #3 and moves with it. Navy and Marine Corps aviators call it a section."
    cite: [{ n: 7, page: "8" }, { n: 3, page: "Formation Description" }]
  - term: Deputy lead
    definition: "#3 in a four-ship: the second element's leader, who should have lead experience and preferably be lead qualified."
    cite: [{ n: 7, page: "8" }]
  - term: Fingertip
    definition: "The standard close four-ship configuration, named for the fingertips of a hand seen from above: #2 on one side of lead, #3 on the other with #4 outboard of it. Strong right or left is the side the second element is on. Every configuration change starts from fingertip."
    cite: [{ n: 7, page: "8-9" }]
  - term: Diamond
    definition: "A four-ship with #4 in the slot behind lead, on the bearing lines of #2 and #3: compact, symmetrical and maneuverable, and the building block of mass display formations."
    cite: [{ n: 7, page: "11, 29" }]
  - term: Slot
    definition: "The position directly behind and below lead in a diamond, flown by #4 with close-trail references."
    cite: [{ n: 7, page: "11, 29" }]
  - term: Route position
    definition: "A relaxed formation position, at least two to four ship widths and no more than 500 ft from lead, between the 3/9 line and the bearing line. Used for frequency changes, in-flight checks and long legs; the farther out, the closer to the 3/9 line."
    cite: [{ n: 7, page: "12" }]
  - term: Phantom wingman
    definition: "An imaginary aircraft that lets an odd-numbered flight fly a four-ship's positions and signals: a three-ship vic usually flies with a phantom #4."
    cite: [{ n: 7, page: "9, 13" }]
  - term: Division wall
    definition: "Four aircraft line abreast in combat spread: the section leads 0.8-1.0 nm apart in the centre, the wingmen the same distance outside them, with a briefed altitude plan. For simultaneous attacks with the most visual mutual support."
    cite: [{ n: 6, page: "7-1" }]
  - term: Fluid four
    definition: "A division with the section leads in combat spread and each wingman in tac wing on its leader. Used for transits and holding: an intermediate step between fingertip and a tactical formation such as division wall."
    cite: [{ n: 6, page: "7-2" }, { n: 3, page: "Fluid Four" }]
  - term: Tac wing
    definition: "A loose wingman position on lead's cruise bearing line or aft of it, 0.2-0.6 nm away and no lower than 1,000 ft below lead, where the wingman flows inside and outside turns using geometry rather than throttle."
    cite: [{ n: 6, page: "6-9" }]
  - term: In-place turn
    definition: "A tactical turn of 180° in which every aircraft turns at the same moment and the formation rolls out on the reciprocal with the aircraft on swapped sides. Used to engage a threat in the rear quarter or to leave a target area."
    cite: [{ n: 6, page: "5-8, 7-5" }]
  - term: Pitchout
    definition: "Breaking a flight up from echelon in sequence, lead first at 45-60° of bank and each wingman at a set interval in seconds, for landing spacing, rejoin practice or extended trail."
    cite: [{ n: 7, page: "37" }]
  - term: Display team
    definition: "A single aircraft, formation or group flying as one display act throughout; counted as one display item. Under UK military rules, a high-energy display team of three or more aircraft makes a display at least Tier 2."
    cite: [{ n: 8, page: "3, 7" }]
  - term: Flypast
    definition: "Aircraft flying singly or in formation over or past spectators along a pre-planned route without maneuvering, except for navigation and repositioning: no aerobatics and up to three planned passes (UK military definition)."
    cite: [{ n: 8, page: "3" }]
  - term: Minimum separation distance
    definition: "MSD: the least separation, in feet, a displaying aircraft keeps. UK military rules set it by aircraft type, close formation or not, and maneuver; for example 300 ft for a fixed-wing close formation in aerobatics, and 500 ft for aerobatics outside the display area."
    cite: [{ n: 8, page: "2, 21-22" }]
  - term: Crowd line
    definition: "The line marking the nearest edge of any area open to spectators, from which display lateral separation is measured: 230 m normally in UK military rules, 450 m for aircraft above 300 KIAS heading toward spectators."
    cite: [{ n: 8, page: "2, 22-23" }]

## The words of formation flying

These are the words of two aircraft flying as one: the formations, the parade checkpoints, the rendezvous and its recovery, losing sight, and the tactical turns. They also cover helicopter flights: the crew roles, the fixed and maneuvering formations, spacing and the breakups; and four-ship and display formations. They come from the Navy training command's T-6B and T-44C formation material [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2), where type numbers are examples from those aircraft, for helicopter flights, the Army's fundamentals of flight circular [[5]](#source-5), and for four-ships and displays the Navy's T-45 tactical formation instruction [[6]](#source-6), a civilian formation guide [[7]](#source-7) and the UK military display regulation. [[8]](#source-8)

BLIND, VISUAL, JOKER, BINGO and PUSH are in the brevity words, and the 3/9 line and lag pursuit in the air combat terms.
