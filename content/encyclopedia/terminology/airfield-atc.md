short: Airfield and ATC
usage: Military and civil air traffic control in the US
terms:
  - term: Abeam
    definition: An aircraft is “abeam” a fix, point, or object when that fix, point, or object is approximately 90 degrees to the right or left of the aircraft track. Abeam indicates a general position rather than a precise point.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "A" }]
  - term: Affirmative
    definition: "Yes."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "A" }]
  - term: Air taxi
    definition: Used to describe a helicopter/VTOL aircraft movement conducted above the surface but normally not above 100 feet AGL. The aircraft may proceed either via hover taxi or flight at speeds more than 20 knots. The pilot is solely responsible for selecting a safe airspeed/altitude for the operation being conducted.
    usage: Helicopters and VTOL aircraft
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "A" }]
  - term: ATIS
    expansion: Automatic terminal information service
    definition: The continuous broadcast of recorded noncontrol information in selected terminal areas. Its purpose is to improve controller effectiveness and to relieve frequency congestion by automating the repetitive transmission of essential but routine information.
    note: "ICAO defines it more generally, as the provision of current, routine information to arriving and departing aircraft by means of continuous and repetitive broadcasts throughout the day or a specified portion of the day. [1]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "A" }]
  - term: Base leg
    definition: A flight path at right angles to the landing runway off its approach end. The base leg normally extends from the downwind leg to the intersection of the extended runway centerline.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "T" }, { n: 2, page: "9-11" }]
  - term: Break point
    definition: "In the overhead approach pattern, a point, normally just above the landing threshold, where the aircraft is turned 180°, left or right depending on the direction of traffic flow, so as to be on the downwind leg."
    usage: Military overhead pattern
    note: "Controllers may ask a pilot to report the break, and name the break point if it is nonstandard. [2] The brevity word BREAK is something else: an immediate maximum-performance turn. [4]"
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "9-13" }]
  - term: Ceiling
    definition: The heights above the earth's surface of the lowest layer of clouds or obscuring phenomena that is reported as “broken,” “overcast,” or “obscuration,” and not classified as “thin” or “partial.”
    note: "ICAO's ceiling is the height above the ground or water of the base of the lowest layer of cloud below 6,000 meters (20,000 feet) covering more than half the sky. [1]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "C" }]
  - term: Cleared for the option
    definition: ATC authorization for an aircraft to make a touch-and-go, low approach, missed approach, stop and go, or full stop landing at the discretion of the pilot. It is normally used in training so that an instructor can evaluate a student's performance under changing situations.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "C" }]
  - term: Crosswind leg
    definition: A flight path at right angles to the landing runway off its upwind end.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "T" }, { n: 2, page: "9-11" }]
  - term: Downwind leg
    definition: A flight path parallel to the landing runway in the direction opposite to landing. The downwind leg normally extends between the crosswind leg and the base leg.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "T" }, { n: 2, page: "9-11" }]
  - term: Final approach
    definition: A flight path in the direction of landing along the extended runway centerline. The final approach normally extends from the base leg to the runway. An aircraft making a straight-in approach VFR is also considered to be on final approach.
    note: "The Navy's controller manual adds that every arriving aircraft, whatever its type or approach, flies at least this one common component of the standard traffic pattern. [2]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "T" }, { n: 2, page: "9-14" }]
  - term: Flight level
    definition: A level of constant atmospheric pressure related to a reference datum of 29.92 inches of mercury. Each is stated in three digits that represent hundreds of feet; flight level (FL) 250 represents a barometric altimeter indication of 25,000 feet.
    note: "ICAO states the same standard datum in hectopascals: 1013.2 hPa. [1]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "F" }]
  - term: Go around
    definition: "Instructions for a pilot to abandon his/her approach to landing. Unless otherwise advised by ATC, a VFR aircraft or an aircraft conducting visual approach should overfly the runway while climbing to traffic pattern altitude and enter the traffic pattern via the crosswind leg. A pilot on an IFR flight plan making an instrument approach should execute the published missed approach procedure or proceed as instructed by ATC."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "G" }]
  - term: Hover taxi
    definition: Used to describe a helicopter/VTOL aircraft movement conducted above the surface and in ground effect at airspeeds less than approximately 20 knots. The actual height may vary, and some helicopters may require hover taxi above 25 feet AGL to reduce ground effect turbulence or provide clearance for cargo slingloads.
    usage: Helicopters and VTOL aircraft
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "H" }]
  - term: IFR
    expansion: Instrument flight rules
    definition: Rules governing the procedures for conducting instrument flight. Also a term used by pilots and controllers to indicate type of flight plan.
    note: "ICAO's definition is a set of rules governing the conduct of flight under instrument meteorological conditions. [1]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "I" }]
  - term: Initial
    definition: "The initial approach of the overhead pattern: a line of flight along the extended centerline of the landing runway, 3 to 5 miles long, normally flown 500 feet above the standard traffic pattern altitude."
    usage: Military overhead pattern
    note: "Controllers may ask the pilot to report the initial. [2] The FAA glossary's overhead maneuver gives the same 3 to 5 miles and at least 500 feet above the conventional pattern. [1]"
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "9-13" }]
  - term: Low approach
    definition: An approach over an airport or runway following an instrument approach or a VFR approach including the go-around maneuver where the pilot intentionally does not make contact with the runway.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "L" }]
  - term: MARSA
    expansion: Military authority assumes responsibility for separation of aircraft
    definition: A condition whereby the military services involved assume responsibility for separation between participating military aircraft in the ATC system. It is used only for required IFR operations which are specified in letters of agreement or other appropriate FAA or military documents.
    usage: Military aircraft in the US ATC system
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "M" }]
  - term: Mayday
    definition: The international radiotelephony distress signal. When repeated three times, it indicates imminent and grave danger and that immediate assistance is requested.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "M" }]
  - term: Military training routes
    definition: Airspace of defined vertical and lateral dimensions established for the conduct of military flight training at airspeeds in excess of 250 knots IAS.
    note: "IFR routes (IR) and VFR routes (VR) are flown below 10,000 feet MSL. [1]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "M" }]
  - term: Minimum fuel
    definition: Indicates that an aircraft's fuel supply has reached a state where, upon reaching the destination, it can accept little or no delay. This is not an emergency situation but merely indicates an emergency situation is possible should any undue delay occur.
    note: "Not the same as the brevity words JOKER and BINGO, which are pre-briefed fuel states for a mission. [4]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "M" }]
  - term: NOTAM
    expansion: Notice to airmen
    definition: A notice containing information (not known sufficiently in advance to publicize by other means) concerning the establishment, condition, or change in any component (facility, service, or procedure of, or hazard in the National Airspace System) the timely knowledge of which is essential to personnel concerned with flight operations.
    note: "ICAO's definition is not limited to one country's airspace: a change in any aeronautical facility, service, procedure or hazard. [1]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "N" }]
  - term: Out
    definition: The conversation is ended and no response is expected.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "O" }]
  - term: Over
    definition: My transmission is ended; I expect a response.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "O" }]
  - term: Overhead maneuver
    definition: "A series of predetermined maneuvers prescribed for aircraft (often in formation) for entry into the visual flight rules (VFR) traffic pattern and to proceed to a landing. It usually specifies an initial approach 3 to 5 miles long, an elliptical pattern of two 180-degree turns starting at a break point, an altitude at least 500 feet above the conventional pattern, and a roll-out on final not less than 1/4 mile from the threshold and not less than 300 feet above the ground."
    usage: Military overhead pattern
    note: "The Navy calls it the overhead approach pattern, made of the initial approach, break point, “the 180” and final approach, and uses it so that fighters and some trainers can join the terminal area alongside slower aircraft flying the standard pattern. [2] An aircraft flying it is VFR; an IFR flight plan is canceled at the initial point. [1]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "O" }, { n: 2, page: "9-13" }]
  - term: Pan-pan
    definition: The international radio-telephony urgency signal. When repeated three times, indicates uncertainty or alert followed by the nature of the urgency.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "P" }]
  - term: PAR
    expansion: Precision approach radar
    definition: Radar equipment in some ATC facilities operated by the FAA and/or the military services at joint-use civil/military locations and separate military installations to detect and display azimuth, elevation, and range of aircraft on the final approach course to a runway. It is primarily used to conduct a precision instrument approach wherein the controller issues guidance instructions to the pilot.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "P" }]
  - term: Roger
    definition: I have received all of your last transmission. It should not be used to answer a question requiring a yes or a no answer.
    note: "The multi-service brevity word ROGER adds that it does not indicate compliance or reaction. [4]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "R" }]
  - term: Say again
    definition: Used to request a repeat of the last transmission. Usually specifies transmission or portion thereof not understood or received.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "S" }]
  - term: Squawk
    form: "(mode, code, function)"
    definition: Used by ATC to instruct a pilot to activate the aircraft transponder and ADS-B Out with altitude reporting enabled, or (military) to activate only specific modes, codes, or functions.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "S" }]
  - term: Stop and go
    definition: A procedure wherein an aircraft will land, make a complete stop on the runway, and then commence a takeoff from that point.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "S" }]
  - term: TACAN
    expansion: Tactical air navigation
    definition: An ultra-high frequency electronic rho-theta air navigation aid which provides suitably equipped aircraft a continuous indication of bearing and distance to the TACAN station.
    note: "The Navy's controller manual adds that TACAN has 126 two-way channels, that its distance element is DME, and that its compact ground equipment can be transported and set up more easily than a VOR. [3]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "T" }, { n: 3, page: "2-28" }]
  - term: The 180
    definition: "In the overhead approach pattern, the second 180° turn, made after the downwind leg to establish the aircraft on final approach. The aircraft stays at traffic pattern altitude until the turn is started."
    usage: Military overhead pattern
    note: "Navy carrier training uses the same name for the point from which the LSO controls the aircraft to touchdown; see the carrier terms. [5]"
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "9-14" }]
  - term: Touch-and-go
    definition: An operation by an aircraft that lands and departs on a runway without stopping or exiting the runway.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "T" }]
  - term: Traffic pattern
    definition: The traffic flow that is prescribed for aircraft landing at, taxiing on, or taking off from an airport. The components of a typical traffic pattern are departure, upwind leg, crosswind leg, downwind leg, base leg, and final approach.
    note: "ICAO calls it the aerodrome traffic circuit: the specified path to be flown by aircraft operating in the vicinity of an aerodrome. [1] Navy airfields normally fly left-hand traffic, with right-hand traffic when required. [2]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "T, A" }, { n: 2, page: "9-11" }]
  - term: Upwind leg
    definition: A flight path that begins after departure and continues straight ahead along the extended runway centerline. Upwind leg is an extension of departure and is used when issuing control instructions for separation, spacing or sequencing.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "T" }, { n: 2, page: "9-11" }]
  - term: VFR
    expansion: Visual flight rules
    definition: Rules that govern the procedures for conducting flight under visual conditions. The term “VFR” is also used in the United States to indicate weather conditions that are equal to or greater than minimum VFR requirements. In addition, it is used by pilots and controllers to indicate type of flight plan.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "V" }]
  - term: Wilco
    definition: I have received your message, understand it, and will comply with it.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "W" }]

## Words of the airfield

These are the words of the airfield: the traffic pattern, the military overhead pattern and the standard radio words pilots and controllers use on the frequency. Most come from the FAA's Pilot/Controller Glossary, which compiles the terms used in the US air traffic control system; a few, such as MARSA, apply only to military aircraft. [[1]](#source-1)

The overhead pattern is military. Its parts, the initial, the break point, the 180 and final approach, are defined from the Navy's air traffic controller manual, which explains that it lets fighters and some trainers enter the terminal area alongside slower aircraft flying the standard pattern. [[2]](#source-2) Helicopter movements such as hover taxi and air taxi are defined for helicopters and VTOL aircraft of any service. [[1]](#source-1)

**Other countries.** Because flying is international, the FAA glossary also prints the ICAO definition of a term wherever it differs from the FAA's. [[1]](#source-1) Those differences are quoted in the notes here: ceiling, flight level, ATIS, NOTAM, IFR and the traffic pattern, which ICAO calls the aerodrome traffic circuit.
