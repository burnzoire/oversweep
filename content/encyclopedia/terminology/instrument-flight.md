short: Instrument flight
usage: Pilots of every service who fly in cloud
terms:
  - term: Control and performance concept
    definition: Attitude instrument flying by setting an attitude and power on the control instruments (attitude indicator and power gauges), trimming, then checking the result on the performance instruments (altimeter, airspeed, vertical velocity, heading, angle of attack, turn and slip) and correcting attitude and power if the result is off.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "12–13" }]
  - term: Crosscheck
    definition: The division of attention between the control and performance instruments, reading them and correcting what is off. It runs like a wheel, with the attitude indicator as the hub and the other instruments as spokes, out and back to the hub each time.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "14" }]
  - term: Unusual attitude
    definition: An aircraft attitude entered inadvertently, from turbulence, channelized attention, instrument failure, inattention, spatial disorientation, a lost wingman or going from visual to instrument conditions.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "26" }]
  - term: Spatial disorientation
    definition: Mistaking the aircraft's attitude or motion because of illusions of motion and position created by complex motions, forces and certain visual scenes. It can be prevented only by visual reference to reliable, fixed points on the ground or to the flight instruments.
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "¶8-1-5" }]
  - term: The leans
    definition: The illusion of banking the opposite way after an abrupt roll out of a bank that was entered too slowly for the inner ear to sense. The pilot rolls back into the original bank, or leans toward the false vertical.
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "¶8-1-5" }]
  - term: Coriolis illusion
    definition: The illusion of rotating in a different axis after an abrupt head movement during a prolonged constant-rate turn. The most overwhelming illusion in flight; avoided by not moving the head suddenly in long turns under IFR.
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "¶8-1-5" }]
  - term: Graveyard spiral
    definition: In a prolonged coordinated turn the inner ear stops sensing the turn; seeing altitude lost, the pilot thinks the wings are level in a descent and pulls back, tightening the spiral and losing more altitude.
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "¶8-1-5" }]
  - term: Somatogravic illusion
    definition: Rapid acceleration, as on takeoff, feels like a nose-up attitude and the pilot pushes nose low; rapid deceleration has the opposite effect, toward a nose-up or stalled attitude.
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "¶8-1-5" }]
  - term: Holding pattern
    definition: A racetrack flown at a fix while awaiting further clearance, inbound on the holding course to the fix, then 180° turns. Standard turns are to the right and nonstandard to the left; the inbound leg is 1 minute at or below 14,000 ft MSL and 1½ minutes above.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "151" }]
  - term: EFC
    expansion: Expect further clearance time
    definition: The time ATC gives a holding aircraft as its best estimate of when it will be cleared on. Ask for one whenever told to hold without it.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "152" }]
  - term: Penetration turn
    definition: The turn back to the inbound course during a high-altitude teardrop approach, flown in the published direction, normally at 30° of bank. If no turn altitude is published, start it after descending half the height between the initial and final approach fix altitudes.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "171" }]
  - term: Final approach fix
    expansion: FAF
    definition: Where the final approach begins, shown as a Maltese cross in the profile view with its crossing altitude. Normally crossed at approach speed in the landing configuration.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "206" }]
  - term: Stepdown fix
    definition: A fix between the final approach fix and the missed approach point on some non-precision approaches, normally to clear an obstacle inside the final approach fix. Do not descend below its altitude until it is identified.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "206" }]
  - term: Visual descent point
    expansion: VDP
    definition: A point on a non-precision straight-in final from which a normal descent of about 3° from the minimum descent altitude to touchdown can begin, if the runway environment is in sight. Shown as a "V" in the profile view.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "206–207" }]
  - term: Minimum descent altitude
    expansion: MDA
    definition: The lowest altitude on a non-precision approach. Stay at or above it until the runway environment is in sight and you are in position to land, or go missed at the missed approach point.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "206, 209" }]
  - term: Decision altitude
    expansion: DA, or decision height (DH)
    definition: The altitude on a precision approach at which the missed approach is flown if the runway environment is not in sight; a precision approach has no separate missed approach point.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "208, 249" }]
  - term: Missed approach point
    expansion: MAP
    definition: The point on a non-precision approach at which a missed approach is flown if the runway environment is not in sight or a safe landing cannot be made. For a straight-in approach it is no farther from the final approach fix than the threshold.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "208, 249" }]
  - term: Touchdown zone elevation
    expansion: TDZE
    definition: The highest point in the first 3,000 ft of the landing surface. Field elevation is the highest point of any usable landing surface.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "105" }]
  - term: Minimum safe altitude
    definition: An emergency altitude with at least 1,000 ft of obstacle clearance around the facility an approach is based on. A minimum sector altitude covers 25 NM; an emergency safe altitude, normally published only for military procedures, covers 100 NM, with 2,000 ft of clearance in designated mountainous areas. Neither guarantees navigation aid reception.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "105" }]
  - term: ASR
    expansion: Airport surveillance radar approach
    definition: A radar approach giving course and range but no glidepath, so a non-precision approach flown to a minimum descent altitude. On request the controller gives recommended altitudes each mile on final.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "220–221" }]
  - term: No-gyro approach
    definition: A radar approach for an aircraft whose heading indicator has failed. The controller says "turn right", "turn left" and "stop turn"; turns are about standard rate (not over 30° of bank) until the controller calls for half-standard rate on final.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "222" }]
  - term: Diverse departure
    definition: A departure from a runway where no obstacle penetrates a 40:1 surface from the departure end, so an aircraft climbing at 200 ft per nautical mile may turn any direction, and no obstacle departure procedure is needed.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "128" }]

## The words of instrument flight

These are the words of flying without looking outside: how the instruments are grouped and scanned, the illusions that make a pilot doubt them, holding, and the fixes and altitudes of an instrument approach. They come from the Air Force's instrument flight procedures manual, which writes for fixed- and rotary-wing pilots. [[1]](#source-1) The illusions are from the FAA's Aeronautical Information Manual, which all US airspace users share. [[2]](#source-2)

PAR, TACAN and IFR are in the airfield and ATC terms, and radials and station passage in the navigation terms.
