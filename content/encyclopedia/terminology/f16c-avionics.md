usage: "Pilots of the F-16C, real or simulated, working its sensors, displays and the HARM Targeting System"
terms:
  - term: SOI
    expansion: Sensor of interest
    definition: "The one sensor or display the hand controls are working at a time. The cursor and target management switches act on the SOI, which is marked by a frame round its display; the display management switch moves it between displays."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "330–331" }]
  - term: SPI
    expansion: System point of interest
    definition: "The single point on the ground the whole system is working on, which every sensor in Slave mode looks at. It starts as the selected steerpoint; designating a target with a sensor, or tracking one with the targeting pod, makes that sensor own it."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "489, 526" }]
  - term: HOTAS
    expansion: Hands on throttle and stick
    definition: "Controls on the throttle grip and side stick that let the pilot work the sensors, displays and weapons without taking a hand off either."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "83–87" }]
  - term: MFD
    expansion: Multifunction display
    definition: "A square cockpit screen ringed by push buttons whose labels change with the page shown, so one screen can show radar, targeting pod, stores or other formats in turn."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "122" }]
  - term: OSB
    expansion: Option select button
    definition: "One of the push buttons round a multifunction display. What it does is shown by the label beside it on the screen."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "122" }]
  - term: DED
    expansion: Data entry display
    definition: "The small text display below the head-up display where navigation, communication and system pages are read and changed, using the integrated control panel's keypad."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "98–101" }]
  - term: ICP
    expansion: Integrated control panel
    definition: "The keypad and switches below the head-up display used to call up and change the pages on the data entry display."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "98–99" }]
  - term: TMS
    expansion: Target management switch
    definition: "A four-way switch on the side stick. On the sensor of interest it designates (forward), rejects (aft) and steps or selects modes (left and right), with a different meaning for a short press and a long hold."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "84, 547" }]
  - term: DMS
    expansion: Display management switch
    definition: "A four-way switch on the side stick that chooses formats and, aft, moves the sensor of interest between displays."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "84, 545" }]
  - term: HTS
    expansion: HARM Targeting System
    definition: "The AN/ASQ-213 pod that passively detects and locates enemy air defence radars from their emissions, across the forward 180° of the aircraft, and cues the AGM-88 HARM and other sensors to them."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "525–527" }]
  - term: HAD
    expansion: HARM attack display
    definition: "The HTS format on a multifunction display: a plan view of the radars the pod has found, coloured by what each is doing, with its cursor, range scale and the data on the radar under the cursor."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "530–532" }]
  - term: TGP
    expansion: Targeting pod
    definition: "An electro-optical and infrared camera pod, on the F-16C the AN/AAQ-33 Sniper, used to find, identify, track and laser-designate targets, and to measure their position."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "486, 489" }]
  - term: Slave mode
    definition: "A targeting pod mode in which the pod looks wherever the system point of interest is, rather than being steered or tracking. It is shown by large crosshairs."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "489, 497" }]
  - term: Point track
    definition: "A targeting pod mode that locks onto a well-defined object at the centre of the picture by its edges, and follows it if it moves. The pod then owns the system point of interest."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "491" }]
  - term: Area track
    definition: "A targeting pod mode that holds a patch of ground steady rather than one object. The pod falls back to it when there is nothing well defined to track."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "491" }]
  - term: Error ellipse
    definition: "The area within which a passively located radar probably lies. It is long along the line of sight when every bearing comes from nearly the same direction, and shrinks as bearings from different angles cross."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "528" }]
  - term: PGM level
    definition: "The HTS's grade for how well it has located a radar, from PGM 5 (largest error ellipse) to PGM 1 (smallest). PGM 2 is usually close enough for a short targeting pod search to find the site; PGM 1 needs TDOA ranging."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "528" }]
  - term: Angle-of-arrival ranging
    definition: "Locating a radar by measuring the direction its signal arrives from on scan after scan, from different points along the aircraft's path, and crossing those bearings. It needs the radar well off the nose and reaches PGM 2 at best."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "527–528, 541" }]
  - term: TDOA ranging
    expansion: Time-difference-of-arrival ranging
    definition: "Locating a radar from the differences in when its signal reaches three HTS-equipped aircraft sharing their measurements by datalink. Faster and more precise than angle-of-arrival ranging, reaching PGM 1."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "529, 545–546" }]
  - term: ALIC code
    definition: "A three-digit number that identifies a radar type to the HTS and the HARM, for example 108 for the SA-6's Straight Flush radar. It is typed in to add a radar to a manual threat class or a HARM table."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "538, 749" }]
  - term: SEAD target
    definition: "The radar designated on the HAD. It is boxed, owns the system point of interest, and is the radar the HARM and slaved sensors are cued to."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "539" }]
  - term: Field of regard
    definition: "The whole region a sensor can look into without the aircraft turning. For the HTS it is the forward 180° of the aircraft."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "527, 541" }]
  - term: FLCS
    expansion: Flight control system
    definition: "The F-16C's flight control system, which moves the control surfaces through flight control computers from the pilot's stick and rudder inputs. Its branches have their own power supply, including a permanent magnet generator, checked before flight."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "73, 125" }]
  - term: EPU
    expansion: Emergency power unit
    definition: "A self-contained unit, run on engine bleed air or hydrazine, that gives emergency hydraulic and electrical power when the engine or main power is lost."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "64" }]
  - term: JFS
    expansion: Jet fuel starter
    definition: "A small turbine that spins the main engine up for start. The RUN light shows it is running."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "63, 134" }]
  - term: FTIT
    expansion: Fan turbine inlet temperature
    definition: "The engine temperature read on its own gauge, watched during start and in flight."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "50" }]
  - term: INS
    expansion: Inertial navigation system
    definition: "Works out the jet's position, heading and attitude from its own motion sensors. It must be aligned on the ground, with the jet kept still, before it can navigate."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "79, 167" }]
  - term: EHSI
    expansion: Electronic horizontal situation indicator
    definition: "The round display below the main instruments showing heading, the course and bearing to the selected steerpoint or TACAN station, and the distance to it."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "27, 228" }]
  - term: UFC
    expansion: Upfront controls
    definition: "The controls below the head-up display: the integrated control panel and the data entry display, with the switches around them."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "98" }]
  - term: CNI page
    definition: "The data entry display's home page, showing the radios, the selected steerpoint, the time and the TACAN. The DCS switch returns the display to it."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "100, 103" }]
  - term: HMCS
    expansion: Helmet-mounted cueing system
    definition: "Symbols projected on the helmet visor: an extension of the head-up display that works wherever the pilot looks, used to aim sensors and weapons off the nose."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "228, 549" }]
  - term: FCR
    expansion: Fire control radar
    definition: "The F-16C's nose radar, with air-to-air and air-to-ground modes, shown on its own MFD format."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "376" }]
  - term: MIDS
    expansion: Multifunctional Information Distribution System
    definition: "The jet's Link 16 datalink terminal, which shares positions and targets between flight members. Its knob on the avionics power panel turns it on, off or zeroises it."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "79" }]
  - term: Steerpoint
    definition: "A numbered point stored in the navigation system for the jet to steer to; the HUD and EHSI show the course and distance to the selected one. Some number ranges are kept for markpoints, pre-planned threats and datalink targets."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "27, 205–206" }]
  - term: HSD
    expansion: Horizontal situation display
    definition: "The MFD format that gives a map-like plan view round the jet: range rings, the steerpoints and route, markpoints, threats and flight members, heading up."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "230, 358–359" }]
  - term: Markpoint
    definition: "A point stored in flight to come back to or target later: the ground below the jet as it passes overhead, or the spot a sensor or the HUD is on. Stored in turn in steerpoints 26 to 30, and drawn on the HSD as a small cyan X."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "205, 219–220" }]
  - term: MGRS
    expansion: Military Grid Reference System
    definition: "A way of giving a position as a grid zone, a 100 km square and an easting and northing in metres (37SCV 28541/55914), instead of latitude and longitude. The DED's MARK page shows it beside a markpoint's latitude and longitude; the STPT page can show steerpoints 21 to 25 in it."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "208, 219–220" }]
  - term: SMS
    expansion: Stores management system
    definition: "The jet's weapons manager. Its MFD format picks the weapon, powers it and shows which stations carry it; the WPN format then shows that weapon's own sensor and modes."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "659" }]
  - term: Master mode
    definition: "One of the modes the mission computer switches the jet's sensors, displays and weapons between: navigation, air-to-air, air-to-ground, missile override, dogfight and the two jettison modes. Each remembers its own settings."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "326–329" }]
  - term: Master arm
    expansion: MASTER ARM switch
    definition: "The switch on the MISC panel that allows weapons to be released. In ARM the weapons can fire; in OFF they cannot; in SIMULATE the cues are shown but nothing fires."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "47" }]
  - term: CCRP
    expansion: Continuously computed release point
    definition: "A pre-planned air-to-ground delivery sub-mode in which the jet gives steering cues to the point where the bomb should be released to hit a designated target (a steerpoint or a targeting-pod point), and releases it automatically there while the pilot holds the release button."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "335, 628" }]
  - term: DTOS
    expansion: Dive toss
    definition: "A visual air-to-ground delivery sub-mode. The pilot puts the HUD cursor on a target they can see and designates it; the target designator box and the sensors then hold that point, and the release is computed to it."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "333, 335" }]
  - term: LADD
    expansion: Low altitude drogue delivery
    definition: "A pre-planned air-to-ground delivery sub-mode for bombs: like CCRP, it aims at a target whose position is set by a steerpoint rather than seen by the pilot. It is offered on the SMS page beside CCIP, CCRP and DTOS."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "335, 619" }]
  - term: POS mode
    expansion: Position known
    definition: "The HARM mode for a radar whose position is already known: the missile is told the radar type and flies toward the position until it hears the radar, then homes on its signal. Designating a radar on the HAD sets it."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "539, 668" }]
  - term: HAS mode
    expansion: HARM as sensor
    definition: "The HARM mode in which the missile's own seeker searches ahead of the jet for the radars on the chosen threat table and shows what it hears on the WPN format, for the pilot to pick one."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "660–661" }]
  - term: AMZ
    expansion: Aircraft maneuver zone
    definition: "The area from which a HARM can reach the target if the launching jet first turns toward it or lofts. Beyond it the missile cannot get there."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "663" }]
  - term: MMZ
    expansion: Missile maneuver zone
    definition: "The area from which a HARM can reach the target by its own manoeuvring alone, with no turn or loft needed first. The weapon release button is live only inside it."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "663" }]
  - term: HARM launch scale
    expansion: HLS
    definition: "The range staple on the right of the HUD in a POS shot: it spans the missile's minimum to maximum launch range, with a dash between the AMZ and the MMZ and a caret for the jet's present range."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "663–664" }]
  - term: WPN format
    expansion: Weapon format
    definition: "The MFD format for the selected weapon's own sensor and modes: for the AGM-65 Maverick, the missile seeker's video under its crosshairs; for the HARM, its search or handoff data."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "660, 673" }]
  - term: Pointer cross
    definition: "The cross on the Maverick's WPN format that shows where the missile's seeker is looking, measured from the jet's boresight. It flashes when the seeker is more than 10 degrees off the boresight or the target image is too small to track: a launch condition is not met."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "673" }]
  - term: PRE mode
    expansion: Pre-planned
    definition: "The Maverick delivery sub-mode that slaves the seeker to the system point of interest, usually a steerpoint, so the pilot finds the target on the WPN format near that point and locks it there."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "681" }]
  - term: VIS mode
    expansion: Visual
    definition: "The Maverick delivery sub-mode for a target the pilot can see: a box on the HUD is slewed over it and designated, and the seeker looks there. Not available with Mavericks on the three-missile LAU-88 rack."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "683" }]
  - term: BORE mode
    expansion: Boresight
    definition: "The Maverick delivery sub-mode for a quick shot at a target of opportunity: the seeker starts on the jet's boresight and is shown as a cross on the HUD, which the pilot flies near the target and slews onto it."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "685" }]
  - term: MBC
    expansion: Missile boresight correlator
    definition: "The system that hands a target from the targeting pod to a Maverick: it compares the pod's picture with the missile's and slews the seeker until they match, then commands the missile to track. A letter over the station on the WPN format shows how far it has got."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "671, 673, 687" }]
  - term: Video polarity
    definition: "The contrast a seeker or pod works on: hot-on-cold (HOC) or cold-on-hot (COH). On the Maverick it is changed on the WPN format or with the TMS; the G and H models add AREA, for force correlate."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "674, 690" }]
  - term: Force correlate
    definition: "A Maverick tracking mode (AGM-65G and H, polarity AREA) that tracks a chosen part of the picture rather than the centre of a vehicle, so the missile hits that exact spot on a building or structure."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "690" }]
  - term: CRM
    expansion: Combined radar mode
    definition: "The fire control radar's main air-to-air mode for fights beyond visual range. It holds the search and tracking sub-modes: range while search, velocity search with ranging, track while scan, situation awareness mode and dual and single target track. The pilot steps between them with a button on the radar page or the target management switch."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "408–409" }]
  - term: B-scope
    definition: "A radar picture drawn as a rectangle with bearing across and range up the screen, the jet's own position at the bottom edge. Contacts at the same range lie on one horizontal line whatever their bearing, which makes range easy to read but distorts geometry close in."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "394" }]
  - term: Situation awareness mode
    expansion: SAM
    definition: "A fire control radar mode that keeps searching while it also tracks one chosen (bugged) contact, by breaking off the search scan now and then to look at it. The pilot gets the contact's heading, speed and height without losing sight of the others; a second designation commands a single-target track."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "417–418" }]
  - term: CATA
    expansion: Collision antenna train angle
    definition: "A cue on the radar page, drawn at the bugged target's range, that steers an intercept. Turning towards it until it sits in the middle of the screen puts the jet on a collision course with the target."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "404–405" }]
  - term: Missile override
    expansion: MSL
    definition: "A master mode for missile shots, selected by sliding the DOG FIGHT switch on the throttle inboard. It sets up the radar and the stores for air-to-air missiles without a hand leaving the controls, recalls the missile type last used in it (AIM-120 by default) and locks out the A-A and A-G buttons until the switch is centred again. The gun cannot be fired in it."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "558–559, 599" }]
  - term: Dogfight mode
    expansion: DGFT
    definition: "A master mode for close-in combat, selected by sliding the DOG FIGHT switch on the throttle outboard. Missiles and the gun can both be fired; the head-up display is decluttered and shows the gun sight."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "558–559" }]
  - term: DLZ
    expansion: Dynamic launch zone
    definition: "The span of ranges in which a missile fired now should reach the bugged target, worked out from both aircraft's speeds, headings and heights and shown on the missile scale. For the AIM-120 it runs from RPI down to RMIN. The box within it, from RTR to RMIN, is the manoeuvre zone, where even a target that turns away and runs should be caught. ROPT and RAERO sit above it."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "595–596" }]
  - term: RAERO
    expansion: Aerodynamic range
    definition: "The AIM-120's longest reach against the bugged target, if the pilot steers on the attack steering cue and pulls up to a 40° loft before the shot. A target that manoeuvres at all will outrun a missile fired this far out."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "595" }]
  - term: RPI
    expansion: Range, probability of intercept
    definition: "The longest range at which an AIM-120 fired with the attack steering cue in its circle has a high chance of hitting a target that does not manoeuvre: the top of the dynamic launch zone's bar."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "596, 602" }]
  - term: RTR
    expansion: Turn-and-run range
    definition: "The longest range at which an AIM-120 should still catch a target that turns hard away and accelerates the moment it is fired: the top of the box on the missile scale."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "596" }]
  - term: ASEC
    expansion: Allowable steering error circle
    definition: "The circle in the head-up display and on the radar page within which the attack steering cue must sit for an AIM-120 shot. Small beyond ROPT, it grows as the range closes to RPI, and shrinks again at close range where the missile has little room to turn. A caret on its rim shows the target's aspect: at the bottom, tail-on; at the top, nose-on."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "594" }]
  - term: ASC
    expansion: Attack steering cue
    definition: "A small circle that shows where to steer for an AIM-120 shot: fly it into the middle of the allowable steering error circle. Beyond the launch zone it steers left and right only; inside it, up and down as well."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "594, 602" }]
  - term: M-pole
    definition: "On the AIM-120's missile scale, the range from the shooter to the target at the moment the missile's own radar goes active, if the target holds its course and speed. Shown before the shot for a missile fired now, and after it for the one in flight."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "595–596" }]
  - term: F-pole
    definition: "The range from the shooter to the target when the missile reaches it, if neither aircraft changes course or speed. The F-16C's missile scale shows it once the AIM-120 in flight has gone active, or before the shot at ranges so close the missile would go active at once."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "595–596" }]
  - term: System track
    definition: "A track the fire control radar has promoted from the ten it keeps in track while scan, so the pilot can work with it. Only a system track can be bugged: the radar looks at it more often, shows its data, and steers a missile shot or a target classification to it."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "414–415" }]
  - term: DT SAM
    expansion: Dual target situation awareness mode
    definition: "Situation awareness mode with a second contact designated: between search sweeps the radar looks hard at the first (primary) contact and then the second. Stepping the bug between the two lets one jet fire at both."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "419" }]
  - term: DTT
    expansion: Dual target track
    definition: "The radar mode that takes over from dual target SAM once either contact is inside 10 NM: the search stops and the radar only alternates between the two contacts, for the steadiest tracks at close range."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "420" }]

## The words of the F-16C's avionics

These are the words for how an F-16C pilot works the jet's sensors and displays: the sensor of interest and system point of interest that tie them together, the hand controls and screens, the HARM Targeting System and targeting pod used to find and target enemy air defence radars, and the weapon formats used to aim the HARM and the Maverick. They describe the aircraft as the DCS F-16C simulation models it [[1]](#source-1). Engine, power and flight control words used in the F-16C procedures are here too.
