short: Precision targeting
usage: Aircrew, JTACs and FAC(A)s of every service who employ laser-guided and coordinate-guided weapons
terms:
  - term: ALTD
    expansion: Airborne laser target designator
    definition: "A laser target designator carried on an aircraft. It does the same job as a ground designator, can lase at very long range and is normally used below 30,000 ft above ground level."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-55" }]
  - term: Attack angle
    definition: "In a laser-guided attack, the angle between the laser-to-target line and the seeker-to-target line. The largest usable angle depends on the weapon; if it is too large, the seeker does not receive enough reflected energy to sense the spot."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-57–III-58" }]
  - term: Autonomous designation
    definition: "Lasing for a weapon from the aircraft that launched it, with its own on-board designator; also called self-designation. Most aircraft that can deliver laser-guided bombs can self-designate."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-58" }]
  - term: Backscatter
    definition: "Laser energy scattered back toward the seeker by smoke, dust, haze or other particles in the air. It competes with the energy reflected from the target, so a seeker may lock onto the obscurant instead; it can happen even on clear days."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-53" }]
  - term: Beam divergence
    definition: "How fast a laser beam spreads. A beam with one milliradian of divergence makes a spot about one metre across at 1,000 m, so the spot grows with the distance from designator to target."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-53" }, { n: 2, page: "10.6.2.3" }]
  - term: Buddy lasing
    definition: "Assisted laser designation by another aircraft: one aircraft lases the target for a laser-guided weapon dropped by another. Assisted designation can also come from a ground team or a FAC(A); aircraft without their own designator always need it."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-58" }]
  - term: CE90
    expansion: Circular error, 90 percent
    definition: "The horizontal part of a target location error: the radius within which there is a 90 percent chance the actual target lies."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-34" }]
  - term: DEAD EYE
    definition: Laser designator system inoperative.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-79" }]
  - term: DPPDB
    expansion: Digital point positioning database
    definition: "Stereo imagery from which certified analysts measure precise target coordinates, one of the sources used in target coordinate mensuration."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-33" }]
  - term: Dual-mode weapon
    definition: "A GPS-aided weapon that also takes laser guidance: it guides to a preprogrammed coordinate and can then follow a laser spot, which lets it hit moving targets."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-61–III-62" }]
  - term: False seeker lock-on
    definition: "A laser seeker locking onto stray energy, such as the designator itself or the scatter along the beam, instead of the target. It is most likely near the designator, so the designator is masked from the seeker's view and correlation follows every laser hand-off."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-53" }]
  - term: GLTD
    expansion: Ground laser target designator
    definition: "A laser target designator used by ground forces. Ranges vary from 10 m to 20 km. Compared with an airborne designator its spot is smaller, but it restricts the attack axis and exposes the operator."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-55–III-56" }]
  - term: IAM
    expansion: Inertially aided munition
    definition: "A weapon guided by its own GPS-aided inertial navigation system from release to target coordinates, regardless of weather, camouflage or obscurants. It needs encrypted GPS signals and accurate three-dimensional target locations."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-61" }]
  - term: Laser code
    definition: "The four-digit pulse repetition frequency (PRF) code that pairs a laser designator with the weapons and spot trackers that may use it. The designator, the weapon and any spot tracker must be on the same code. A laser-guided bomb's code is normally set before flight; most missiles, trackers and designators can change code in flight."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-56, III-58" }]
  - term: Laser safety zone
    definition: "A 20-degree wedge with its apex at the target, 10 degrees either side of the target-to-designator line, from the surface to infinity. Attack headings avoid it, unless the tactical situation dictates otherwise, because a seeker there may guide on the designator."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-56–III-57" }]
  - term: LOAL
    expansion: Lock-on after launch
    definition: "A missile launch in which the seeker locks onto the coded laser energy after launch. The aircraft can fire without line of sight to the target, which reduces its exposure, delays the designation and extends range with a remote designator."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-60" }]
  - term: LOBL
    expansion: Lock-on before launch
    definition: "A missile launch in which the seeker locks onto properly coded laser energy before launch, so it needs line of sight to the target. It confirms the missile sees the right code and target, and gives a higher probability of hit close to the target."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-60" }]
  - term: LRF
    expansion: Laser range finder
    definition: "A device that uses low-power laser pulses to measure range to an object. A target locating device combines one with a compass, tilt sensors and GPS to work out a target's coordinates."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-34" }]
  - term: LST
    expansion: Laser spot tracker
    definition: "An aircraft sensor that searches for laser energy on a set code and cues the pilot's head-up display to the spot. It has a limited field of view, is used to find the target, and must never be the only source of target identification."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-54, III-56" }]
  - term: LTD
    expansion: Laser target designator
    definition: "A laser that marks a target with coded energy, either for a laser-guided weapon's terminal guidance or for a coded laser spot tracker, or both. It may be on the ground (GLTD) or on an aircraft (ALTD)."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-55, III-58" }]
  - term: Mensuration
    definition: "Target coordinate mensuration: measuring a feature or location on the earth to find its absolute latitude, longitude and height, with its errors understood and reported. People who mensurate points for coordinate-seeking weapons must be certified."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-33–III-34" }]
  - term: Optimal attack zone
    definition: "For a ground laser designator, a 120-degree wedge with its apex at the target, 60 degrees either side of the target-to-designator line. Aircraft must be inside it and outside the safety zone; the 90-degree wedge, 10 to 45 degrees off the line, is preferred for the best seeker acquisition."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-57" }]
  - term: Semi-active homing
    definition: "Homing in which the target is illuminated from somewhere else, such as a designator on the ground or another aircraft, and the weapon carries only a receiver and steers on the reflected energy. The target may not reflect well toward the weapon."
    cite: [{ n: 3, page: "16.4.2.2" }]
  - term: Spillover
    definition: "Laser energy that misses the target because the spot is too large or only partly on it. Energy travelling past the target may reflect back to the weapon and cause erroneous guidance."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-54" }]
  - term: TLE
    expansion: Target location error
    definition: "The difference between the coordinates generated for a target and its actual location, given as circular and vertical error (CE90 and VE90), or sometimes spherical error (SE90). It is grouped into six categories, from CAT I (best) to CAT VI."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-34–III-35" }]
  - term: VE90
    expansion: Vertical error, 90 percent
    definition: "The vertical part of a target location error: the elevation error within which there is a 90 percent chance the actual target lies. A steeper impact angle reduces its effect on a coordinate-guided weapon."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-34, III-61" }]
  - term: WGS 84
    expansion: World Geodetic System 1984
    definition: "The datum the US Department of Defense assumes for coordinates unless the SPINS say otherwise. Everyone in an attack must use the same maps, databases and datum, or coordinates can be significantly wrong."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-28, III-62" }]

## The words of laser and coordinate guidance

These are the words for putting a guided weapon on a precise point: the designators, trackers and codes of laser guidance, the geometry that keeps a seeker off the designator, and the errors that decide how well a coordinate-guided weapon hits. They come from the joint close air support publication, which every service follows, and the Naval Academy's weapons systems text. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2) [[3]](#source-3)

The laser calls (LASER ON, LASING, SPOT, CEASE LASER and the rest), the laser target line, bomb on target and bomb on coordinate are in the close air support terms; laser-guided weapon and precision-guided munition are in the weapons terms. The Manual's [Precision Targeting](module:precision-targeting) module uses all of them.
