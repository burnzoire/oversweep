terms:
  - term: Command guidance
    definition: "Missile guidance in which a fire control computer on the ground tracks both the target, with its tracking radar, and the missile, through a beacon on the missile, and sends steering commands to the missile throughout its flight. The beacon is usually masked until the booster separates, so the missile flies unguided for its first 2–3 seconds, one reason these systems have a minimum launch range."
    note: "The missile carries no seeker, so its accuracy is only as good as the ground radar's track, and its maneuvers lag the target's. [1]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "8-3, 8-5" }]
  - term: Active guidance
    definition: "Missile guidance in which the missile carries its own small radar and fire control computer and turns them on to finish the intercept itself, after a mid-course phase flown on command or semi-active guidance. Once it goes active it needs no help from the launcher."
    note: "Hard to jam in the terminal phase; the mid-course phase may still be jammed through the launcher's tracking radar. [1]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "8-9, 8-10" }]
  - term: Track-via-missile
    expansion: TVM
    definition: "Missile guidance in which a ground radar illuminates the target and the missile's seeker receives the reflected energy, but the missile sends that data down to the ground fire control system, which computes the steering and uplinks it. Seeker-aided ground guidance works the same way with less processing in the missile."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "8-11" }]
  - term: Aimed fire
    definition: "Anti-aircraft gunfire laid at the point where the aircraft is predicted to be when the rounds arrive, computed by a radar fire control computer or by a gunner with an optical sight. The prediction assumes the aircraft holds the same heading, altitude and airspeed for the projectile's time of flight."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "8-12, 8-13" }]
  - term: Barrage fire
    definition: "Anti-aircraft gunfire fired into a sector of airspace that aircraft are expected to fly through, used when they cannot be tracked accurately, so that they fly into a curtain of fire. Also called sector fire; especially effective for the point defence of a fixed target, whose attack directions and altitudes can be foreseen."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "8-14" }]
  - term: MANPADS
    expansion: Man-portable air defense system
    definition: "A shoulder-fired surface-to-air missile, normally infrared-homing, carried by infantry units down to platoon level."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "14-1" }, { n: 3, page: "5-26" }]
  - term: Indirect terrain masking
    definition: "Flying close to a prominent terrain feature that the radar beam also illuminates, so that a pulse radar cannot tell the aircraft's return from the terrain's. Most effective abeam the radar; pulse-Doppler and moving-target-indicator radars may be able to negate it."
    note: "Direct terrain masking puts the terrain between the aircraft and the radar instead; see Terrain masking. [1]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "6-25" }]
  - term: Threat reaction
    definition: "The maneuvers an aircrew flies, the chaff, flares and jamming it uses, and the calls it makes to the rest of the flight, when a surface or air threat engages it."
    note: "A reacting pilot tells the flight the reference heading, altitude and type of reaction, because no one can easily watch the others during one. [2]"
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "275" }]
  - term: Orthogonal break
    definition: "A training maneuver like the last-ditch maneuver against a SAM confirmed to be guiding on the aircraft: a level hard turn to put the threat at 3 or 9 o'clock, then, with the threat in sight, a hard pull out of plane, perpendicular to its intercept axis, rolling to keep it at 3 or 9 o'clock until it overshoots, then a recovery from the resulting nose-high attitude."
    cite: [{ n: 3, page: "5-15, 5-16" }]
  - term: Vertical jink
    definition: "A climb to a planned angle of not more than 30°, a short delay, an unloaded roll to inverted and a pull through level to a dive about 10° shallower, then upright and a pull back to level; a low-altitude exercise that may also be flown in an oblique plane."
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "260" }]
  - term: Curvilinear approach
    definition: "A weapons delivery in which the aircraft constantly changes heading, altitude, airspeed and G from roll-in until it rolls out on final, so it tracks the target only briefly and the AAA's chance of a hit falls. Commonly a descending turn at 30–60° of bank."
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "167" }]
  - term: Imminent threat warning
    expansion: ITW
    definition: "A surface-to-air threat warning passed by a command and control or ISR platform to a friendly aircraft that comes within 5 NM of the briefed maximum effective range of an active threat. Not needed once that aircraft has called MUD, DEFENDING or SINGER for the threat."
    cite: [{ n: 3, page: "A1-82" }]

## The words of threat avoidance

These are the words for how surface-to-air missiles and guns find and guide on an aircraft, and how aircrew avoid and defeat them. The guidance and gunfire terms come from an Air Force electronic warfare student text; the maneuvers and the warning from two versions of the Air Force's F-16 fundamentals, used here for principles that apply to any aircraft. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2) [[3]](#source-3)

Semi-active homing is in the precision targeting terms; the Doppler notch, decoy flares and break lock in the sensor terms; chaff in the weapons terms; terrain masking in the SEAD terms; DIRT, MUD, SINGER, DEFENDING and SAM in the brevity words; and the terrain flight modes of helicopters in the rotary-wing attack terms.
