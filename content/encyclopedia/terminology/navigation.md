short: Navigation
usage: Pilots of every service
terms:
  - term: Deviation
    definition: The compass's own error from the aircraft's magnetic fields. Metal, electrical current and equipment turn with the aircraft, so deviation changes with heading; a deviation card near the compass gives the correction.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "2-6–2-7" }]
  - term: Direct to fix
    definition: An area navigation leg type (DF) that runs from wherever the aircraft is straight to a waypoint.
    cite: [{ n: 4, page: "¶1-2-1" }]
  - term: Drift
    definition: The decay of an inertial navigation system's accuracy with time after it is aligned with a known position.
    note: "Drift is also the angle between heading and track; see drift angle. [5]"
    cite: [{ n: 4, page: "¶1-1-15" }]
  - term: Drift angle
    definition: The angle between the aircraft's heading, where the nose points, and its track over the ground.
    cite: [{ n: 5, page: "16-9–16-10" }]
  - term: Fly-by waypoint
    definition: A waypoint at which the aircraft begins the turn to the next course before reaching the point (turn anticipation). A fly-over waypoint must be crossed before the turn begins.
    cite: [{ n: 4, page: "¶1-2-1" }]
  - term: Grid zone designation
    definition: "The zone number and latitude band letter that begin a Military Grid Reference System (MGRS) reference, such as “16S”. Between 80°S and 84°N the earth is divided into 60 zones 6° of longitude wide, numbered west to east from the 180° meridian, and 20 bands lettered C to X from south to north."
    cite: [{ n: 3, page: "4-12–4-13" }]
  - term: Homing
    definition: Flying to a station by keeping the bearing pointer at the top of the instrument without correcting for wind. In a crosswind it flies a curved path.
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "64" }]
  - term: Lead point
    definition: Where a turn starts so that the aircraft rolls out on the course or arc. For a 90° intercept the lead is 1% of ground speed, and a proportion of that for smaller angles.
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "65–66" }]
  - term: Radial
    definition: A magnetic bearing from a VOR or TACAN station. Whatever its heading, an aircraft on the 090 radial is due east of the station, and flying to the station on that radial its magnetic course is 270.
    note: "On the horizontal situation indicator, the tail of the bearing pointer sits on the radial the aircraft is on. [2]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "2-28" }, { n: 2, page: "12–13" }]
  - term: RAIM
    expansion: Receiver autonomous integrity monitoring
    definition: A GPS receiver's check of its own solution, using a satellite beyond those needed for a position. Without it the pilot has no assurance that the position is right.
    cite: [{ n: 4, page: "¶1-1-17" }]
  - term: Relative bearing
    definition: The angle measured clockwise from the aircraft's nose to an object, so an object directly behind is at 180 on any heading. Heading plus relative bearing gives the true bearing.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "2-11–2-13" }]
  - term: Slant range
    definition: The straight-line distance from the aircraft to a station, including height, which is what DME measures, rather than horizontal distance.
    cite: [{ n: 4, page: "¶1-1-7" }]
  - term: Station passage
    definition: Crossing over the station. With VOR, it is when the bearing pointer falls past the 90° benchmark; TACAN has a larger cone of confusion over the station, so TACAN station passage is when DME stops decreasing.
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "65" }]
  - term: Track
    definition: The actual path of the aircraft over the ground. Course is the intended path; heading is where the nose points.
    cite: [{ n: 5, page: "16-9–16-10" }]
  - term: Variation
    definition: The angle between true north and magnetic north. It is east when magnetic north lies east of true north and west when it lies west, and it differs from place to place.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "2-6" }]

## The words of navigation

These are the words of finding the way: bearings and radials, the compass's errors, flying to and from a station, waypoints and satellite and inertial systems, and the military grid. The Navy's air traffic controller manual and its T-45 instrument flight instruction come first, with the Army's map-reading manual for the grid that every service uses. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2) [[3]](#source-3)

The FAA's manual and handbook are cited for area navigation, GPS integrity and the difference between heading, course and track, which military and civil pilots share. [[4]](#source-4) [[5]](#source-5) TACAN and NOTAM are in the airfield and ATC terms.
