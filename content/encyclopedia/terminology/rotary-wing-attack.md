---
short: Rotary-wing attack
usage: Attack helicopter aircrew of every service, and the controllers and ground units they support
terms:
  - term: Attack by fire position
    form: "(ABF)"
    definition: "Army. A position that attack and reconnaissance helicopters fly to along preplanned attack routes to engage the enemy, set as a control measure in planning or selected in hasty attack planning. It is occupied much like a battle position, but normally with more freedom of movement, and is chosen for sensor range rather than weapons range."
    cite: [{ n: 3, page: "3-4, 3-7" }, { n: 4, page: "3-19, 3-85" }]
  - term: Attack helicopter control point
    definition: "A special control point that attack helicopter aircrews can use in addition to the normal close air support control points."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-91" }]
  - term: Bounding overwatch
    definition: "The movement technique for when enemy contact is imminent. The flight moves by nap-of-the-earth flight, deliberately, with speed not essential, in two elements: one bounds forward while the other takes up an overwatch position, covering it from a covered, concealed position with observation and fields of fire."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-92" }]
  - term: Bump attack
    definition: "Army. An attack method that uses masking terrain while increasing the angle of attack: each aircrew bumps up before or during the engagement, normally in running fire transitioning to diving fire, then returns to terrain flight altitude. It gives longer range, less dispersion and a better look-down angle, but silhouettes the aircraft and can bleed airspeed."
    cite: [{ n: 4, page: "3-70" }]
  - term: Continuous attack
    definition: "Army. A method of employment in which at least one attack company is always in the fight while the others wait at the holding area or FARP, or move to a battle position or attack by fire position, to relieve it. It keeps constant pressure on the enemy and gives the most flexibility."
    cite: [{ n: 3, page: "3-12 to 3-13" }]
  - term: Contour flight
    definition: "Terrain flight that follows the contour of the earth or vegetation to conceal the aircraft from enemy observation or detection, normally from 50 to 100 ft above ground level. It is used until the aircraft reaches a higher threat area."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-91" }]
  - term: Defilade
    definition: "Protection from hostile observation and fire given by an obstacle such as a hill, ridge or bank; also the vertical distance by which a position is concealed from enemy observation, or the act of shielding from enemy fire or observation with natural or artificial obstacles."
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "63" }]
  - term: Diving fire
    definition: "Fire delivered from altitude in descending forward flight. With unguided ordnance it may give the most accurate results, and it lets the aircrew stay above or outside the threat envelope; attack helicopters often use it from an overhead position."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-94" }]
  - term: Firing point
    form: "(FP)"
    definition: "An individual position inside a battle position from which an attack helicopter fires. Aircrews choose their own firing points once in the battle position and stay masked until the time on target, time to target or the order to attack."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-92–III-93" }]
  - term: High attack
    definition: "Army. An attack method in diving fire from higher altitudes, normally above 1,000 ft. It suits following targets through urban areas and remote engagements, keeps the aircraft above accurate small arms fire, and reduces dispersion and laser error, but makes infrared and radar missiles more effective."
    cite: [{ n: 4, page: "3-70" }]
  - term: Holding area
    form: "(HA)"
    definition: "An area where helicopters wait for targets or missions. Holding areas may be set up during planning, named or numbered, and activated during operations; while in use they serve as informal airspace coordination areas."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-91" }]
  - term: Hovering fire
    definition: "Fire delivered with the helicopter stationary or with little forward motion, after unmasking from a defilade position or while standing off in a safe area. It is held only briefly and followed by remasking or displacing. It is the most effective profile for precision guided munitions but may reduce the accuracy of rockets and guns."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-93–III-94" }]
  - term: Indirect fire
    form: "(IDF)"
    definition: "Fire delivered when the aircrew cannot see the target, as opposed to direct fire with an unobstructed view of it. Helicopters can deliver it in running fire, or in hovering fire hidden from the enemy by terrain."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-94" }]
  - term: JAAT
    expansion: Joint air attack team
    definition: "Fixed-wing close air support aircraft and helicopters operating together to locate and attack high-priority targets and other targets of opportunity, normally as a coordinated effort supported by fire support, air defense artillery, naval surface fire support and intelligence."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "I-5–I-6" }]
  - term: Low-level attack
    definition: "Army. An attack method flown near the terrain or at NOE, normally in hover or running fire. The low profile is masked by terrain or vegetation behind it, but the aircrew cannot engage at the weapon's maximum range and accuracy and dispersion suffer."
    cite: [{ n: 4, page: "3-69" }]
  - term: Low-level flight
    definition: "Terrain flight at a constant altitude, normally 100 to 200 ft above ground level, and airspeed. It reduces or avoids enemy detection or observation."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-91" }]
  - term: Maximum destruction attack
    definition: "Army. A method of employment in which all three companies of a battalion attack at once with massed fires. The whole battalion may then be out of the fight for 45 to 90 minutes, and much of it unable to fly further missions for at least 24 hours."
    cite: [{ n: 3, page: "3-13" }]
  - term: Mixed section
    definition: "A helicopter flight or section made of more than one aircraft type, such as an AH-64 and an OH-58 in an Army flight or an AH-1 and a UH-1 in a Marine Corps section. It gives the most flexible mix of sensors, communications, maneuverability, firepower and mutual support."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "I-5, III-90" }]
  - term: Munition attack azimuth
    definition: "An attack direction given for the munition rather than the aircraft. Because helicopters and unmanned aircraft can fire off-axis, they need not point at the target, so a controller may give one instead of a final attack heading."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "V-11" }]
  - term: NOE flight
    form: "(nap-of-the-earth)"
    definition: "Terrain flight as close to the earth's surface as vegetation and obstacles permit while following the earth's contours, so that terrain and vegetation give cover and concealment. Airspeed and height above ground vary with the terrain, weather, ambient light and enemy situation."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-91" }]
  - term: Overhead position
    definition: "An attack helicopter position over the target area from which it delivers diving fire. It gives high situational awareness, a view down into urban settings and a constant weapons solution, but leaves the aircraft continuously exposed; its altitude reduces the small-arms and RPG threat but helps an infrared SAM."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-94" }]
  - term: Phased attack
    definition: "Army. A method of employment in which one company shapes the battlefield, a second is phased in quickly from a different battle position or attack by fire position, and the third is the reserve. It gives more firepower but needs short FARP turnaround times."
    cite: [{ n: 3, page: "3-13" }]
  - term: Remask
    definition: "To move back behind cover after unmasking to fire. After hovering fire, aircrews remask or displace to avoid being targeted."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-94" }]
  - term: Running fire
    definition: "Fire delivered with the helicopter in level, forward flight. Forward flight may add stability and improve the accuracy of unguided ordnance, and a moving aircraft with less dust and debris than a hover may be harder for enemy air defenses to hit. It can be direct or indirect fire."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-94" }]
  - term: Support by fire position
    form: "(SBF)"
    definition: "Army. A position, like a battle position or attack by fire position, from which helicopters fire in support of a ground unit. It is normally offset from the flank of the friendly ground position, close enough for efficient target handoffs, so rotor wash and the aircraft's signature do not interfere with or reveal the ground unit, and the aircraft can hit the enemy's flank."
    cite: [{ n: 4, page: "3-19, B-3" }]
  - term: Terrain flight
    definition: "Flight close to the earth's surface, with airspeed, height and altitude adapted to the contours and cover of the ground to avoid enemy detection and fire. Its three profiles are low-level, contour and nap-of-the-earth."
    cite: [{ n: 2, page: "240" }, { n: 1, page: "III-90" }]
  - term: Traveling
    definition: "The movement technique for when enemy contact is remote: the flight moves at a constant speed, as steadily as the terrain allows, by low-level or contour flight. It allows rapid movement in relatively secure areas."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-92" }]
  - term: Traveling overwatch
    definition: "The movement technique for when enemy contact is possible. The flight moves by contour or NOE flight in two elements: the main element keeps moving forward while the overwatch element moves to give visual and weapons coverage of terrain from which the enemy might fire on it."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "III-92" }]

## The words of helicopter attack

These are the words for how attack helicopters get to a target unseen and fire on it: the terrain flight profiles and movement techniques, the holding areas, battle positions and firing points, and the three ways of firing. They come from the rotary-wing sections of the joint close air support publication, which every service follows, and the joint dictionary, with the Army's attack by fire positions, attack methods and methods of employment from its aviation field manuals. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2) [[3]](#source-3) [[4]](#source-4)

Battle position, close combat attack, the 5-line, LOBL and LOAL, FARP and BDA are in the close air support, precision targeting and joint terms. The Manual's [Rotary-Wing Attack](module:rotary-wing-attack) module uses all of them.
