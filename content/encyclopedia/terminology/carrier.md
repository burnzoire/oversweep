terms:
  - term: Angels
    definition: Altitude in thousands of feet.
    note: "The multi-service brevity word ANGELS adds that it is the height of friendly aircraft above mean sea level. [4] For helicopters ships use Cherubs, in hundreds of feet."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "11-2" }]
  - term: Ball
    definition: "A pilot report indicating that the visual landing aid is in sight. The ball itself is the bar of light the optical landing system shows the pilot: level with the green datum lights on the glide slope, above them when high and below them when low, with a red bottom cell to warn of a low approach."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "11-2" }, { n: 2, page: "5-52" }]
  - term: Base Recovery Course (BRC)
    definition: The ship's magnetic heading during flight operations.
    note: "Not the landing heading: the final bearing is an extension of the angled landing area's centerline. [1]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "11-2" }]
  - term: Bingo
    definition: "An order to proceed to and land at the field specified by the use of a bingo profile. The aircraft is in an emergency or fuel critical situation."
    note: "In the multi-service brevity list BINGO is the pre-briefed fuel state needed for recovery, which need not mean a divert. [4]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "11-2" }]
  - term: Bingo
    definition: The minimum fuel state required to divert safely to the nearest suitable field. Bingo is an emergency situation; the bingo fuel state depends on the aircraft type.
    usage: Navy carrier training
    cite: [{ n: 3, page: "A-1" }]
  - term: Bolter
    definition: A touchdown on the carrier in which the arresting hook does not engage the arresting wires.
    usage: Navy carrier training
    cite: [{ n: 3, page: "A-1" }]
  - term: Bullseye
    definition: A term referring to the Independent Landing Monitor (ILM).
    note: "Unrelated to the multi-service brevity word BULLSEYE, a reference point for positions given by bearing and range. [4]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "11-2" }]
  - term: Bullseye
    definition: "The instrument carrier landing system (ICLS) display. A controller who loses the ACLS lock directs “fly the bullseye” until the lock is regained."
    usage: Navy carrier training
    cite: [{ n: 3, page: "1-10" }, { n: 3, page: "2-23" }]
  - term: Buster
    definition: An order given to an aircraft to proceed at maximum speed.
    note: "Navy carrier training adds “generally for an immediate Charlie”. [3] The multi-service brevity word BUSTER is maximum continuous speed (military power), and GATE is afterburner. [4]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "11-2" }]
  - term: Case I, II and III
    definition: "Weather criteria for carrier operations. Case I: ceiling no lower than 3,000 feet and visibility not less than 5 miles. Case II: ceiling no lower than 1,000 feet and visibility not less than 5 miles. Case III: ceiling below 1,000 feet or visibility less than 5 miles."
    note: "Navy carrier training adds that Case III is used at night, and that Case I recoveries marshal overhead the ship and enter the pattern via the break. [3] Amphibious assault ships use the same fixed-wing figures, and lower ones for helicopters. [1]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "11-9" }, { n: 3, page: "A-2" }]
  - term: Charlie
    definition: "Signal for aircraft to land aboard the ship. A number suffix indicates time delay before landing."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "11-3" }]
  - term: Charlie
    definition: "The time the first aircraft is expected at the ramp. A “Charlie” or “Charlie on arrival” call is a directive to enter the pattern now; “Charlie five” means be at the ramp in five minutes."
    usage: Navy carrier training
    cite: [{ n: 3, page: "A-2" }]
  - term: Cherubs
    definition: Altitude in hundreds of feet (applies only to helicopters).
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "11-3" }]
  - term: Chicks
    definition: Wingmen in a flight.
    usage: Navy carrier training
    note: "The multi-service brevity word CHICK(S) means friendly aircraft in general. [4]"
    cite: [{ n: 3, page: "A-2" }]
  - term: Clara
    definition: A pilot's transmission meaning they do not have the visual landing aid (ball) in sight.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "11-3" }]
  - term: Cross-Deck Pendant
    definition: "Arresting gear wire. Three or four span the landing area, numbered from aft to forward; the aftmost is the one wire. A touchdown in which the hook engages none of them is a bolter."
    usage: Navy carrier training
    cite: [{ n: 3, page: "A-2, 1-7, A-1" }]
  - term: Delta
    definition: A signal given to hold and conserve fuel at an altitude and position appropriate to the type of aircraft and case recovery in effect.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "11-4" }]
  - term: Divert
    definition: "An order for an aircraft to proceed to and land at the field specified. This is a nonemergency situation."
    note: "Compare Bingo, which the same glossary defines as an emergency or fuel-critical order. [1]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "11-4" }]
  - term: Feet Wet/Dry
    definition: "Feet dry: a pilot report that the aircraft is passing over the shoreline and proceeding over land. Feet wet: passing over the shoreline and proceeding over water."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "11-4" }]
  - term: Final Bearing
    definition: The magnetic bearing assigned by CATCC or AATCC for final approach, an extension of the landing area centerline.
    note: "Navy carrier training adds that it is usually the BRC minus the landing area angle of 10°. [3]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "11-4" }]
  - term: Groove
    definition: "The final, straight-in part of the carrier approach, flown on the ball. The glossary divides it into thirds: the start, the middle and in close. The pilot makes the ball call when rolling into the groove."
    usage: Navy carrier training
    cite: [{ n: 3, page: "A-4, A-6, 2-18" }]
  - term: Landing Signal Officer (LSO)
    definition: Controls all fixed-wing aircraft off the 180 to touchdown during carrier and field carrier landing practice (FCLP) landings. Paddles is the LSO's call sign.
    usage: Navy carrier training
    cite: [{ n: 3, page: "A-4, A-5" }]
  - term: Marshal
    definition: A bearing, distance, and altitude fix designated by CATCC or AATCC that the pilots will orient holding and that the initial approach will commence.
    note: "Navy carrier training also uses Marshal for the ship's radar controller. [3]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "11-5" }, { n: 3, page: "A-4" }]
  - term: Mother
    definition: Code name used to signify the carrier.
    usage: Navy carrier training
    cite: [{ n: 3, page: "A-5" }]
  - term: Parrot
    definition: Military IFF/transponder.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "11-6" }]
  - term: Pigeons
    definition: Magnetic bearing and distance from an aircraft to a specific location.
    note: "The multi-service brevity word PIGEONS is the bearing and range to home plate. [4]"
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "11-6" }]
  - term: Platform
    definition: "A point of 5,000 feet altitude in the approach pattern at which all jet and turboprop aircraft decrease their rate of descent to not more than 2,000 feet per minute and continue let-down to the 10-nm DME fix for carriers and 12-nm for amphibious assault ships."
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "11-6" }]
  - term: Popeye
    definition: A pilot's term used to indicate that their aircraft has entered IMC.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "11-6" }]
  - term: Primary Flight Control (PriFly)
    definition: The position on the ship where the air officer or designated representative observes flight deck operations and provides visual control to aircraft that operate in the carrier control zone.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "11-7" }]
  - term: Roger Ball
    definition: The call made by the LSO that indicates you are cleared to land and the LSO has positive control, made less than a mile before landing.
    usage: Navy carrier training
    note: "Under Zip Lip the LSO flashes the green cut lights instead. [3]"
    cite: [{ n: 3, page: "A-6, 1-12" }]
  - term: Spin
    definition: A signal given to one or more aircraft that indicates a departure from and reentry into the break. The command may be issued by either the air officer or the flight leader.
    cite: [{ n: 1, page: "11-7" }]
  - term: Trick or Treat
    definition: "An aircraft in the pattern that has enough fuel for one more approach. If it does not trap, it will have to bingo or refuel in flight, if able."

  - term: Waveoff
    definition: "A discontinued landing attempt, which the pilot must fly immediately when the LSO illuminates the red waveoff lights on the lens. The LSO waves off any aircraft when the deck is foul or the aircraft is not within safe approach parameters."

## The carrier vocabulary

Carrier aviation has its own vocabulary. These are the words a pilot hears in the marshal stack, on the approach and on the ball, as the Navy's air traffic controller training manual and its carrier training glossary define them. [[1]](#source-1) [[3]](#source-3)

Several of these words are also multi-service brevity words with a different meaning. Bingo is the clearest case: aboard ship it is an order to divert to a shore field, while the brevity word is only a pre-briefed fuel state. Buster, Bullseye, Chicks and Pigeons differ too. Each note says how. [[1]](#source-1) [[3]](#source-3) [[4]](#source-4)

The Navy glossary covers amphibious assault ships (LHA and LHD) as well as carriers; where the two differ, as for the 10-nm and 12-nm fixes after platform, both are given. [[1]](#source-1)
