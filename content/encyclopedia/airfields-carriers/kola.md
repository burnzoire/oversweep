## Historical significance

The Kola Peninsula is the home of Russia's Northern Fleet, which in the Soviet era operated more than 200 submarines, from diesel-electric boats to nuclear ballistic-missile submarines; since 2014 the fleet has also commanded all Russian forces in the Murmansk and Arkhangelsk regions and on the Arctic islands. [[1]](#source-1)

The Nordic neighbours prepared for war in their own ways. Sweden's air force planned to disperse its squadrons across many wartime bases, including stretches of road, so that an attacker could not destroy it on the ground; the Bas 90 system grew out of that idea in the 1970s and 1980s, and Israel's destruction of the Egyptian Air Force on its airfields in 1967 was taken as proof of it. [[6]](#source-6) Finland, neutral throughout the Cold War, joined NATO on 4 April 2023, and Sweden followed on 7 March 2024. [[7]](#source-7) [[8]](#source-8)

## Forces deployed

- **Severomorsk**, on the Kola Bay north-east of Murmansk: the main base and administrative centre of the Northern Fleet. [[2]](#source-2)
- **Olenya**, 92 km south of Murmansk: long the headquarters of a naval reconnaissance air division, now a Long-Range Aviation base whose 3,500 m runway is the longest on the peninsula. [[3]](#source-3)
- **Bodø**, Norway: the main air station of northern Norway, home of NATO's quick reaction alert until 2022, when the mission passed to F-35s at Evenes. Its [F-16](record:f-16) squadrons moved to Ørland in 2023; a search-and-rescue detachment remains. [[4]](#source-4)
- **Andøya**, Norway: base of the Norwegian P-3C Orions until 30 June 2023, when 333 Squadron moved to Evenes; Norway's defence ministry has named it a permanent reception base for allied forces. [[5]](#source-5)
