## Historical significance

After Iraq invaded Kuwait on 2 August 1990, a 42-nation coalition built up its forces in Operation Desert Shield and, from 17 January 1991, fought Operation Desert Storm, which ended with the liberation of Kuwait on 28 February. [[1]](#source-1) The US Army's VII Corps, with 1,487 tanks and 146,321 troops, was the coalition's main combat formation, and the 1st and 2nd Marine Divisions drove on Kuwait City. [[1]](#source-1)

For the next twelve years coalition aircraft patrolled no-fly zones: Operation Southern Watch south of the 32nd parallel, later the 33rd, from August 1992 to March 2003, and Operation Northern Watch above the 36th parallel from 1997. [[3]](#source-3) [[2]](#source-2) The US-led invasion of March 2003 overthrew Saddam Hussein's government and was followed by an insurgency; US forces left in 2011 but returned in 2014 to lead the coalition against the Islamic State in Operation Inherent Resolve. [[4]](#source-4) [[5]](#source-5)

## Forces deployed

- **Balad**, north of Baghdad: taken by the 4th Infantry Division in 2003, it became the second-largest US base in Iraq, known as LSA Anaconda and from 2008 Joint Base Balad, with some 27,500 take-offs and landings a month. It was handed back to the Iraqi Air Force on 8 November 2011 and now houses its F-16s. [[6]](#source-6)
- **Al Asad**, Anbar: the second-largest US air base of the Iraq War and until January 2010 the home of II Marine Expeditionary Force. It was hit by Iranian ballistic missiles on 8 January 2020. [[7]](#source-7)
- **Tallil**, near Nasiriyah: known to the US Air Force as Ali Air Base and to the Army as Camp Adder; the ancient city of Ur lies inside its perimeter. [[8]](#source-8)
- **Al Taqaddum**, west of Baghdad: built by the RAF in 1952 as a satellite of RAF Habbaniya, and known as Camp Taqaddum since 2004. [[9]](#source-9)
- **Carriers**: the US Navy sent battle groups built around USS Dwight D. Eisenhower and USS Independence to the Gulf, where they were ready by 8 August 1990. [[1]](#source-1)
