## Historical significance

On 7 June 1981 Israeli Air Force [F-16](record:f-16)As escorted by F-15As destroyed Iraq's unfinished Osirak nuclear reactor near Baghdad in Operation Opera. They took off from Etzion Airbase in the Sinai and flew unchallenged through Jordanian and Saudi airspace. [[1]](#source-1) A year later, on 6 June 1982, Israel invaded southern Lebanon to drive out the Palestine Liberation Organization, and its forces briefly clashed with the Syrian Army in the north-east of the country. [[2]](#source-2)

On 13 April 2024 Iran launched about 170 drones, more than 30 cruise missiles and more than 120 ballistic missiles at Israel. The United States coordinated the multinational defence from the Combined Air Operations Center at [Al Udeid](record:al-udeid) in Qatar, and US aircraft reportedly destroyed more than 80 of the Iranian weapons. [[3]](#source-3) A senior US official said five ballistic missiles hit Nevatim, damaging a C-130, an unused runway and empty storage buildings, and four hit Ramon; the overall damage was minimal. [[3]](#source-3)

## Forces deployed

- **Nevatim**, Negev: built between 1979 and 1982, with Ramon and Ovda, mainly by US companies, to replace the four Israeli bases in the Sinai given up after the Camp David Accords. [[5]](#source-5) Its first eight F-35I Adir stealth fighters were declared operational in December 2017, and in May 2018 Israel said it had become the first country to use the F-35 in combat. No. 116 Squadron reopened there with the F-35I in November 2019. [[4]](#source-4)
- **Ramon**, near Mitzpe Ramon in the Negev: built at the same time as Nevatim and Ovda. [[5]](#source-5)
- **Ramat David**, Jezreel Valley: the northernmost Israeli Air Force base with fighter jets and drones, with three runways of about 2.5 km each. [[6]](#source-6)
- **Hatzerim**, near Beersheba: besides operational fighter squadrons, it houses the IAF Flight Academy and the IAF Aerobatic Team. [[7]](#source-7)
