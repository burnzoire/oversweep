## Role today

Kadena is the largest and busiest US Air Force base in East Asia, and is often called the "Keystone of the Pacific" for where it sits: about 650 km from the coast of China. [[1]](#source-1) More than 20,000 Americans and Japanese employees live or work on it. [[1]](#source-1) Its host, the 18th Wing, is the Air Force's largest combat wing, and flies tankers, airborne warning aircraft and rescue helicopters. [[1]](#source-1) The 48 F-15C Eagles once stationed there permanently have gone, and Nikkei Asia reported in late 2023 that the Air Force would base F-15EX Eagle IIs there instead. [[1]](#source-1)

## History

The field was a small strip, Yara Hikojo, finished for the Imperial Japanese Army Air Force just before the Battle of Okinawa, and was one of the first objectives of the US 7th Infantry Division. [[1]](#source-1) What the Americans captured was 4,600 ft of badly damaged coral runway; by nightfall on 4 April it could take emergency landings, and by August engineers had built a 6,500 ft and a 7,500 ft runway for fighters and bombers. [[1]](#source-1) Eighth Air Force moved its headquarters nearby in July 1945 to prepare B-29 groups for the invasion of Japan, which the surrender made unnecessary. [[1]](#source-1)

From Kadena, B-29s flew over Korea from 1950, and B-52s flew Arc Light missions over Southeast Asia from 1965 to 1970. [[1]](#source-1) The SR-71s of Detachment OL-8 flew from Kadena from 1968 until 1990, earning the nickname "Habu" after an Okinawan pit viper. [[1]](#source-1) The F-15 Eagle arrived in 1979, and in 1991 the 18th Wing took on tankers, E-3 Sentries and, from 1993, rescue for the Western Pacific and Indian Ocean. [[1]](#source-1)
