## Role today

Tbilisi is Georgia's busiest airport, with 4.75 million passengers in 2024, and the hub of Georgian Airways. [[1]](#source-1) It has been run since October 2005 by TAV Urban Georgia, majority-owned by TAV Airports Holding. [[1]](#source-1) The sources consulted describe no based military flying unit.

## History

The airport opened in 1952; a second terminal followed in 1968, and in 1981 it was the Soviet Union's twelfth-busiest airport, with 1,478,000 passengers on domestic Soviet routes. [[1]](#source-1) After independence, civil war and economic crisis cut that to 230,000 by 1998. [[1]](#source-1)

On 20 July 1992 an overloaded Tu-154 freighter carrying about 20 tonnes of tea overran the runway on take-off, killing all 24 aboard and 4 people on the ground. [[1]](#source-1) A $90.5 million rebuilding finished in February 2007 with a new international terminal and work on the apron, taxiways and runway; a rail link to the city was built but never brought into service. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2) The main runway was renovated in 2016, with new lighting and an upgraded instrument landing system. [[1]](#source-1)

## The airfield

Two parallel runways: 13R/31L, 3,000 m of concrete, in use, and 13L/31R, 2,500 m of asphalt, closed; TAV agreed with United Airports of Georgia to rebuild the closed runway to take the largest aircraft. [[1]](#source-1) [[3]](#source-3)
