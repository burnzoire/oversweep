Incirlik is a Turkish Air Force base on the edge of Adana, in southern Turkey, about 32 km inland from the Mediterranean, used jointly by the Turkish and US air forces. [[1]](#source-1) The Turkish Air Force owns the base and all its facilities and keeps its 10th Main Jet Base there; the US Air Force's 39th Air Base Wing is the main American unit, and the Royal Air Force and Royal Saudi Air Force have also flown from it. [[1]](#source-1) A Spanish Army Patriot regiment is based there too. [[1]](#source-1)

The decision to build the base dates from the Second Cairo Conference of December 1943, but work began only in 1951, as an emergency staging and recovery field for US medium and heavy bombers. [[1]](#source-1) In the early Cold War it was a reconnaissance base: GENETRIX balloons were launched from it from 1956, and it was the main U-2 base in the region until Francis Gary Powers was shot down over the Soviet Union by SA-2 missiles on 1 May 1960. [[1]](#source-1) In the 1958 Lebanon crisis, a Tactical Air Command composite strike force of [F-100s](record:f-100), B-57s, RF-101s and B-66s flew straight to Incirlik from the United States. [[1]](#source-1)

US fighter squadrons rotated through Incirlik for training through the 1970s and 1980s, using a Turkish air-to-ground range near Konya and an air-to-air range over the Mediterranean; [F-4s](record:f-4), F-15s, [F-16s](record:f-16), F-111s, [A-10s](record:a-10) and C-130s all served temporary tours there. [[1]](#source-1) In the 1991 Gulf War, Incirlik was the base for Joint Task Force Proven Force, about 140 aircraft that opened a northern front against Iraq, and afterwards it supported the northern no-fly zone, Operation Northern Watch. [[1]](#source-1)

The base has a single 10,000 ft runway, among about 57 hardened aircraft shelters. [[1]](#source-1) Figure 1 draws it from its threshold coordinates. [[2]](#source-2)
