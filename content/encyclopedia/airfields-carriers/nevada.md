## Era

The DCS map shows the range as it is today: the largest contiguous air and ground space for peacetime military operations in the free world, about the size of Germany, and home to Red Flag. [[1]](#source-1) It suits training and present-day scenarios rather than a historical war.

## Historical significance

The land was withdrawn for a bombing range in October 1940, and from 1942 Army Air Forces units trained on the Tonopah and Las Vegas gunnery and bombing ranges. [[2]](#source-2) In December 1950 part of the range became the Nevada Proving Grounds, and the first nuclear test there, for Operation Ranger, was on 27 January 1951. [[2]](#source-2) In 1955 the U-2 programme laid out a runway at Groom Lake, the site now known as Area 51. [[2]](#source-2)

The range is where much of modern US air combat training took shape. Red Flag was first flown there in 1975. [[2]](#source-2) At Tonopah, pilots flew captured and acquired Soviet-built MiGs against US aircraft in secret from the late 1970s, and from 1982 the F-117 Nighthawk stealth fighter worked up to service there, flying only at night. [[3]](#source-3) The range took its current name in 2001. [[2]](#source-2)

## Forces deployed

- **[Nellis Air Force Base](record:nellis)**: home of the Air Force Warfare Center, which runs the range. [[2]](#source-2)
- **Creech Air Force Base**, formerly Indian Springs: flies remotely piloted aircraft on missions around the world, and is the Thunderbirds' training site and one of the range's two emergency divert fields. [[5]](#source-5)
- **Tonopah Test Range Airport**: a 12,000 ft runway with more than fifty hangars, run by Air Combat Command. [[3]](#source-3)
- **Groom Lake**, Area 51, on the range's north-east edge. [[2]](#source-2)
- **Army air defence**: sites east of the range host Patriot communications exercises. [[2]](#source-2)

The range is landlocked, so no carriers operate in this theatre.
