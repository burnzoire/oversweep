## Historical significance

The Fulda Gap, two corridors of lowland between the Hesse–Thuringia stretch of the inner German border and Frankfurt, was seen throughout the Cold War as a route by which Soviet and Warsaw Pact tanks might drive in a surprise attack to seize crossings of the Rhine. [[1]](#source-1) Its defence was the mission of the US V Corps, with armored cavalry watching the border: the 14th Armored Cavalry Regiment from 1951 to 1972 and then the 11th, whose task in war was to delay an attack until the corps' divisions could deploy. [[1]](#source-1) Observation Post Alpha, near Rasdorf, overlooked the gap until the army left it in 1991. [[2]](#source-2)

In 1948–1949 the Soviet blockade of West Berlin was answered by an airlift flown from bases such as Wiesbaden and Rhein-Main. [[10]](#source-10) From 1969 to 1993 NATO's annual Reforger exercises practised flying and shipping reinforcements from North America to West Germany in a crisis. [[3]](#source-3)

## Forces deployed

- **US Army**: V Corps defended the Fulda Gap from Frankfurt [[1]](#source-1), and VII Corps was headquartered at Kelley Barracks in Stuttgart from 1951 until it went to the Gulf War in 1990–1991 and was inactivated in 1992. [[4]](#source-4)
- **Ramstein Air Base**: built between 1951 and 1953 and today the headquarters of US Air Forces in Europe – Air Forces Africa and NATO's Allied Air Command. [[5]](#source-5)
- **Spangdahlem Air Base**: in November 1983 its 52nd Tactical Fighter Wing became the only all-defence-suppression wing outside the United States, pairing [F-4E](record:f-4) and F-4G Phantoms as Wild Weasel hunter-killer teams; [F-16C](record:f-16)s replaced the F-4Es from 1987. [[6]](#source-6)
- **Bitburg Air Base**: home of the 36th Tactical Fighter Wing from 1952 until it was returned to Germany on 1 October 1994. [[7]](#source-7)
- **Hahn Air Base**: home of the 50th Tactical Fighter Wing, inactivated in 1991, before the base was handed over in September 1993. [[8]](#source-8)
- **Sembach**: a front-line NATO base for US reconnaissance, close-air-support and tactical air control units during the Cold War, and a US Army post since 2012. [[9]](#source-9)
