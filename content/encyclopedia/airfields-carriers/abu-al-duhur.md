## Role today

Abu al-Duhur was a major Syrian Air Force base, 5 km east of the town of the same name in Idlib Governorate. [[1]](#source-1) The civil war left it completely destroyed and useless. [[1]](#source-1) On 18 August 2026 Israeli aircraft struck its runways and storage in several raids; Israel said it was deterring Turkish use of the base, and experts quoted by *The New York Times* linked the strikes to fears that Turkish air defences there could close Syrian airspace to Israeli aircraft heading for Iran. [[1]](#source-1)

## History

Rebels besieged the base from 2012 until 9 September 2015, when the Army of Conquest coalition captured it, finding fighters that had been out of service for years pushed to the edges of the aprons. [[1]](#source-1) The Syrian army retook it on 10 January 2018. [[1]](#source-1) On 30 November 2024, in the offensive that brought down the Assad government, rebels led by Hayat Tahrir al-Sham captured it again. [[1]](#source-1)

## The airfield

Source 2 lists one paved runway, 09/27, of 9,810 ft. [[2]](#source-2) OpenStreetMap maps two parallel runways, each about 2,970 m long. [[4]](#source-4)
