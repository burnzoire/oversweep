## Role today

Sukhum is the main airport of Abkhazia, which Georgia and most of the world regard as part of Georgia. [[1]](#source-1) After more than thirty years without regular passenger flights it reopened on 1 May 2025 with flights to Moscow, following a Russian-funded rebuilding; ICAO does not recognise it as an international airport and withdrew its code in 2006. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2) Since 2019 it has been a joint civil and state-aviation airfield, and the Abkhaz air force, five L-39s and six helicopters, is based there. [[2]](#source-2)

## History

The airport and its terminal were built in the mid-1960s for holiday traffic to the Abkhaz coast; the concrete was thickened in the late 1970s for Il-76s, and the runway was lengthened in the mid-1980s for Il-86s. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2) On 14 August 1982 a Tu-134A and an L-410 collided on the runway, killing all 11 aboard the L-410. [[1]](#source-1)

The airport was heavily damaged in the war of the early 1990s and closed in 1993; mines left on it, even on the runway, were later cleared by the HALO Trust. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2) During the 2008 war, on 11 August, Russian Il-76 transports landed there with paratroopers and equipment, the first military transports in 15 years, according to airport workers' accounts. [[2]](#source-2)

## The airfield

A single concrete runway, 12/30, on the coastal lowland by Sukhumi Bay, which allows approaches from both directions. [[2]](#source-2) Published lengths differ: 3,145 m in one source and 12,012 ft in another. [[1]](#source-1) [[3]](#source-3)
