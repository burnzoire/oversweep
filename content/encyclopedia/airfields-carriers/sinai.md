## Historical significance

The Sinai has been fought over in three Arab–Israeli wars. In the Suez Crisis of 1956 Israel invaded on 29 October to reopen the Straits of Tiran, and Britain and France joined two days later to try to regain the canal, until pressure from the United States, the Soviet Union and the United Nations forced them out. [[1]](#source-1) At 07:45 on 5 June 1967 the Israeli Air Force opened the Six-Day War with Operation Focus, which destroyed most of the Egyptian Air Force on the ground and put 18 Egyptian airfields out of action; by noon about 450 Egyptian, Jordanian and Syrian aircraft had been destroyed. [[2]](#source-2) In October 1973 Egypt crossed the Suez Canal in Operation Badr, before Israel halted its advance and crossed the canal in turn. [[3]](#source-3)

The 1979 peace treaty, signed in Washington on 26 March, returned the Sinai to Egypt. [[4]](#source-4) Israel gave up its four air bases there, among them Etzion and Eitam, and US companies built Ramon, Nevatim and Ovda in the Negev to replace them between 1979 and 1982. [[5]](#source-5) Before it left, Israel launched the June 1981 raid on Iraq's Osirak reactor, Operation Opera, from Etzion. [[6]](#source-6)

## Forces deployed

- **Multinational Force and Observers**: set up outside the UN by a protocol of 3 August 1981 to oversee the treaty, with a US infantry battalion and the 1st US Support Battalion (Task Force Sinai) alongside battalions from Fiji and Colombia. [[7]](#source-7) Its North Camp at el Gorah was attacked by militants in September 2012, and by 2016 most of its duties had moved to the south of the peninsula. [[7]](#source-7)
- **Ramon Airbase**, Negev: home of three Israeli F-16I Sufa squadrons, whose jets took part in the 2007 strike on a Syrian reactor. [[5]](#source-5)
- **Ovda Airbase**, north of Eilat: hosted the international Blue Flag exercise every two years until 2021 and the Israeli aggressor squadron until 2025. [[8]](#source-8)
