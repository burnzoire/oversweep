## The Eagle's warning receiver

On the [F-15](record:f-15c) the ALR-56 detects radar signals, identifies them as a specific threat and alerts the crew on the TEWS display. [[1]](#source-1) It also manages the aircraft's ALE-45 countermeasures dispenser and ALQ-135 jammer. [[1]](#source-1) Its four main antennas sit on the fin tips and wingtips, with a fifth under the forward fuselage, and its software can be changed as threats change. [[1]](#source-1)

## Limits of the processor

A 1995 Air Force assessment of the ALR-56C found problems with memory, processing speed, sorting out ambiguous threats and false alarms. [[1]](#source-1) A common processor upgrade for the F-15 and F-16 receivers was started to fix them. [[1]](#source-1)

## The ALR-56M

The Air Force wanted a replacement for the F-16's [ALR-69](record:alr-69) from 1980 and chose the ALR-56M in 1988. [[1]](#source-1) It is a miniaturised ALR-56C that fits where the ALR-69 did, built to cope better with a dense signal environment and modern threat signals. [[1]](#source-1) It went mainly into F-16 Block 40 and later aircraft. [[1]](#source-1)
