## Chaff and flares

A dispenser protects an aircraft from radar-guided and infrared-homing missiles by ejecting decoys. [[1]](#source-1) Chaff is a cloud of thin aluminium strips, each cut to match the wavelengths of the radars it is meant to confuse; flares are hot-burning magnesium meant to defeat a missile's infrared tracking. [[2]](#source-2)

## The ALE-40

The ALE-40 is a simple system: the pilot releases chaff or flares, depending on the threat. [[2]](#source-2) Each dispenser holds 30 chaff cartridges or 15 flares. [[4]](#source-4) It flew on a long list of US aircraft, from the [A-10](record:a-10) and [F-16](record:f-16) to the F-4 and transports. [[4]](#source-4)

## The ALE-47

The ALE-47 is an improved ALE-40 with more automation and software. [[1]](#source-1) [[3]](#source-3) It connects to the [radar warning receiver](term:radar-warning-receiver) and missile warning sensors, and when they detect a threat it can launch countermeasures automatically. [[1]](#source-1) The aircrew decide how through the programs they load. [[3]](#source-3) The Navy also bought it to replace the ALE-39 in its F-14s, EA-6Bs and F/A-18s, because the older dispenser was less reliable and cost much more to run. [[3]](#source-3)
