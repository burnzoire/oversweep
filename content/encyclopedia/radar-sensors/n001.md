## The Flanker's radar

NIIP developed the N001 for the [Su-27](record:su-27). It was meant to draw on the experimental Soyuz radar and to share much with the MiG-29's [N019](record:n019); NATO gave both the name Slot Back. [[1]](#source-1) Its twist-cassegrain antenna is 1.075 m across, and it is a [pulse-Doppler radar](term:pulse-doppler-radar) working in the 3 cm band with medium and high PRFs for looking down. [[1]](#source-1)

## Range depends on aspect

The published figures show how much a target's aspect matters. Against a fighter-sized target head-on, the radar searches out to about 80 to 100 km; against the same target from behind, only about 40 km. [[1]](#source-1) Treat the numbers as a guide, not as a performance figure.

## Reliability and versions

The first sets averaged only 5 hours between failures; later ones reached 100 and eventually 200 hours. [[1]](#source-1) Later versions added more targets, the R-77 missile and ground mapping. [[1]](#source-1)
