## A receiver, not a designator

Pave Penny is a [laser spot tracker](term:lst): it searches for laser light reflected from a target that someone else is marking, and shows the pilot where the spot is on the head-up display. [[1]](#source-1) It makes no laser beam of its own and cannot designate, so it works with a designator on the ground or in another aircraft. [[1]](#source-1) The pilot selects the four-digit [laser code](term:laser-code) in the cockpit, and it can be changed in flight. [[2]](#source-2)

## What it is for

The FAS calls the seeker very sensitive: with good designation, a pilot can engage targets not yet in sight, because the spot is found before the target is. [[2]](#source-2) Its limit is that the spot must be inside the seeker's field of view. [[2]](#source-2) On the A-7 it could also give an automatic release. [[2]](#source-2)

## An old design

Pave Penny dates from the mid-1970s and grew out of the Pave Sword tracker used on F-4s over Vietnam. [[1]](#source-1) By 1998 the FAS described it as becoming unreliable and unsupportable, and noted that the A-10's gun and Maverick were by then used at ranges beyond what Pave Penny could cover. [[2]](#source-2) Modern targeting pods such as [LITENING](record:litening) and [Sniper](record:sniper) carry a spot tracker of their own as well as a laser.
