## Seeing over the trees

The US Army wanted a scout helicopter that could carry out reconnaissance, surveillance and target acquisition while staying hidden behind trees and terrain, and a mast-mounted sight was the main focus of that work. [[1]](#source-1) The result, on the [OH-58D](record:oh-58d), is a ball that sits above the rotor like a beach ball. [[1]](#source-1)

## What is in it

The sight has a gyro-stabilised platform with a television system, a thermal imaging system and a laser rangefinder/designator. [[1]](#source-1) It gave the OH-58D target acquisition and laser designation by day or night, in limited visibility and in bad weather. [[1]](#source-1) The OH-58D was the first US Army helicopter with a 1553 databus, so target data from the sight could go directly to precision-guided weapons. [[1]](#source-1)

## Beyond the Army

The FAS calls the sight one of the key elements of the Kiowa Warrior. [[2]](#source-2) The US Navy chose it for its ships and then developed the cheaper, lighter Mast Mounted Sight II. [[2]](#source-2)
