## What it does

The ALR-69 watches the radar environment and alerts the pilot to hostile or foreign activity. When it receives a radar signal it compares it with its threat database and shows a symbol for that radar, so the crew can see what is painting them, friend or foe. [[1]](#source-1) It detects and displays airborne interceptors, surface-to-air missiles and anti-aircraft artillery. [[1]](#source-1)

## Keeping an old receiver current

The receiver is 1970s technology, and by the early 1990s it was hard to support. [[1]](#source-1) A 1993 modification replaced its processor and controller, added memory, allowed reprogramming on the flight line and incorporated hundreds of software changes with the latest threat data. [[1]](#source-1) It is not fitted to Block 50 F-16s, [[1]](#source-1) which carry the [ALR-56M](record:alr-56) instead. [[3]](#source-3)
