## Radar above the rotor

The Longbow began in the 1980s as the Airborne Adverse Weather Weapon System, part of a programme to improve the AH-64A; by 1990 it had been renamed Longbow. [[1]](#source-1) Its millimetre-wave radar and radar frequency interferometer sit in a dome above the main rotor. [[1]](#source-1) That raised position lets the [Apache](record:ah-64d) detect targets while the helicopter itself stays behind terrain, trees or buildings. [[1]](#source-1)

## Many targets, shared

The radar can track up to 128 targets and engage up to 16 at once, and an attack can start within 30 seconds. [[1]](#source-1) A radio modem shares what it finds with ground units and other Apaches, so they can fire on targets that one helicopter detected. [[1]](#source-1) The AH-64E's version adds overwater capability, which could allow strikes against ships. [[1]](#source-1)
