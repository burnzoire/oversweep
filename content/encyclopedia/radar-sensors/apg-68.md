## The F-16C's radar

The APG-68 replaced the [APG-66](record:apg-66) when the F-16C Block 25 arrived, and with it came more range, better resolution and more modes. [[3]](#source-3) Block 25 also gave the F-16 the [AIM-120](record:aim-120). [[3]](#source-3) Its programmable signal processor runs the scan patterns for air-to-air missiles and air-to-ground weapons, and its software can be changed for new threats. [[1]](#source-1)

## Ground mapping

For a non-visual attack the radar, fire control computer and navigation system work together: the computer puts the cursors where the inertial system thinks the target is, and the pilot refines them. [[2]](#source-2) Expand gives four times the detail. In the 10, 20 and 40 NM scopes, DBS-1 sharpens returns more than 5 degrees off the nose and DBS-2, at 64 times the resolution, returns more than 15 degrees off. [[2]](#source-2) The handbook cautions that the cursors stay centred in these modes, which can disorient the pilot. [[2]](#source-2)

## Replacement

The last version, the APG-68(V)9, added synthetic aperture radar and went to many export F-16s. [[1]](#source-1) From 2022 the US Air Force began replacing APG-68s with the APG-83 AESA radar. [[1]](#source-1)
