## One radar for fighter and attack

Hughes designed the APG-65 for the [F/A-18 Hornet](record:fa-18c), which needed one radar for both air-to-air and air-to-surface missions. [[1]](#source-1) In the air it searches, tracks and runs [track-while-scan](term:track-while-scan), which with an active missile such as the [AIM-120](record:aim-120) gives the Hornet a fire-and-forget shot. [[1]](#source-1) Against the surface it maps by Doppler beam sharpening and synthetic aperture, tracks fixed and moving targets, and searches the sea. [[1]](#source-1)

## Search modes for different targets

Velocity search gives the longest detection range against targets coming head-on, and [range-while-search](term:range-while-search) detects targets from any aspect. [[1]](#source-1) Raid assessment helps when a single contact may be a formation: it expands the area around a tracked target until the radar can separate aircraft flying close together. [[1]](#source-1) In the Hornet the radar sits on a rack that slides out of the nose for maintenance. [[1]](#source-1)

## The APG-73

From 1992 the APG-73 replaced it in US Navy and Marine Corps F/A-18C/Ds. [[1]](#source-1) It processes faster, has more memory and is easier to maintain, and with extra equipment it can make high-resolution ground maps to improve weapon aiming. [[1]](#source-1)
