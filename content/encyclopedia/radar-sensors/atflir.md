## The Navy's pod

ATFLIR, the AN/ASQ-228 Advanced Targeting Forward-Looking Infrared, is Raytheon's targeting pod for Navy Hornets and Super Hornets. [[1]](#source-1) It combines a thermal camera, a low-light TV camera, a laser designator and rangefinder, and a [laser spot tracker](term:lst). [[1]](#source-1) It is normally carried on a fuselage station that would otherwise hold an AIM-120. [[1]](#source-1) It was intended to replace the AN/AAS-38 Nite Hawk, which the FAS describes as the night-attack F/A-18C/D's laser designation pod, carried on the left fuselage station. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2)

## Range and service

The pod is 72 in long, weighs 420 lb and has a slant range of 40 miles; it is said to be usable up to 50,000 ft. [[1]](#source-1) It was designed with fewer parts to make it easier to maintain, though early pods with VFA-115 in 2003 had problems. [[1]](#source-1) The Navy took 410 pods. [[1]](#source-1)

## What comes next

The Navy is weighing whether to improve ATFLIR or replace it because of its readiness and support costs. [[1]](#source-1) As of 2024, flight tests of [LITENING](record:litening) as a replacement had concluded successfully. [[1]](#source-1)
