## Everything in one pod

Rafael's Missiles Division developed LITENING for the Israeli Air Force, and Northrop Grumman joined it in 1995. [[1]](#source-1) The FAS describes it as putting in one pod what earlier systems needed several pods for. [[2]](#source-2) It has an infrared sensor and a TV camera, a laser designator and rangefinder, and a laser spot detector, all on one optical bench. [[2]](#source-2)

## Finding someone else's spot

The spot detector finds laser energy from another source, so a ground or airborne controller can mark a target and the pilot can find it in the pod. [[2]](#source-2) That is the [laser spot tracker](term:lst) job that [Pave Penny](record:pave-penny) did alone, now in the same pod as a designator.

## Many versions

LITENING has been upgraded many times, from Litening II in 1999 through AT, G4 and SE, and it flies on a long list of aircraft. [[1]](#source-1) As of 2024, flight tests of LITENING as a replacement for the Navy's [ATFLIR](record:atflir) had concluded successfully. [[1]](#source-1)
