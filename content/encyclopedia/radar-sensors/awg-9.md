## A radar for fleet defence

The AWG-9 came out of the Navy's search for a fleet-defence fighter: an aircraft with very long-range radar and missiles that could engage enemy formations far from the carriers. [[1]](#source-1) Hughes adapted its radar and missile work for the Air Force's cancelled XF-108 into the AWG-9 and the [AIM-54 Phoenix](record:aim-54), first for the F-111B and then, when that failed, for the [F-14](record:f-14). [[1]](#source-1)

## Tracking many targets at once

Its [track-while-scan](term:track-while-scan) mode was designed to let the Tomcat shoot down bomber formations at long range. [[1]](#source-1) It can track 24 targets, show 18 and launch against 6 at the same time. [[1]](#source-1) [[2]](#source-2) The original system used an analog computer. [[1]](#source-1)

## The APG-71

The F-14D's APG-71 replaced the analog processing with digital hardware, which made the radar more flexible and gave it fewer, lighter units. [[2]](#source-2) It also added features meant to make it harder to jam. [[1]](#source-1) Only 55 F-14Ds received it before the programme was cut. [[1]](#source-1)
