## The Eagle's radar

Hughes designed the APG-63 for the [F-15](record:f-15c) air superiority fighter. It is an X-band [pulse-Doppler radar](term:pulse-doppler-radar) for both air-to-air and air-to-ground work, built to see low-flying targets against the ground as well as high ones against the sky. [[1]](#source-1) It feeds target data to the aircraft's central computer for weapon delivery. [[1]](#source-1)

## Changed by software

In 1979 the APG-63 became the first airborne radar with a programmable signal processor, so new modes and weapons could be added by reprogramming instead of new hardware. [[1]](#source-1) That processor is one of the main differences between the F-15A/B and the F-15C/D. [[1]](#source-1) By the 1990s the original radar was hard to support: the FAS describes a mean time between failures of under 15 hours and almost no spare processing or memory for new software. [[2]](#source-2) The APG-63(V)1 redesign was meant to raise that to about 120 hours. [[2]](#source-2)

## The APG-70 and the Strike Eagle

The APG-70 was a 1980s redesign of the APG-63 for reliability, easier maintenance and new modes. [[1]](#source-1) It went into the last F-15C/Ds and the [F-15E Strike Eagle](record:f-15e), whose ground-mapping modes it sharpened; the US Air Force's APG-70 maps at least three times finer than the export version. [[1]](#source-1) The F-15E is now getting the AESA APG-82(V)1, which also lets the radar and the jammer work at the same time without degrading each other. [[1]](#source-1)
