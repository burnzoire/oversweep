## A compromise design

Phazotron was asked in the mid-1970s for a modern radar for the [MiG-29](record:mig-29). The plan was a flat planar antenna and fully digital signal processing, to detect and track a fighter-sized target at 100 km or more. [[1]](#source-1) Prototypes could not reach that in time and still fit in the nose, so Phazotron went back to the Sapfir-23ML's twisted cassegrain antenna and analog processors, with a new digital computer. [[1]](#source-1) The result worked, but kept the older design's weaknesses: it could not detect and track targets as far out as the R-27 and R-77 missiles could reach. [[1]](#source-1)

## Compromised

The radar was also betrayed to the CIA by Phazotron designer Adolf Tolkachev, who was executed in 1986. [[1]](#source-1) The Soviets answered with the N019M Topaz for the MiG-29S. [[1]](#source-1) NATO calls it Slot Back I and the [Su-27](record:su-27)'s [N001](record:n001) Slot Back II, because their electronics are similar. [[2]](#source-2)
